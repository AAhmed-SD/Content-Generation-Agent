## How the library works

The public page is a grid. Each card has a title, a short summary, and a cover image, or a document mark when the source is a PDF. Opening a card shows the summary, key points, and numbered steps under “How to apply it.” A published card ends with “Apply with your strategist.” A draft is visible only to the owner and ends with “Edit blueprint.”

The owner’s studio can add a blueprint from three sources, alone or together:

- A PDF, PNG, JPEG, or WebP up to 6 MB. A PDF’s first page can become the cover.
- An optional display image.
- A teaching transcript, pasted or uploaded as TXT, Markdown, SRT, or VTT, up to 120,000 characters.

“Generate summary” reads those sources and fills five fields: title, short summary, key points, how to apply it, and a long extracted framework. The studio asks for at most eight points and eight steps. Review notes appear before publish. The original transcript is kept for editing and is not part of the public card. The long extracted text is what the strategist reads. Visibility is draft or published.

The strategist is a signed-in chat. It can be opened on its own, or from one blueprint. Replies can cite the blueprint they used, and that citation opens the card. Two starter prompts are offered: help choosing a blueprint, and help applying the open one. Each account has 100 messages, with no expiration stated in the interface. Voice input can fill the message box. The owner connects an OpenAI key in admin. The key stays on the server. The library can be exported from the admin screen.

Signing in uses Clerk. The page also links to Content OS.

## The 23 published blueprints

Each entry is the subject of the public card. Source type is what the library stored.

### Money, offers, and scaling

**$10k /mo as a Multi-Passionate Creator.** PDF. For a creator with many interests. Make one offer profitable and automated before chasing the next passion. Names three income paths: lower-ticket automation, high-ticket work handed to other people, and subscriptions. Names the usual stalls as too many starts, unfinished work, and spending that undoes the profit.

**7 Universal Money Laws.** PDF. Seven named ideas about where money goes: Leverage, Communication, Magnetism, Overflow, Assumption, Relativity, and Alignment. Each law is split into three smaller ideas.

**How to Monetize Your Zone of Genius.** PDF. Cross what you are good at with what you care about, name the change a customer wants, and list their wants, frustrations, and beliefs. Turn that into steps and marketing, then into a product that does not need you in every delivery.

**Monetization Phases On the Way to $20K Months.** JPEG. Three stages: prove the offer and get cash in, make delivery repeatable, then widen it. The page treats a quiet $20k month from products that run without you as the aim, and says the stage depends on demand and audience.

**Offer Engineering Framework.** PNG. Make the yes easier by pairing a specific result with less work, less confusion, and less fear. The offer mixes done-for-you pieces, a clear process, and support.

**The Splinter Scaling Method.** PDF. Write one signature method and build the offer and the marketing from it. Each piece of content breaks off one part of that method and points back to the whole. Cash from the offer pays for the next refinement.

**The Profit Without Pressure Gameplan.** PNG. Treat brand, content, offer, and sales as one system. Decide whether the business is still proving demand, making the work repeatable, or ready to widen. Do the earliest missing step by hand before automating it.

**The Personal Brand Growth Engine.** PDF. Five parts: the brand people know you for, an automated sales path called the Infinity Funnel, weekly content as fuel, a core offer, and monthly recurring revenue to keep the business steady. Products are narrower than the brand. The funnel starts with an email opt-in, sends useful mail, and uses real deadlines. The page suggests monthly short promotions and a larger launch about once a quarter.

**How to Successfully Launch a Course or Group Coaching Program.** PDF. Three phases: find leads with a clear offer and short posts, earn trust over time, then deal with beliefs before the sale.

**How to Design a Program.** PNG. A course or group program built from five pieces: a result and a time span, phases, milestones you can measure, systems that repeat, and accelerators that make the work easier.

### Content and copy

**10 Hook Points.** PDF. Ten ways to hold attention, used as a menu rather than a fixed order. The public card groups them as outcome and frame, an open problem or a story, a fresh or disputed idea, and sensory detail or a shared opponent.

**77 Content Creation Prompts.** PDF. Prompts for posts, mail, video, and podcasts. The instruction on the card is to answer them inside your own subject, and to use them for ideas, openings, and a clearer account of the value you offer.

**7 Method-Centric Headline Formulas.** PDF. Name a method that is yours, even if it adapts an older one, then fill one of seven headline shapes. The shapes stress how-to, a change, a benefit, the system itself, its force, who it is for, or a story with a time span. The card says any result or time claim has to be true.

**33 Conversion Codes For Creators.** PDF. Prompts for persuasive posts and sales copy. They start from the reader’s symptom, then use contrast, story, and a named method. One of the codes is a 14-step sales letter. The card says deadlines and proof have to be real.

**21 Persuasion Principles.** PDF. A map for a message that asks someone to act: relevance, plain language, trust, less friction, and room for what the reader already believes, objects to, or could buy instead. The card says the diagram is not a promise of results.

**Short Form Content and Copy Scripting Framework.** PDF. Six stages for one short piece or a run of them: the opportunity, the problem, the solution, the benefits, the system, then a call to action.

**The Pillar Content Flywheel.** PNG. Five passes on one idea: an unedited draft, a structure with named parts, the promise and the hook, a rest then an edit, then publish and split the piece into follow-ups.

**Turn One Idea Into 6 Pieces of Content.** PNG. Six angles on one topic: a system, a story, a metaphor, a mistake, an insight, and a question. Use one angle or several.

**The Writing-Centric Business Model.** PDF. The newsletter is the source. The same piece becomes the weekly email, a blog post, daily emails from its subtopics, at least seven short pieces, a lightly edited video script, and later a book or a product. Old letters stay in a bank.

**Streamlined Business Growth Strategy.** PDF. A weekly pillar and a monthly project. The week is topics, channels, and a schedule. The month is product, marketing, or operations.

**The Hero's Journey 12-Phase Storytelling Framework.** PDF. The familiar twelve phases, from the ordinary world to the return. The card says a short piece can drop phases, and the hero can be you, a client, or an invented person.

### Self-mastery

**Cycles of Awareness: The Reasons Behind Success and Failure.** PDF. Awareness opens a choice. A chosen response can compound. Distraction or no choice is described as the path that stalls.

**3 Types of Systems.** PDF. After a brain dump, sort the parts into a line (steps toward an end), a cluster (parts that stand alone and still belong), or a cycle (work that repeats).

## Content OS, as the public page describes it

Content OS is sold as a system installed around one business, its voice, and the beliefs that lead someone to pay. The page argues that more posts do not fix a buying-belief problem, and it names eight beliefs the system is meant to shift.

The workspace features shown on the page:

- Monday research that returns seven content ideas.
- A way to turn an old piece into a new one.
- Patterns taken from strong content in the same subject, applied to your own idea.
- A place for a half-formed thought.
- A “Business Brain” chat and a set of strategy assistants.
- A store of drafts so the next piece starts from the last one.

The page said enrollment was closed on this date. The library’s banner points here with the line that these strategies can be built into your own content system.
