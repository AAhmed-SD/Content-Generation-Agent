# For the Offload subscription

Taken from Beau Norton’s public library on 2 November 2026, then cut to the offer Offload sells and to the work of keeping an audience. The writer does not read this file. A line in here is not a product fact. Price, trial length, and features stay in `brand/product-facts.md` and `brand/platforms.md`.

## Subscription

Offload is one subscription. Stay with that offer until people want it, delivery is the same each time, and only then widen it. Do the earliest missing step by hand before automating it.

The recurring fee is the steady part. The weekly cards are what bring people back. Each account is narrower than the brand: one subject, one object, the same subscription.

The yes is a specific result, with less of the pile left to face alone. The caption may end on the trial line. That sentence is still unset.

Every post points back at the same method. The words already allowed are FSRS, the 4 phases, rescheduling, the remediation deck, and calendar sync. A card takes one of those and stops.

## Audience

Name the situation they already have, then the product. Clinical and GCSE carry revision that does not finish. Analytical carries the unfinished problem set. Mental health carries time that will not hold still. The post stays on that situation. It stays clear of advice, a diagnosis, and a worked answer.

Put the outcome in the first line. Leave the line worth saving for the payoff the same day. A save, a finish, and a return the next week are the signs the audience is holding.

The useful card comes before the ask. The trial line is the last line of the caption.

Someone continues when the work looks worth paying for. The card can say only what `product-facts.md` allows. A number, a deadline, or a result has to already be in the week’s brief.

## Content

One idea becomes the four pieces. Angles that fit that idea: a process someone can follow, an example, a mistake, a change in understanding, a question. The hook stops. The payoff is the line worth saving. The still is the screenshot. The carousel is three beats, then stop.

Make the piece in passes. Capture it. Name the parts. Write the hook with the same care as the rest. Let it sit. Publish. Cut the next piece from what is already published.

The week’s brief is the source. The same sentence is cut for TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, and Facebook. A line the loop has shown to work goes in the bank. A line that went quiet is dropped. Monday’s note says what to repeat.

A headline names a method Offload already has. Patterns worth copying are the way students in that niche already talk, applied to this week’s sentence. The card does not quote a person.
