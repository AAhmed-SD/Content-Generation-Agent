# Scripting

How a film script sounds. Read with `product-facts.md` (what may be claimed), `safety.md` (what may never be said), and the niche file (who it is for). This file decides the voice; those files decide the content.

## Who is speaking

| Account | Narrator | Says | Never says |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| offload.clinical | An older student in the same degree, speaking to someone a year or two behind | "you", "we", "most of us" | "I passed", "on my placement I", any first-person exam or placement claim. No patient, no case. |
| offload.analytical | The same older-student voice, a little drier and more deadpan | "you", "we" | "I got a first", a worked answer, an invented number |
| offload.gcsealevel | An older sibling who did their GCSEs a few years ago and remembers it | "you", "be honest", "everyone does this" | Teacher voice, parent voice, fake slang, a grade promise, "DM me" |
| offload.mentalhealth (time) | Hadiqa, the founder, in her own words | "I", "my brother", "I built this because" | That the viewer has a condition, treatment language, exam or placement claims about herself |

The founder story is told as written in `product-facts.md` section 7, and only on the time account and LinkedIn.

## The shape

1. **The first line is the whole hook.** Under two seconds. Specific, a little uncomfortable, true. "When did you last open anatomy?" beats "Revision can be hard."
2. **The on-screen words add to the voice.** They do not repeat it. Voice: "Be honest." Screen: "When did you last open anatomy?"
3. **One idea per film.** If it needs "and also", it is two films.
4. **The useful part comes before the product.** The tip, the reveal, the joke. A viewer who leaves before the product still got something.
5. **The product is one short line, said like a person.** "The app brings each card back right before it fades." Not: "Offload builds flashcards from your own lecture notes, and brings each one back right before you'd forget it."
6. **Something changes every two to three seconds:** a new line on screen, a cut, a reveal.
7. **Leave a reason to stay:** a question answered at the end, "the third one is the one nobody does", a loop back to the first line.

## Words

- Be specific: "pharmacology lecture six, still unopened, Thursday, 11pm" beats "your lectures".
- Use contractions. Short sentences. One clause where you can.
- A little humour, never at the viewer's expense.
- British English and UK terms: revise, placement, finals, mocks, sixth form.
- Write for the ear. Read it aloud. If you run out of breath, cut it.

Never use: "game-changer", "unlock", "level up", "hack", "journey", "seamless", "AI-powered", "study smarter not harder", "in today's video", "let's dive in", "it's that simple".

## The product line

- One LIVE feature per film, from `product-facts.md`, using its "Say it like this" words loosely. Rotate through all of them. Do not use the same feature twice on one account in a week.
- Name the product at most once. "The app" is fine after that, or nothing at all.
- The tagline is not spoken in most films. It may sit on the end card as text.

## Endings

Rotate. Do not use the same ending twice in a row on one account.

- A loop back to the first line, as a question.
- A punchline.
- "Part two?" when there is one to make.
- "Save this for the night before."
- "Comment your letter" only on a choice film, and at most twice a week per account.
- The spoken tagline: rare.

## Before and after

> Before: "Offload holds the plan, and moves the work when you miss a day. Get a card wrong, and it comes back sooner."
>
> After: "If you said A, the problem isn't you. Paper can't move. This one moves itself."

> Before: "You're eight weeks in, and you haven't touched anatomy since week two."
>
> After: "Quick check. When did you last open anatomy? If the answer's the week you learnt it, same for most of us."
