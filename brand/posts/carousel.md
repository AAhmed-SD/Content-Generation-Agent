# Carousel

Three cards. About nine seconds, or three stills a person swipes.

## What a carousel is for

A carousel is free, data-backed and genuinely useful, even to someone who never uses Offload. It is the thing a student saves and sends to a friend.

- **Start from what students are already looking up.** The topic comes from the "For carousels" half of the Research page: the questions and guides students are searching for this week (blurting, ADHD time management, NEBDN revision, problem sets).
- **Answer it properly:** the method, why it works, and the mistake most people make.
- **Back it with something real:** a named method, a study finding already in the brief, or the mark scheme's own words. Never invent a number. Never name a source that is not in the brief.
- **Offload gets one line at the end, at most,** or none at all. The carousel is not an advert.
- **Videos are different.** They come from the "For videos" half (Virlo and Signals) and chase reach. Carousels chase saves.

## Job

Teach the same idea in three beats, then stop. Beat one is the line. Beat two is the fact. Beat three is what this account will not claim.

## On screen

One beat at a time. Under twelve words each. The object stays. The words change.

## Success

People reach the third card. If the third card is quiet, the idea was finished on card two and the third was padding. Cut it next time.
