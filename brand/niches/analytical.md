This account advertises Offload to students in maths, economics, and computer science. The post relates to unfinished problem sets. It does not work the question.

## Line

A student should recognise the unfinished set and save the card. The post never teaches the method and never shows a worked answer.

Students on engineering, physics, maths and other analysis-based degrees (statistics, data science, computer science, economics and similar), plus A-level maths, further maths and physics students heading there (decided 2026-11-07). ## UK context (researched 2026-11-05)

Background for the team and the writer. **Never put these numbers on a card or in a film** unless the week's brief carries them. Use them to pick true, timely subjects.

### Who they are

UK undergraduates in maths, economics and computer science. Their week runs on problem sheets: a new set most weeks, marked or not, plus coursework and labs.

### Their exams, and when

| What | When |
| --- | --- |
| Exams on first-term modules | January at many UK universities, straight after Christmas. Some sit everything in summer. |
| Exams on second-term and year-long modules | May to June. |
| Problem sheets and coursework | Weekly or fortnightly, all term. |

Dates are set by each university. Never give one.

### A typical day

- Lectures in the morning, often two or three back to back.
- A problem class, tutorial or lab in the afternoon.
- The problem sheet in the library or at home, often late, often from the model solution.
- **A paid job for most of them:** about two-thirds of full-time UK undergraduates work in term time, around 14 hours a week among those who work (HEPI and Advance HE, 2026). The average study week is already near 29 hours.

The thread: **the sheet is always open, and revision for January gets pushed to "over Christmas".**

### What is true right now

- **The gap is between watching and doing.** Rereading solutions and watching lectures at double speed feel productive. Closing the solution and doing it from blank is what transfers to the exam.
- **November is the middle of term:** sheets pile up while January exams get closer.
