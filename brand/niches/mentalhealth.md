# Time, for ADHD and other mental health

This account is for kids and young people with ADHD and other mental health difficulties. The post is about managing time. It is not treatment and it does not tell them what they have.

## Line

The afternoon disappeared.

## Goal

A young person should see the day as too many starts, and Offload as the next block of time. The post does not assess them.

## Audience

Kids and young people with ADHD and other mental health difficulties. Some of them are under 18.

## Pains

A day with too many starts. An afternoon that disappeared. A caption that turns the comment into a case.

## Vocabulary

Day, time, card, start, next. Do not use a condition as a personality.

## Exams

None. Do not attach an exam to this account.

## Rules

The post is about managing time. Do not name a condition as theirs. No treatment claim. Age-appropriate. If the line is distressing, the caption points to help and stops. Both stamps before the pack opens. Do not describe how Offload works.

## UK context (researched 2026-11-05)

Background for the team and the writer. **Never put these numbers on a card or in a film** unless the week's brief carries them. Use them to pick true, timely subjects.

### Who they are

Young people in the UK, at school, college or university, who lose afternoons to starting. Many are waiting for an assessment, many have no diagnosis, many never will. **The account never asks, never names a condition as theirs, and never treats.** It is about time.

### Their exams, and when

They sit the same exams as everyone else at their stage: GCSEs and A-levels from 10 May to 18 June 2027, and university exams in January and May to June. What differs is how the weeks before go.

### A typical day

- Classes or lectures in the morning, often fine.
- **The afternoon disappears:** a lot of small starts, switching, the phone, nothing finished.
- An evening burst of work near a deadline, then exhaustion.
- A to-do list that grows, and a new planner every few weeks.

The thread: **starting is the hard part, not ability.**

### What is true right now

- **NHS waits for an adult ADHD assessment are long.** University support pages describe waits of up to two or three years, and some reports describe far longer backlogs. Many viewers are waiting, so content that helps today matters.
- **Support does not always need a diagnosis.** Several UK universities offer support on a GP referral letter. Disabled Students' Allowances help some students. Never give eligibility advice: point to the university's disability service.

### What helps, and what to say

- Show the next small block, not a whole new system.
- Short blocks, visible time, one next thing, rest that counts, a plan that moves when a day goes wrong.
- Never: "you have", "this is so ADHD", "your brain is broken", treatment, medication, a diagnosis quiz.
