# Medicine, dentistry, nursing, radiology

This account advertises Offload to students in medicine, dentistry, nursing, and radiology. The post relates to their revision and their workload. It does not give clinical advice.

## Line

## Goal

A student in medicine, dentistry, nursing, or radiology should recognise the unfinished reading and save the card. The post never tells them what a finding, a drug, or a result means.

## Audience

Students on clinical, healthcare and science degrees: medicine, dentistry, nursing, radiography, and other healthcare or science-related degrees (pharmacy, midwifery, physiotherapy, biomedical science, biology and similar) (decided 2026-11-07). Neurodivergent students are the time account's audience, not this one's.

## UK context (researched 2026-11-05)

Background for the team and the writer. **Never put these numbers on a card or in a film** unless the week's brief carries them. Use them to pick true, timely subjects.

### Who they are

UK students on four courses that mix lectures with clinical placement. Each one is regulated, and each one's revision has to fit around shifts.

- **Medicine** (MBBS or MBChB, five or six years, GMC).
- **Dentistry** (BDS, five years, GDC).
- **Nursing** (BSc, three years, NMC: adult, child, mental health or learning disability).
- **Diagnostic radiography** (BSc, three years, HCPC).

### Their exams, and when

| Course | What they sit | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medicine | The UKMLA: the **AKT** (written) and the **CPSA** (clinical, OSCE-style), plus their school's own exams and finals. | Set by each medical school, usually January to summer of the final year. UCL lists the AKT on 2 to 3 February 2027. Earlier years sit end-of-year exams and OSCEs in late spring and summer. |
| Dentistry | University written exams and clinical assessments, then BDS finals. | End of each year, with finals in the final year. Dates set by each dental school. |
| Nursing | University assignments and exams, and the practice assessment document (PAD) signed off on placement. No national exam. | Throughout the year, at the end of each placement block. |
| Radiography | University exams and assignments, and placement competencies signed off. | End of modules and placement blocks. Dates set by each university. |

### A typical day

- **Medicine:** on the ward by 9am for the ward round, often writing the notes. Then teaching, clinics, taking bloods and talking to patients. Some weeks are one campus day and three or four hospital days. Revision happens in the evening, after placement.
- **Dentistry:** clinic sessions treating real patients under supervision, plus lab and lecture time. Stress comes mainly from academic and clinical pressure together.
- **Nursing:** long days are common: 07:30 to 19:30 or 20:30, twelve hours or more with breaks, or shorter 7.5-hour shifts, sometimes nights. Some weeks are placement, some are university. The 2,300 placement hours must be logged and signed.
- **Radiography:** placement days are often 9 to 5 (about 30 hours a week in first year), with extended hours and some nights later in the course. Final-year students report the same pressures as qualified radiographers: time management and workload.

The thread through all four: **revision has to fit around a rota they don't control.**

### What is true right now

- **UKMLA dates are set by each medical school**, not nationally. Never say a national date.
- **The MLA content map changed for 2026.** Resources written before January 2026 may not match. "Is your question bank even on the new map?" is a true, useful hook.
- **The NMC is consulting on nursing hours** (the 2026 practice learning review proposes cutting the total from 4,600 to 3,600, keeping 2,300 in practice). It is a proposal, not a rule. Do not say it has changed.
