# LinkedIn

Three accounts, two posts a day each. Read with `product-facts.md` (what may be claimed) and `safety.md` (what is never said). Posts are drafted on the desk, approved, then copied into LinkedIn by hand or scheduled through Blotato, which posts to personal profiles and company pages.

## The shape of a post

1. **The first line is the hook.** It shows before "see more". Specific, under about twelve words, no emoji at the start.
2. **Short paragraphs.** One to three lines each, with a blank line between.
3. **One idea.** If it needs "and also", it is two posts.
4. **End on a question or one soft line.** "What did your first placement week look like?" Not "Like and share".
5. **No link in the post.** LinkedIn shows posts with links to fewer people. Say "link in the comments" and put it there.
6. **Three hashtags at most,** at the very end, or none.
7. **About 600 to 1,300 characters.** Long enough to say something, short enough to finish.

## What Offload is, in one breath

Offload builds your revision plan from your notes and exam dates, moves it when life happens, tests you as you go, and brings topics back before you forget. You just click the link in your calendar and show up.

## Rules

- British English. No price, ever. No trial length.
- No named students, patients or customers. No screenshots of private messages.
- Under-18s: GCSE and A-level students are often under 18. Talk to teachers, parents and schools about them, never to them directly on LinkedIn.
- The time account's rules hold here too: never name a condition as someone else's, never treat.
- Comparisons: never name a competitor.
