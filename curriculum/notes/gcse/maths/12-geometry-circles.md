# Geometry: circles and circle theorems

This file covers the names of the parts of a circle and the circle theorems, with how to use and prove them.

## Overview

The parts of a circle are on both tiers. The circle theorems are Higher only on AQA, Edexcel and Eduqas. Circumference, area, arcs and sectors are in [13-geometry-mensuration.md](13-geometry-mensuration.md). The equation of a circle is in [06-algebra-graphs.md](06-algebra-graphs.md).

### Board differences

- AQA and Edexcel: G9 (both tiers) and G10 (Higher only).
- OCR J560: 8.05a (circle words) and 8.05b to 8.05h, one statement per theorem. OCR places 8.05f (a tangent is perpendicular to the radius) partly on Foundation; the other theorems are Higher only.
- Eduqas C300QS: FG8 (circle words) on Foundation, HG9 and HG10 on Higher.

## G9 Parts of a circle

*Refs: AQA G9 · Edexcel G9 · OCR 8.05a · Eduqas FG8, HG9. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Know and use these words: centre, radius, chord, diameter, circumference, tangent, arc, sector, segment.

### Explained

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Centre | The middle point, the same distance from every point on the circle |
| Radius | A line from the centre to the circle (plural radii) |
| Diameter | A chord through the centre; twice the radius |
| Circumference | The distance all the way round the circle |
| Chord | A straight line joining two points on the circle |
| Tangent | A straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point |
| Arc | Part of the circumference |
| Sector | A "pizza slice" bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Segment | The region between a chord and its arc |

A chord splits a circle into a minor (smaller) and a major (larger) segment. Two radii split it into a minor and a major sector.

### Worked example

Name the region between a chord and the arc it cuts off.

1. A segment.

### Common mistakes

- Mixing up sector and segment. A sector has two radii as edges; a segment has a chord.
- Calling a tangent a chord. A tangent only touches the circle.

## G10 Circle theorems

*Refs: AQA G10 · Edexcel G10 · OCR 8.05b, 8.05c, 8.05d, 8.05e, 8.05f, 8.05g, 8.05h · Eduqas HG10. Tier: Higher only.*

### What you need to know

(HT only) Apply and prove these theorems, and use them to prove related results:

1. The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference, when both stand on the same arc.
2. The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
3. Angles in the same segment (standing on the same arc) are equal.
4. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
5. A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
6. Two tangents from an outside point are equal in length.
7. The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord (and the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre).
8. The alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.

### Explained

(HT only) Before using a theorem, look for radii: any two radii make an isosceles triangle, which is the most common hidden fact.

(HT only) Writing reasons: use the full theorem wording, for example "the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference", not "centre = 2 × edge".

(HT only) A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle. If one vertex is the centre, it is not cyclic.

(HT only) Proof of the angle at the centre (for the standard case): join the point on the circumference, P, to the centre O and extend the line. The two triangles formed with the arc's end points are isosceles (two radii each). If the base angles in one triangle are x, the exterior angle at O is 2x (exterior angle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles). Similarly for the other triangle with y. So the angle at the centre is 2x + 2y = 2(x + y), twice the angle at P.

(HT only) The angle in a semicircle follows: the angle at the centre is 180° (a straight line), so the angle at the circumference is 90°.

(HT only) Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral follow too: the two angles at the centre on the two arcs add up to 360°, so the opposite angles at the circumference add up to 180°.

### Worked example

(HT only) O is the centre of a circle. A, B and C are on the circle. Angle AOC = 136°. Find angle ABC, where B is on the major arc.

1. Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
2. Angle ABC = 136 ÷ 2 = 68°.

(HT only) PQRS is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle PQR = 104°. Find angle PSR.

1. Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
2. Angle PSR = 180 − 104 = 76°.

(HT only) TA is a tangent to a circle with centre O at A. Angle ATO = 28°. Find angle AOT.

1. Tangent is perpendicular to the radius, so angle OAT = 90°.
2. Angle AOT = 180 − 90 − 28 = 62°.

(HT only) A tangent touches a circle at A. Chord AB makes an angle of 55° with the tangent. C is a point on the circle in the other segment. Find angle ACB.

1. Alternate segment theorem: angle ACB = 55°.

(HT only) A and B are points on a circle with centre O. M is the midpoint of chord AB. OM = 5 cm and the radius is 13 cm. Find AB.

1. The line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it, so triangle OMA is right-angled at M.
2. AM = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm.
3. AB = 24 cm.

### Common mistakes

- (HT only) Using the angle at the centre theorem when the angles stand on different arcs.
- (HT only) Treating a quadrilateral as cyclic when one vertex is the centre.
- (HT only) Forgetting that triangles made from two radii are isosceles.
- (HT only) Giving vague reasons that do not name the theorem.

## Check questions

1. Write down the name of a straight line that touches a circle at one point only. [1 mark]
2. What is the difference between a sector and a segment? [2 marks]
3. (HT only) A, B and C lie on a circle with centre O. Angle ABC = 47°. Find angle AOC, giving a reason. [2 marks]
4. (HT only) AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. Angle CAB = 33°. Find angle CBA, giving reasons. [3 marks]
5. (HT only) ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle DAB = 2x and angle BCD = x + 30. Find x. [3 marks]
6. (HT only) PA and PB are tangents from P to a circle with centre O. Angle APB = 50°. Find angle AOB, giving reasons. [3 marks]
7. (HT only) A chord of length 16 cm is 6 cm from the centre of a circle. Find the radius. [3 marks]
8. (HT only) Prove that the angle in a semicircle is a right angle. [3 marks]
9. (HT only) A tangent at T meets chord TU at an angle of 72°. V is on the major arc. Find angle TVU and name the theorem. [2 marks]

## Mark scheme

1. A tangent (1).
2. A sector is bounded by two radii and an arc (1); a segment is bounded by a chord and an arc (1).
3. 94° (1); the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference (1).
4. Angle ACB = 90°, angle in a semicircle (1); angles in a triangle add up to 180° (1); CBA = 57° (1).
5. 2x + x + 30 = 180, because opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180° (1); 3x = 150 (1); x = 50 (1).
6. Angles OAP and OBP are 90°, tangent perpendicular to radius (1); angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360° (1); AOB = 360 − 90 − 90 − 50 = 130° (1).
7. Perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord, so half-chord 8 cm (1); r² = 8² + 6² = 100 (1); r = 10 cm (1).
8. Let AB be a diameter, O the centre, C on the circle; OA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles OAC and OBC are isosceles (1); angles: OAC = OCA = x and OBC = OCB = y (1); in triangle ABC, 2x + 2y = 180, so x + y = 90, and angle ACB = x + y = 90° (1). Accept a proof from the angle at the centre being 180°.
9. 72° (1); alternate segment theorem (1).
