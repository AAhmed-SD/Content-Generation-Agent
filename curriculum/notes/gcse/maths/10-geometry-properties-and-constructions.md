# Geometry: properties of shapes, angles and constructions

This file covers geometric words and notation, symmetry, ruler-and-compass constructions and loci, angle facts (including parallel lines and polygons), the properties of triangles and quadrilaterals, geometric reasoning and proof, 3D solids, and plans and elevations.

## Overview

Everything here is on both tiers. The Higher-only part of G6 is writing formal proofs (for example proving a circle theorem or a congruence result); Foundation students give reasons and simple proofs using known facts.

### Board differences

- AQA and Edexcel: G1, G2, G3, G4, G6, G12 and G13.
- OCR J560: section 8 except circles (8.01 conventions and terms, 8.02 constructions, 8.03 angles, 8.04 properties of polygons, 8.06 3D shapes). OCR 8.01f (geometrical instruments) and 8.01g (coordinates) are also here. Circles (8.05) are in [12-geometry-circles.md](12-geometry-circles.md).
- Eduqas C300QS: FG1 to FG4, FG6, FG10 and FG11 on Foundation; HG1 to HG4, HG6, HG12 and HG13 on Higher. Eduqas FG6 names Pythagoras' theorem and the equal base angles of an isosceles triangle as results to derive. Eduqas FG4 and HG4 name special triangles as well as quadrilaterals.

## G1 Geometric terms, notation and symmetry

*Refs: AQA G1 · Edexcel G1 · OCR 8.01a, 8.01b, 8.01c, 8.01e, 8.04c, 8.06b · Eduqas FG1, HG1. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use the terms point, line, line segment, vertex (plural vertices), edge, plane, parallel, perpendicular, right angle, polygon, regular polygon.
- Know the types of angle: acute (less than 90°), right (90°), obtuse (between 90° and 180°), reflex (between 180° and 360°).
- Label triangles: vertices with capital letters (A, B, C), the side opposite angle A with a small a, and so on. Angle ABC (or ∠ABC) is the angle at B between BA and BC.
- Line symmetry (reflection symmetry) and rotational symmetry, with its order.
- Draw diagrams from written descriptions.

### Explained

A regular polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. A regular n-sided polygon has n lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order n.

The order of rotational symmetry is the number of times a shape looks the same in one full turn. A shape with no rotational symmetry has order 1.

Polygon names: triangle (3), quadrilateral (4), pentagon (5), hexagon (6), heptagon (7), octagon (8), nonagon (9), decagon (10).

In ∠PQR the middle letter is the vertex of the angle.

### Worked example

Describe the symmetry of a rhombus that is not a square.

1. Two lines of symmetry (the diagonals).
2. Rotational symmetry of order 2.

### Common mistakes

- Naming the angle by the wrong letter: in ∠ABC the angle is at B, not A.
- Giving a rectangle four lines of symmetry. It has two (its diagonals are not lines of symmetry).
- Saying a shape with no rotational symmetry has order 0. It has order 1.

## G2 Constructions and loci

*Refs: AQA G2 · Edexcel G2 · OCR 8.01f, 8.02a, 8.02b, 8.02c, 8.02d · Eduqas FG2, HG2. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use a ruler and compasses to construct:
  - the perpendicular bisector of a line segment
  - a perpendicular to a line from a point, or at a point on the line
  - the bisector of an angle.
- Use these to solve loci problems.
- The shortest distance from a point to a line is along the perpendicular.
- Construct triangles from given sides and angles.

### Explained

A locus is the set of all points that obey a rule.

| Rule | Locus |
| --- | --- |
| A fixed distance from a point | A circle |
| A fixed distance from a line segment | Two parallel lines joined by semicircles at the ends |
| Equidistant from two points | The perpendicular bisector of the line joining them |
| Equidistant from two intersecting lines | The bisector of the angle between them |

Leave your construction arcs showing; they earn the marks.

Perpendicular bisector of AB: with the compasses set to more than half of AB, draw arcs from A and from B above and below the line. Join the two crossing points.

Angle bisector at B: draw an arc from B cutting both arms. From each of those two points draw arcs of the same radius that cross. Join B to the crossing point.

Perpendicular from a point P to a line: draw an arc from P cutting the line twice; then construct the perpendicular bisector of those two points.

Triangle with sides 7 cm, 5 cm and 4 cm: draw the 7 cm side, then arcs of radius 5 cm and 4 cm from its ends. Where the arcs cross is the third vertex.

Region questions: shade the area that satisfies every condition, such as "closer to A than B" (the side of the perpendicular bisector nearer A) and "within 3 cm of C" (inside the circle).

### Worked example

A goat is tied to a post with a 4 m rope in a field. The post is 2 m from a straight fence. Describe the region where the goat can graze.

1. Locus: all points within 4 m of the post, a circle of radius 4 m.
2. The fence cuts off part of the circle, since the post is only 2 m from it.
3. The goat grazes inside the circle on the post's side of the fence.

On a scale drawing, a phone mast must be closer to town P than to town Q and within 20 km of town R. Explain how to find the possible positions.

1. Construct the perpendicular bisector of PQ and take the side containing P.
2. Draw a circle of radius 20 km (to scale) centred on R.
3. The mast can be in the overlap of these two regions.

### Common mistakes

- Rubbing out construction arcs.
- Changing the compass width between the arcs of the same step.
- Shading the wrong side of a bisector.

## G3 Angle facts

*Refs: AQA G3 · Edexcel G3 · OCR 8.03a, 8.03b, 8.03c, 8.03d · Eduqas FG3, HG3. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Angles at a point add up to 360°.
- Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- With parallel lines: alternate angles are equal; corresponding angles are equal; co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180°.
- Angles in a triangle add up to 180°; angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°.
- Interior and exterior angles of polygons:

```latex
\text{sum of interior angles} = (n - 2) \times 180^\circ
```

```latex
\text{exterior angles sum to } 360^\circ \qquad \text{each exterior angle of a regular } n\text{-gon} = \frac{360^\circ}{n}
```

- Interior angle + exterior angle = 180° at each vertex.

### Explained

Alternate angles make a Z shape, corresponding angles make an F shape, and co-interior angles make a C (or U) shape between the parallel lines. In answers, use the proper names, not the letters.

A polygon with n sides can be split into n − 2 triangles from one vertex, which is why the interior angles add up to (n − 2) × 180°.

To find the number of sides of a regular polygon from an angle, use the exterior angle: n = 360 ÷ exterior angle.

### Worked example

Find the interior angle of a regular decagon.

1. Exterior angle: 360 ÷ 10 = 36°.
2. Interior angle: 180 − 36 = 144°.

A regular polygon has interior angles of 156°. How many sides does it have?

1. Exterior angle: 180 − 156 = 24°.
2. n = 360 ÷ 24 = 15.

In a diagram, AB is parallel to CD. A line crosses them, making angle 68° with AB. Find the co-interior angle on CD and give reasons.

1. Co-interior angles add up to 180°: 180 − 68 = 112°.

### Common mistakes

- Calling angles "Z angles" instead of alternate angles. Exams want the correct name.
- Using (n − 2) × 180 and forgetting to divide by n for one angle of a regular polygon.
- Applying parallel-line facts to lines that are not marked parallel.

## G4 Properties of triangles, quadrilaterals and polygons

*Refs: AQA G4 · Edexcel G4 · OCR 8.04a, 8.04b, 8.04c · Eduqas FG4, HG4. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Properties of special triangles: equilateral, isosceles, scalene, right-angled.
- Properties of special quadrilaterals: square, rectangle, parallelogram, trapezium, kite, rhombus.
- Use the right language to describe and classify shapes.

### Explained

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Square | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | All 90° | Equal, bisect each other at 90° |
| Rectangle | Opposite sides equal and parallel | All 90° | Equal, bisect each other |
| Parallelogram | Opposite sides equal and parallel | Opposite angles equal | Bisect each other |
| Rhombus | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | Opposite angles equal | Bisect each other at 90° |
| Kite | Two pairs of equal adjacent sides | One pair of opposite angles equal | Cross at 90°; one bisects the other |
| Trapezium | One pair of parallel sides | Co-interior angles between the parallel sides add to 180° | No special property (isosceles trapezium: equal diagonals) |

An isosceles triangle has two equal sides and two equal base angles. An equilateral triangle has three equal sides and three 60° angles.

### Worked example

An isosceles triangle has one angle of 40°. Find the possible sizes of the other two angles.

1. If 40° is a base angle: the other base angle is 40°, and the third is 100°.
2. If 40° is the top angle: the base angles are (180 − 40)/2 = 70° each.

### Common mistakes

- Thinking a square is not a rectangle or a rhombus. It is both.
- Giving only one case in isosceles triangle questions.

## G6 Geometric reasoning and proof

*Refs: AQA G6 · Edexcel G6 · OCR 8.03a, 8.03b, 8.03c, 8.03d, 8.04a, 8.04b · Eduqas FG6, HG6. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- Apply angle facts, triangle congruence, similarity and properties of quadrilaterals to find unknown angles and lengths, giving reasons.
- Derive results about angles and sides, such as the angle sum of a triangle and the equal base angles of an isosceles triangle, and use known results to obtain simple proofs.
- (HT only) Construct formal geometric proofs, including congruence proofs and proofs of circle theorems.

### Explained

A geometric reason names the fact used. For example: "angles on a straight line add up to 180°", "alternate angles are equal", "base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal".

Proof that the angles in a triangle add up to 180°: draw a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side. The two angles next to the vertex are alternate angles to the triangle's base angles. The three angles at that vertex lie on a straight line, so they add up to 180°.

Proof that an exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two opposite interior angles: the exterior angle and the adjacent interior angle make 180° (straight line); the three interior angles also make 180°; so the exterior angle equals the other two interior angles.

(HT only) A congruence proof states the three pairs of equal sides or angles, gives a reason for each, and names the condition (SSS, SAS, ASA or RHS). See G5 in [11-geometry-transformations-congruence-and-similarity.md](11-geometry-transformations-congruence-and-similarity.md).

### Worked example

ABC is a triangle. D lies on BC extended beyond C. Angle BAC = 52° and angle ABC = 61°. Find angle ACD, giving a reason.

1. Exterior angle = sum of the two opposite interior angles.
2. Angle ACD = 52 + 61 = 113°.

(HT only) ABCD is a parallelogram. Prove that triangles ABD and CDB are congruent.

1. AB = CD (opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal).
2. AD = CB (opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal).
3. BD is common to both triangles.
4. So the triangles are congruent (SSS).

### Common mistakes

- Giving the answer without a reason when the question says "give reasons".
- Giving reasons that are not the standard facts, such as "because it looks like it".
- (HT only) Using the result you are trying to prove.

## G12 Properties of 3D shapes

*Refs: AQA G12 · Edexcel G12 · OCR 8.01d, 8.06a · Eduqas FG10, HG12. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Identify the faces, surfaces, edges and vertices of cubes, cuboids, prisms, cylinders, pyramids, cones and spheres.
- Recognise nets of 3D solids.

### Explained

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cube or cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Tetrahedron (triangular-based pyramid) | 4 | 6 | 4 |
| Cylinder | 3 surfaces (2 flat, 1 curved) | 2 curved edges | 0 |
| Cone | 2 surfaces (1 flat, 1 curved) | 1 curved edge | 1 |
| Sphere | 1 curved surface | 0 | 0 |

A prism has the same cross-section all the way through. A prism whose cross-section has n sides has n + 2 faces, 3n edges and 2n vertices. A pyramid with an n-sided base has n + 1 faces, 2n edges and n + 1 vertices.

For polyhedra (solids with flat faces), faces + vertices − edges = 2. This is a useful check.

### Worked example

A prism has a hexagonal cross-section. Find its numbers of faces, edges and vertices.

1. n = 6. Faces: 8. Edges: 18. Vertices: 12.
2. Check: 8 + 12 − 18 = 2 ✓.

### Common mistakes

- Counting only the visible faces in a drawing.
- Calling a cylinder a prism in questions about flat faces.

## G13 Plans and elevations

*Refs: AQA G13 · Edexcel G13 · OCR 8.06b · Eduqas FG11, HG13. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Interpret and construct plans and elevations of 3D shapes.
- The plan is the view from above. The front elevation is the view from the front. The side elevation is the view from the side.

### Explained

Each view is a flat 2D drawing showing every edge you would see from that direction, drawn to scale on squared or isometric paper. Lines where the depth changes are drawn even though they are only edges, not outlines.

To build a solid from its views, start with the plan to see how many columns there are, then use the elevations to see how tall each column is.

### Worked example

A solid is made from cubes: a row of 3 cubes along the front, with 1 extra cube on top of the left-hand cube. Describe the plan, front elevation and side elevation.

1. Plan: 3 squares in a row.
2. Front elevation: 3 squares in a row with 1 square on top of the left one (an L shape).
3. Side elevation (from the left): a column of 2 squares.

### Common mistakes

- Drawing a 3D picture instead of a flat view.
- Mixing up the front and side elevations.

## Check questions

1. Write down the order of rotational symmetry of a regular hexagon and its number of lines of symmetry. [2 marks]
2. Describe the locus of points that are equidistant from two points A and B. [1 mark]
3. Explain how to construct the bisector of an angle with ruler and compasses. [2 marks]
4. Find the sum of the interior angles of a nonagon. [2 marks]
5. Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 18°. How many sides does it have, and what is each interior angle? [2 marks]
6. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One of the angles formed is 117°. Find the alternate angle and the co-interior angle to it, giving reasons. [3 marks]
7. The angles of a quadrilateral are x, 2x, 3x − 10 and 70 degrees. Find x. [3 marks]
8. Write down two properties of the diagonals of a rhombus. [2 marks]
9. In triangle PQR, PQ = PR and angle QPR = 36°. Find angle PQR, giving a reason. [2 marks]
10. Prove that the angles in a triangle add up to 180°. [3 marks]
11. (HT only) In the kite ABCD, AB = AD and CB = CD. Prove that triangles ABC and ADC are congruent. [3 marks]
12. A pentagonal prism: find the number of faces, edges and vertices. [2 marks]
13. A solid has a plan view of a 2 by 2 square of cubes and a front elevation showing heights 1 and 3. What is the greatest number of cubes it could contain? [2 marks]

## Mark scheme

1. Order 6 (1); 6 lines of symmetry (1).
2. The perpendicular bisector of AB (1).
3. Arc from the vertex cutting both arms (1); equal arcs from those two points crossing, then join the vertex to the crossing point (1).
4. (9 − 2) × 180 (1) = 1260° (1).
5. 360 ÷ 18 = 20 sides (1); 180 − 18 = 162° (1).
6. Alternate angle 117° (1), alternate angles are equal (1); co-interior angle 63°, co-interior angles add up to 180° (1).
7. x + 2x + 3x − 10 + 70 = 360 (1); 6x + 60 = 360 (1); x = 50 (1).
8. Any two: they bisect each other; they cross at right angles; they bisect the angles of the rhombus (2).
9. (180 − 36) ÷ 2 (1) = 72°, because base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal (and angles in a triangle add up to 180°) (1).
10. Line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side (1); two pairs of alternate angles equal (1); the three angles at the vertex lie on a straight line, so add up to 180°, and they equal the triangle's angles (1).
11. AB = AD (given) and CB = CD (given) (1); AC is common (1); congruent by SSS (1).
12. Faces 7, edges 15, vertices 10 (2). One mark for two correct.
13. The front elevation gives the tallest stack in each left-right position, so the two stacks on the left are at most 1 high and the two on the right at most 3 high: 2 × 1 + 2 × 3 (1) = 8 cubes (1).
