# Number: fractions, decimals and percentages

This file covers calculating with fractions and decimals, converting between fractions, decimals and percentages (including recurring decimals), fractions and percentages as operators, percentage change and reverse percentages, simple and compound interest, and growth and decay.

## Overview

Most of this content is on both tiers. Changing recurring decimals into fractions is Higher only, and so is working with general iterative processes in growth and decay. Percentages are listed under Ratio in the DfE content (R9), and growth and decay is R16; both are here because they build directly on fractions and decimals.

- AQA and Edexcel: N2 and N8 (the fraction and decimal parts), N10, N11, N12, R9 and R16.
- OCR J560: section 2 (2.01 fractions, 2.02 decimals, 2.03 percentages) and 5.03a (growth and decay). OCR puts fractions in ratio problems (N11) under 5.01c, which is covered in [09-ratio-and-proportion.md](09-ratio-and-proportion.md).
- Eduqas C300QS: FN10 to FN12 and HN10 to HN12; percentages are FR10 and HR10; growth and decay is FR16 and HR17 (the Higher wording adds general iterative processes).
- Iteration to solve equations is in [07-algebra-equations-and-inequalities.md](07-algebra-equations-and-inequalities.md).

## N2 Calculating with fractions and decimals

*Refs: AQA N2 · Edexcel N2 · OCR 2.01b, 2.02a, 2.02b, 2.02c · Eduqas FN2, HN2. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Add, subtract, multiply and divide proper fractions, improper fractions and mixed numbers, positive and negative.
- Add, subtract, multiply and divide decimals using written methods.
- Find equivalent fractions and simplify fractions by cancelling common factors.

### Explained

Equivalent fractions: multiply or divide the top (numerator) and bottom (denominator) by the same number. 18/24 = 3/4 after dividing both by 6.

| Operation | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Add or subtract | Write both over a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators |
| Multiply | Multiply tops together and bottoms together; cancel first if you can |
| Divide | Keep the first fraction, flip the second (use its reciprocal), then multiply |
| Mixed numbers | Change to improper fractions first for × and ÷; for + and − you can deal with the whole numbers separately, but improper fractions are safer when subtracting |

Decimals:

- Adding and subtracting: line up the decimal points.
- Multiplying: ignore the points, multiply, then put back as many decimal places as there were in total in the question.
- Dividing: multiply both numbers by 10, 100 and so on until you divide by a whole number.

### Worked example

Work out 2 3/4 − 1 5/6.

1. Improper fractions: 11/4 − 11/6.
2. Common denominator 12: 33/12 − 22/12 = 11/12.

Work out 3 1/3 × 1 4/5.

1. 10/3 × 9/5.
2. Cancel: 10 and 5 share 5; 9 and 3 share 3. So 2/1 × 3/1 = 6.

Work out 4/9 ÷ 2/3.

1. 4/9 × 3/2 = 12/18 = 2/3.

Work out 0.36 × 0.7.

1. 36 × 7 = 252.
2. Three decimal places in the question, so 0.252.

### Common mistakes

- Adding the denominators: 1/3 + 1/4 is 7/12, not 2/7.
- Dividing by flipping the first fraction instead of the second.
- Multiplying mixed numbers as wholes and fractions separately: 2 1/2 × 3 1/2 is not 6 1/4 (it is 35/4 = 8 3/4).
- Leaving an answer unsimplified when the question asks for its simplest form.

## N8 Exact answers with fractions

*Refs: AQA N8 · Edexcel N8 · OCR 2.01b · Eduqas FN8, HN8. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- Keep answers as exact fractions where a decimal would recur or need rounding.
- Exact answers with π and surds are in [02-number-indices-surds-and-standard-form.md](02-number-indices-surds-and-standard-form.md).

### Explained

1/3 is exact; 0.333 is not. If a question says "give your answer as a fraction" or "exactly", carry fractions through the whole working rather than switching to rounded decimals. Calculators have a fraction key that shows exact answers.

### Worked example

Work out 1/3 + 1/7 exactly.

1. Common denominator 21: 7/21 + 3/21 = 10/21.
2. 10/21 = 0.476190… recurs, so the fraction is the exact answer.

### Common mistakes

- Rounding part-way through and giving an inexact final answer.

## N10 Fractions and decimals, including recurring decimals

*Refs: AQA N10 · Edexcel N10 · OCR 2.02a · Eduqas FN10, HN10. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- Convert between terminating decimals and fractions, such as 3.5 = 7/2 and 0.375 = 3/8.
- A fraction in its simplest form gives a terminating decimal if the only prime factors of its denominator are 2 and 5; otherwise the decimal recurs.
- Recurring decimals are shown with dots over the repeating digits: 0.3̇ = 0.333…, 0.1̇2̇ = 0.1212…, 0.2̇14̇ = 0.214214…
- (HT only) Change recurring decimals into fractions, and fractions into recurring decimals.

### Explained

Terminating decimal to fraction: use place value. 0.375 = 375/1000; divide top and bottom by 125 to get 3/8.

Fraction to decimal: divide the numerator by the denominator (short division works for small denominators). 5/8 = 5 ÷ 8 = 0.625. 2/11 = 0.181818… = 0.1̇8̇.

(HT only) Recurring decimal to fraction, the algebra method:

1. Let x equal the decimal.
2. Multiply by 10, 100, 1000… so the repeating part lines up (one zero for each digit in the repeating block).
3. Subtract to remove the recurring tail.
4. Solve for x and simplify.

If the decimal has a non-recurring part before the repeat, either multiply to shift it past the decimal point first or subtract the two versions that share the same tail.

### Worked example

Write 0.45 as a fraction in its simplest form.

1. 0.45 = 45/100 = 9/20.

Without dividing, decide whether 7/40 terminates.

1. 40 = 2³ × 5. Only 2s and 5s, so it terminates (it is 0.175).

(HT only) Write 0.4̇7̇ as a fraction.

1. x = 0.474747…
2. 100x = 47.474747…
3. Subtract: 99x = 47.
4. x = 47/99.

(HT only) Write 0.83̇ (0.8333…) as a fraction in its simplest form.

1. x = 0.8333…
2. 10x = 8.333… and 100x = 83.333…
3. 100x − 10x = 90x = 75.
4. x = 75/90 = 5/6.

(HT only) Work out 0.2̇5̇ + 0.1̇, giving your answer as a fraction in its simplest form.

1. 0.2̇5̇ = 25/99 and 0.1̇ = 1/9 = 11/99.
2. Total: 36/99 = 4/11.

### Common mistakes

- Putting the dot over every digit, or over the wrong ones. Dots go over the first and last digits of the repeating block.
- (HT only) Multiplying by 10 when the block has two digits; the tails then do not cancel.
- (HT only) Not simplifying the final fraction.
- (HT only) In "show that" questions, not writing the subtraction step clearly.

## N11 Fractions in ratio problems

*Refs: AQA N11 · Edexcel N11 · OCR none · Eduqas FN11, HN11. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Link fractions and ratios: if A : B = 2 : 5, then A is 2/7 of the total and A is 2/5 of B.
- Most ratio work is in [09-ratio-and-proportion.md](09-ratio-and-proportion.md).

### Explained

A ratio compares parts with parts. A fraction usually compares a part with the whole. For a ratio a : b there are a + b parts altogether, so the first quantity is a/(a + b) of the total.

### Worked example

In a club the ratio of juniors to seniors is 3 : 8. What fraction of members are juniors?

1. Total parts: 3 + 8 = 11.
2. Juniors are 3/11 of the members.

Two-fifths of a bag of counters are red, and the rest are blue. Write the ratio of red to blue.

1. Blue are 3/5. Red : blue = 2/5 : 3/5 = 2 : 3.

### Common mistakes

- Writing the fraction as 3/8 (part over part) instead of 3/11 (part over whole).

## N12 Fractions and percentages as operators

*Refs: AQA N12 · Edexcel N12 · OCR 2.01c, 2.03b, 2.03c · Eduqas FN12, HN12. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Find a fraction or percentage of an amount.
- Use multipliers: 3/4 of x is 0.75x; 18% of x is 0.18x; increasing by 18% is ×1.18; decreasing by 18% is ×0.82.

### Explained

"Of" means multiply. To find 3/5 of 40, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator: 40 ÷ 5 × 3 = 24.

Non-calculator percentages build up from easy ones: 10% (divide by 10), 5% (half of 10%), 1% (divide by 100). For example 35% = 10% + 10% + 10% + 5%.

A multiplier does it in one step, which is useful on calculator papers and for repeated changes.

| Change | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Find 7% | 0.07 |
| Increase by 7% | 1.07 |
| Decrease by 7% | 0.93 |
| Increase by 120% | 2.2 |

### Worked example

Without a calculator, find 35% of £260.

1. 10% = £26, so 30% = £78.
2. 5% = £13.
3. 35% = £91.

A coat costs £84. It is reduced by 15%. Find the sale price.

1. Multiplier 0.85.
2. 84 × 0.85 = £71.40.

### Common mistakes

- Using 1.15 for a 15% decrease, or 0.15 for a 15% decrease (0.15 finds the reduction, not the new price).
- Writing money answers with one decimal place: £71.4 should be £71.40.

## R9 Percentages

*Refs: AQA R9 · Edexcel R9 · OCR 2.03a, 2.03b, 2.03c, 5.03a · Eduqas FR10, HR10. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- A percentage means a number of parts per hundred.
- Convert between fractions, decimals and percentages.
- Express one quantity as a percentage of another.
- Compare two quantities using percentages.
- Percentage increase and decrease, percentage change, reverse percentages (finding the original value), and simple interest, including in financial maths.

### Explained

Conversions:

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 1/3 | 0.3̇ | 33 1/3% |

Decimal to percentage: multiply by 100. Percentage to decimal: divide by 100.

One quantity as a percentage of another: make a fraction (with the same units) and multiply by 100.

Percentage change:

```latex
\text{percentage change} = \frac{\text{change}}{\text{original}} \times 100
```

Reverse percentages: the sale price or new value is a percentage of the original. If a price after a 20% rise is £72, then £72 is 120% of the original, so divide by 1.2.

Simple interest: the same amount of interest is added every year, worked out on the original amount only.

```latex
\text{simple interest} = \text{principal} \times \frac{\text{rate}}{100} \times \text{years}
```

### Worked example

Write 45 minutes as a percentage of 2 hours.

1. Same units: 2 hours = 120 minutes.
2. 45/120 × 100 = 37.5%.

A car was bought for £12 500 and sold for £9 750. Find the percentage loss.

1. Change: 12 500 − 9 750 = £2 750.
2. 2 750 ÷ 12 500 × 100 = 22%.

After a 15% reduction, a sofa costs £680. Find the original price.

1. £680 is 85% of the original.
2. 680 ÷ 0.85 = £800.

£3 000 is invested at 2.5% simple interest per year for 4 years. Find the total value at the end.

1. Interest per year: 3000 × 0.025 = £75.
2. Over 4 years: £300.
3. Total: £3 300.

### Common mistakes

- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change. Always divide by the original.
- In reverse percentages, finding 15% of the sale price and adding it on: 680 × 1.15 = £782, which is wrong.
- Mixing units in "one quantity as a percentage of another".
- Treating simple interest like compound interest.

## R16 Growth and decay

*Refs: AQA R16 · Edexcel R16 · OCR 5.03a · Eduqas FR16, HR17. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- Set up, solve and interpret growth and decay problems, including compound interest and depreciation.
- Repeated percentage change uses the multiplier to a power:

```latex
\text{final amount} = \text{original} \times (\text{multiplier})^{n}
```

- (HT only) Work with general iterative processes, where each value is worked out from the one before using a rule.

### Explained

Compound interest adds interest on the interest. Each year the amount is multiplied by the same multiplier, so after n years you multiply by it n times.

- Growth by r% per period: multiplier 1 + r/100.
- Decay (depreciation) by r% per period: multiplier 1 − r/100.

To find how many periods it takes to reach a target, keep multiplying (trial and improvement) until you pass it.

(HT only) An iterative process is written with a subscript: u₍ₙ₊₁₎ = 1.04uₙ − 200 means "next value = 1.04 × this value − 200". This models, for example, a savings account that earns 4% and has £200 withdrawn each year. Work out one term at a time. Use the ANS key on your calculator to speed this up.

### Worked example

£5 000 is invested at 3% compound interest per year. Find the value after 6 years.

1. Multiplier 1.03.
2. 5000 × 1.03⁶ = 5970.26 (to the nearest penny).

A van worth £24 000 depreciates by 18% each year. After how many whole years is it first worth less than £10 000?

1. Multiplier 0.82.
2. After 4 years: 24 000 × 0.82⁴ = 10 850.92. After 5 years: 24 000 × 0.82⁵ = 8 897.76.
3. Answer: 5 years.

A population of bacteria doubles every 20 minutes. It starts at 300. How many are there after 2 hours?

1. 2 hours = 6 lots of 20 minutes.
2. 300 × 2⁶ = 19 200.

(HT only) A fish stock is modelled by Pₙ₊₁ = 1.2Pₙ − 500, where Pₙ is the number of fish at the start of year n. P₁ = 4000. Find P₃.

1. P₂ = 1.2 × 4000 − 500 = 4300.
2. P₃ = 1.2 × 4300 − 500 = 4660.

### Common mistakes

- Using simple interest when the question says compound.
- Using the percentage instead of the multiplier: 5000 × 3⁶ makes no sense.
- Giving the interest when the question asks for the total amount (or the other way round).
- Stopping at the wrong year in "first worth less than" questions. Check both sides of the target.
