# Ratio, proportion and compound units

This file covers scale factors and maps, one quantity as a fraction of another, ratio notation, sharing in a ratio, ratio and fractions, proportion (direct and inverse), compound units such as speed, density and pressure, and forming proportion equations.

## Overview

Almost everything here is on both tiers. The Higher-only part is constructing equations for direct and inverse proportion (y = kx², y = k/x and so on); Foundation students interpret such equations but do not have to set them up.

## R2 Scale factors, scale diagrams and maps

*Refs: AQA R2 · Edexcel R2 · OCR none · Eduqas FR3, HR3. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use scale factors, scale diagrams and maps.
- Map scales can be given as a ratio (1 : 25 000) or in words (1 cm represents 250 m).

### Explained

A scale of 1 : 25 000 means 1 unit on the map is 25 000 of the same units in real life. Multiply map lengths by 25 000 to get real lengths, then convert units. Divide real lengths by 25 000 to get map lengths.

Areas do not scale by the same factor: see [11-geometry-transformations-congruence-and-similarity.md](11-geometry-transformations-congruence-and-similarity.md).

### Worked example

On a map with scale 1 : 50 000, two villages are 7.2 cm apart. Find the real distance in km.

1. 7.2 × 50 000 = 360 000 cm.
2. 360 000 cm = 3600 m = 3.6 km.

A garden is 18 m long. It is drawn with scale 1 cm to 2.5 m. How long is the drawing?

1. 18 ÷ 2.5 = 7.2 cm.

### Common mistakes

- Forgetting to convert cm to km (divide by 100 000).
- Multiplying when you should divide (real to map).

## R3 One quantity as a fraction of another

*Refs: AQA R3 · Edexcel R3 · OCR none · Eduqas FR4, HR4. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Express one quantity as a fraction of another, where the fraction is less than 1 or greater than 1.

### Explained

Write the first quantity over the second, in the same units, and simplify.

### Worked example

Write 45 cm as a fraction of 2 m.

1. 2 m = 200 cm.
2. 45/200 = 9/40.

Write 5 hours as a fraction of 3 hours.

1. 5/3 (greater than 1, as 5 hours is longer).

### Common mistakes

- Leaving the units different: 45/2 is wrong.
- Writing the fraction upside down.

## R4 Ratio notation and simplest form

*Refs: AQA R4 · Edexcel R4 · OCR 5.01a · Eduqas FR5, HR5. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use ratio notation a : b, and simplify ratios to their simplest form (whole numbers with no common factor).
- Write ratios in the form 1 : n or n : 1.

### Explained

Simplify a ratio like a fraction: divide every part by the highest common factor. If the parts have different units, convert first. If they contain decimals or fractions, multiply all parts to make whole numbers.

For 1 : n, divide both parts by the first part. The answer may be a decimal.

### Worked example

Simplify 1.2 kg : 450 g.

1. 1200 g : 450 g.
2. Divide by 150: 8 : 3.

Simplify 2/3 : 5/4.

1. Multiply both by 12: 8 : 15.

Write 8 : 20 in the form 1 : n.

1. Divide by 8: 1 : 2.5.

### Common mistakes

- Simplifying without converting units.
- Including the units in the final ratio.

## R5 Dividing in a ratio and ratio problems

*Refs: AQA R5 · Edexcel R5 · OCR 5.01a, 5.01b, 5.01c, 5.01d · Eduqas FR6, HR6. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Divide a quantity into two or more parts in a given part : part or part : whole ratio.
- Solve ratio problems in context, including best-buy problems and recipes.

### Explained

Sharing in a ratio:

1. Add the parts to find the total number of shares.
2. Divide the amount by that total to find one share.
3. Multiply by each part.

If you are told the difference between two parts, or the size of one part, use that to find one share instead.

Best buys: compare the cost per unit (for example per gram) or the amount per penny. The better value is the lower cost per unit.

### Worked example

Share £360 in the ratio 2 : 3 : 7.

1. Total parts: 12. One part: £30.
2. £60, £90, £210.

Red and blue counters are in the ratio 5 : 2. There are 21 more red than blue. How many counters are there?

1. The difference is 5 − 2 = 3 parts, which is 21. One part is 7.
2. Total: 7 parts × 7 = 49 counters.

A 400 g jar costs £2.60; a 650 g jar costs £4.10. Which is better value?

1. 260 ÷ 400 = 0.65p per g. 410 ÷ 650 = 0.6307…p per g.
2. The 650 g jar is better value.

### Common mistakes

- Dividing the amount by one of the ratio numbers rather than by the total number of parts.
- In difference problems, treating 21 as one of the parts rather than the gap between them.

## R6 Multiplicative relationships as a ratio or fraction

*Refs: AQA R6 · Edexcel R6 · OCR 5.01b, 5.01c · Eduqas FR7, HR7. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Express a multiplicative relationship between two quantities as a ratio or a fraction.

### Explained

If A is three times B, then A : B = 3 : 1 and B is 1/3 of A. If y is 2/5 of x, then y : x = 2 : 5.

### Worked example

Ben's pay is 1.5 times Cara's. Write the ratio of Ben's pay to Cara's in its simplest form.

1. 1.5 : 1 = 3 : 2.

### Common mistakes

- Writing the ratio in the wrong order. The order matches the order of the words.

## R7 Proportion as equality of ratios

*Refs: AQA R7 · Edexcel R7 · OCR 5.02a · Eduqas FR8, HR8. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Understand and use proportion as two equal ratios, for example scaling a recipe.
- Use the unitary method: find the value of one, then scale.

### Explained

If 4 tickets cost £26, then 1 ticket costs £6.50 and 7 tickets cost £45.50. The ratio of tickets to cost stays the same.

### Worked example

A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of rice. How much rice is needed for 10 people?

1. Per person: 450 ÷ 6 = 75 g.
2. 10 people: 750 g.

A recipe for 8 cakes uses 200 g flour and 3 eggs. What is the greatest number of cakes that can be made with 900 g flour and 10 eggs?

1. Flour allows 900 ÷ 200 × 8 = 36 cakes.
2. Eggs allow 10 ÷ 3 × 8 = 26.6… cakes, so 26.
3. Eggs run out first: 26 cakes. (If cakes are made in whole batches of 8, the answer is 3 batches = 24 cakes; read the question carefully.)

### Common mistakes

- Adding instead of multiplying when scaling.
- Not checking which ingredient runs out first.

## R8 Ratios, fractions and linear functions

*Refs: AQA R8 · Edexcel R8 · OCR 5.01c, 5.01d · Eduqas FR9, HR9. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Relate ratios to fractions and to linear functions: if y : x = 3 : 2, then y = 1.5x, a straight line through the origin with gradient 1.5.

### Explained

A ratio that stays constant describes direct proportion, so its graph is a straight line through the origin. The gradient is the multiplier. Fractions of a whole come from the parts: in a : b, the first part is a/(a + b) of the total.

### Worked example

In a drink, juice : water = 2 : 7. Write a formula for the amount of water w in terms of the amount of juice j.

1. w/j = 7/2, so w = 3.5j.

### Common mistakes

- Writing w = 2/7 j (the ratio flipped).

## R10 Direct and inverse proportion

*Refs: AQA R10 · Edexcel R10 · OCR 5.02a, 5.02b · Eduqas FR11, HR11. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Solve problems with direct and inverse proportion, including in graph and algebraic form.
- Direct proportion: when one quantity doubles, so does the other. The graph is a straight line through the origin.
- Inverse proportion: when one quantity doubles, the other halves. Their product stays constant.

### Explained

Direct: 5 kg costs £12, so 1 kg costs £2.40.

Inverse: if 6 workers take 10 days, the total work is 60 worker-days. 4 workers would take 60 ÷ 4 = 15 days (assuming they work at the same rate).

### Worked example

It takes 3 pumps 8 hours to empty a tank. How long would 4 pumps take?

1. Total: 3 × 8 = 24 pump-hours.
2. 24 ÷ 4 = 6 hours.

£1 = €1.16. Convert €290 to pounds.

1. 290 ÷ 1.16 = £250.

### Common mistakes

- Treating inverse proportion as direct: more workers do not take more time.

## R11 Compound units: speed, rates, density and pressure

*Refs: AQA R11 · Edexcel R11 · OCR 10.01b · Eduqas FR12, HR12. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use compound units such as speed, rates of pay, unit pricing, density and pressure.
- Know and use:

```latex
\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}} \qquad \text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}} \qquad \text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}
```

- Units: speed in m/s or km/h; density in g/cm³ or kg/m³; pressure in N/m² (pascals, Pa) or N/cm².
- Convert compound units, for example km/h to m/s.

### Explained

A compound unit combines two units, and its name tells you the formula: km per hour is km ÷ hours; grams per cm³ is grams ÷ cm³.

A formula triangle can help you rearrange: distance = speed × time; time = distance ÷ speed; mass = density × volume; force = pressure × area.

To convert km/h to m/s: multiply by 1000 (km to m), then divide by 3600 (hours to seconds). So 72 km/h = 72 000 ÷ 3600 = 20 m/s.

Average speed for a whole journey is total distance ÷ total time, not the mean of the speeds.

Eduqas lists density and pressure as a separate statement (FR2, HR2), stressing the meaning: density is how much mass is packed into each unit of volume; pressure is how concentrated a force is over an area. The same force on a smaller area gives a greater pressure.

### Worked example

A train travels 156 km in 1 hour 12 minutes. Find its average speed in km/h.

1. 1 hour 12 minutes = 1.2 hours.
2. 156 ÷ 1.2 = 130 km/h.

A gold bar has mass 9.65 kg and volume 500 cm³. Find its density in g/cm³.

1. 9.65 kg = 9650 g.
2. 9650 ÷ 500 = 19.3 g/cm³.

A box of weight 360 N rests on a face measuring 0.6 m by 0.4 m. Find the pressure.

1. Area = 0.24 m².
2. Pressure = 360 ÷ 0.24 = 1500 N/m².

Metal A (density 8 g/cm³) and metal B (density 3 g/cm³) are mixed: 200 cm³ of A and 300 cm³ of B. Find the density of the mixture.

1. Mass of A = 1600 g; mass of B = 900 g; total 2500 g.
2. Volume 500 cm³.
3. Density = 2500 ÷ 500 = 5 g/cm³.

### Common mistakes

- Writing 1 hour 12 minutes as 1.12 hours.
- Averaging two densities or two speeds instead of using totals.
- Leaving mass in kg and volume in cm³ in the same calculation.

## R13 Proportion equations

*Refs: AQA R13 · Edexcel R13 · OCR 5.02a, 5.02b · Eduqas FR14, HR14. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- X is inversely proportional to Y is the same as X is proportional to 1/Y.
- Interpret equations that describe direct and inverse proportion.
- (HT only) Construct equations for direct and inverse proportion, including with squares, cubes and roots, find the constant k, and use the equation.

### Explained

The symbol ∝ means "is proportional to".

| Statement | Equation |
| --- | --- |
| y ∝ x | y = kx |
| y ∝ x² | y = kx² |
| y ∝ √x | y = k√x |
| y is inversely proportional to x | y = k/x |
| y is inversely proportional to x² | y = k/x² |

(HT only) Method:

1. Write the equation with k.
2. Substitute the given pair of values to find k.
3. Write the equation with the value of k.
4. Use it to answer the question.

### Worked example

y is inversely proportional to x. Interpret the equation y = 24/x when x is doubled.

1. If x doubles, y halves.

(HT only) d is directly proportional to the square of t. When t = 4, d = 40. Find d when t = 6.

1. d = kt².
2. 40 = k × 16, so k = 2.5.
3. d = 2.5t². When t = 6, d = 2.5 × 36 = 90.

(HT only) F is inversely proportional to the square of r. F = 20 when r = 3. Find r when F = 5.

1. F = k/r².
2. 20 = k/9, so k = 180.
3. 5 = 180/r², r² = 36, r = 6.

### Common mistakes

- Using y = k + x instead of y = kx.
- (HT only) Forgetting to square (or root) when substituting.
- (HT only) Not writing the final equation with the value of k, which often earns a mark.
