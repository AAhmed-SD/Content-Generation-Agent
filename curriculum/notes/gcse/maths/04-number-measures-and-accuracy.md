# Number: measures, units and accuracy

This file covers standard units and converting between them (including area and volume units), estimating and checking, rounding to decimal places and significant figures, error intervals, and limits of accuracy with upper and lower bounds.

## Overview

Everything here is on both tiers except calculations with upper and lower bounds, which are Higher only. Compound units (speed, density, pressure) are in [09-ratio-and-proportion.md](09-ratio-and-proportion.md).

### Board differences

- AQA and Edexcel: N13 to N16, R1 and G14.
- OCR J560: 4.01a (rounding), 4.01b (estimation), 4.01c (upper and lower bounds, with the calculation part Higher only) and 10.01a (units of measurement).
- Eduqas C300QS: FN13 to FN16 and HN13 to HN16; unit conversion is FR1 and HR1; units of measure in geometry are FG12 and HG14.

## N13 Standard units of measure

*Refs: AQA N13 · Edexcel N13 · OCR 10.01a · Eduqas FN13, HN13. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use standard units of mass, length, time, money and other measures, including with decimals.
- Know the metric prefixes and conversions: 1 km = 1000 m, 1 m = 100 cm, 1 cm = 10 mm, 1 kg = 1000 g, 1 tonne = 1000 kg, 1 litre = 1000 ml, 1 litre = 1000 cm³, 1 ml = 1 cm³.
- Time: 60 seconds in a minute, 60 minutes in an hour, 24 hours in a day; 12-hour and 24-hour clock.

### Explained

To convert to a smaller unit, multiply (there will be more of them). To convert to a larger unit, divide.

Time is not decimal. 2.5 hours is 2 hours 30 minutes, but 2.25 hours is 2 hours 15 minutes, and 1 hour 20 minutes is 1 1/3 hours (about 1.33 hours), not 1.2 hours.

Money: give answers to 2 decimal places in pounds (£4.50, not £4.5), or in pence. Do not mix £ and p in one answer.

### Worked example

A recipe needs 750 g of flour for one batch. How many full batches can be made from a 2.5 kg bag?

1. 2.5 kg = 2500 g.
2. 2500 ÷ 750 = 3.33…, so 3 full batches.

A train leaves at 10:47 and arrives at 13:12. How long is the journey?

1. 10:47 to 11:00 is 13 minutes; 11:00 to 13:00 is 2 hours; 13:00 to 13:12 is 12 minutes.
2. Total: 2 hours 25 minutes.

### Common mistakes

- Treating time as decimal: 3.5 hours is not 3 hours 50 minutes.
- Dividing when you should multiply in a conversion. Ask: should the number get bigger or smaller?

## R1 Converting between related units

*Refs: AQA R1 · Edexcel R1 · OCR 10.01a · Eduqas FR1, HR1. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Change freely between related standard units of time, length, area, volume (capacity) and mass.
- Area and volume conversions use the square and the cube of the length factor.
- Converting compound units (for example km/h to m/s) is covered with compound units in [09-ratio-and-proportion.md](09-ratio-and-proportion.md).

### Explained

1 m = 100 cm, so a square 1 m by 1 m is also 100 cm by 100 cm. Its area is 1 m² = 10 000 cm².

| Length | Area | Volume |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm² = 100 mm² | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m² = 10 000 cm² | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² | |

Square the length factor for area and cube it for volume.

### Worked example

Change 3.2 m² into cm².

1. 1 m² = 100² = 10 000 cm².
2. 3.2 × 10 000 = 32 000 cm².

A tank holds 0.45 m³ of water. How many litres is this?

1. 0.45 m³ = 0.45 × 1 000 000 = 450 000 cm³.
2. 1 litre = 1000 cm³, so 450 000 ÷ 1000 = 450 litres.

### Common mistakes

- Using 100 for m² to cm² (it is 10 000) or 1000 for m³ to cm³ (it is 1 000 000).
- Forgetting that 1 litre is 1000 cm³.

## G14 Units of measure in geometry

*Refs: AQA G14 · Edexcel G14 · OCR 10.01a · Eduqas FG12, HG14. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Use standard units of measure and related ideas: length, area, volume and capacity, mass, time, money.
- Give the correct units with every answer: mm, cm, m, km for length; mm², cm², m² for area; mm³, cm³, m³ for volume; litres and millilitres for capacity.

### Explained

The unit tells you what kind of quantity it is. A length times a length is an area, so its unit is squared. A length times an area is a volume, so its unit is cubed. If you find yourself with cm³ for an area, you have made an error in the method.

Choose sensible units: the length of a pencil in cm, the distance between towns in km, the mass of a lorry in tonnes. Make sure all lengths are in the same units before using an area or volume formula.

### Worked example

A rectangular garden is 12 m long and 850 cm wide. Find its area in m².

1. Same units: 850 cm = 8.5 m.
2. Area = 12 × 8.5 = 102 m².

### Common mistakes

- Multiplying a length in metres by a length in centimetres.
- Leaving off units or giving cm instead of cm².

## N14 Estimation and checking

*Refs: AQA N14 · Edexcel N14 · OCR 4.01b · Eduqas FN14, HN14. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Estimate answers by rounding each number to 1 significant figure (or another sensible value) and calculating.
- Check calculations, including calculator answers, using approximation and estimation.
- Say whether an estimate is an overestimate or underestimate where you can.

### Explained

Round every number to 1 significant figure unless a nearby "nice" number makes the calculation easier (for example √48 ≈ √49 = 7). Then work it out mentally.

If you rounded the numbers you multiply up, the estimate is too big (an overestimate). If you rounded a number you divide by up, the estimate is too small. For mixed rounding, you often cannot tell.

### Worked example

Estimate the value of (48.7 × 0.312) / 0.0485.

1. Round to 1 s.f.: (50 × 0.3) / 0.05.
2. 50 × 0.3 = 15.
3. 15 ÷ 0.05 = 300.

A student says 6.1² × 39.8 = 148.9. Use estimation to show they are wrong.

1. 6² × 40 = 36 × 40 = 1440.
2. 148.9 is about ten times too small, so the answer is wrong.

### Common mistakes

- Working out the exact answer and then rounding it. That is not an estimate.
- Rounding 0.0485 to 0 and then dividing by zero. Round to 1 significant figure: 0.05.

## N15 Rounding and error intervals

*Refs: AQA N15 · Edexcel N15 · OCR 4.01a, 4.01c · Eduqas FN15, HN15. Tier: F & H.*

### What you need to know

- Round to a given number of decimal places (d.p.) or significant figures (s.f.), or to a sensible degree of accuracy.
- Use inequality notation to write the error interval for a number that has been rounded or truncated.
- Truncating means chopping off digits after a certain place without rounding.

### Explained

Decimal places count from the decimal point. Significant figures count from the first non-zero digit.

| Number | 2 d.p. | 2 s.f. |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.4567 | 3.46 | 3.5 |
| 0.006 049 | 0.01 | 0.0060 |
| 28 451 | 28 451.00 | 28 000 |

When rounding a large number to significant figures, keep the place value by filling with zeros: 28 451 to 2 s.f. is 28 000, not 28.

Error interval for rounding: if x has been rounded to the nearest 10 to give 70, x could be anything from 65 up to (but not including) 75. Half the rounding unit goes each side.

```latex
65 \le x < 75
```

The lower limit uses ≤ because 65 rounds up to 70. The upper limit uses < because 75 would round to 80.

Error interval for truncation: if x is truncated to 1 d.p. to give 4.3, the true value is from 4.3 up to (not including) 4.4. So 4.3 ≤ x < 4.4.

### Worked example

Round 0.040 87 to 3 significant figures.

1. First significant figure is the 4. Count three: 4, 0, 8.
2. The next digit is 7, so round the 8 up: 0.0409.

The length L of a rod is 8.4 m, correct to 1 decimal place. Write the error interval for L.

1. Rounding unit 0.1, half of it 0.05.
2. 8.35 ≤ L < 8.45.

A number y is truncated to 2 significant figures and gives 360. Write the error interval.

1. 360 ≤ y < 370.

### Common mistakes

- Counting leading zeros as significant figures.
- Using ≤ at both ends of an error interval.
- Treating a truncation question as a rounding question, or the other way round.
- Writing 28 for 28 451 to 2 s.f.

## N16 Limits of accuracy and bounds

*Refs: AQA N16 · Edexcel N16 · OCR 4.01c · Eduqas FN16, HN16. Tier: F & H (part Higher only).*

### What you need to know

- Apply and interpret limits of accuracy: the smallest and largest values a rounded measurement could have.
- (HT only) Work out upper and lower bounds of calculations using measurements that have been rounded.
- (HT only) Decide the appropriate degree of accuracy for a final answer by comparing its bounds.

### Explained

The lower bound (LB) and upper bound (UB) of a rounded value are the ends of its error interval. A mass of 60 kg to the nearest kg has LB 59.5 kg and UB 60.5 kg.

(HT only) To find the bounds of a calculation, choose bounds that push the answer as far as possible each way.

| Calculation | Upper bound of answer | Lower bound of answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a + b | UB a + UB b | LB a + LB b |
| a − b | UB a − LB b | LB a − UB b |
| a × b | UB a × UB b | LB a × LB b |
| a ÷ b | UB a ÷ LB b | LB a ÷ UB b |

For subtraction and division, the second number goes the opposite way.

(HT only) To give an answer to a suitable accuracy, round both bounds to the same degree. If they agree, that is your answer. If they do not, try a lower degree of accuracy.

### Worked example

A bag of sugar weighs 1 kg to the nearest 10 g. Write its lower and upper bounds in grams.

1. 1 kg = 1000 g; half of 10 g is 5 g.
2. LB 995 g, UB 1005 g.

(HT only) A car travels 145 km (to the nearest 5 km) in 1.8 hours (to the nearest 0.1 hour). Find the upper bound of its average speed.

1. Distance bounds: 142.5 ≤ d < 147.5. Time bounds: 1.75 ≤ t < 1.85.
2. Upper bound of speed = UB distance ÷ LB time = 147.5 ÷ 1.75 = 84.29 km/h (2 d.p.).

(HT only) A rectangle is 6.2 cm by 3.8 cm, each to 1 d.p. Find the area to a suitable degree of accuracy.

1. LB area = 6.15 × 3.75 = 23.0625. UB area = 6.25 × 3.85 = 24.0625.
2. To 2 s.f. the bounds are 23 and 24, which disagree. To 1 s.f. both are 20.
3. So the area is 20 cm² to 1 s.f. (both bounds round to 20).

### Common mistakes

- Using 6.24999… or 6.249 as an upper bound. Use 6.25; the inequality takes care of it.
- (HT only) Dividing upper bound by upper bound for the largest quotient.
- (HT only) Subtracting upper bound minus upper bound for the largest difference.
- (HT only) Not explaining why an accuracy is chosen. Say that both bounds round to the same value.
