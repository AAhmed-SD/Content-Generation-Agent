# 2.1 The rock cycle

The three rock groups (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic) are not fixed: over geological time, rocks are continually changed from one type to another. This topic covers the rock cycle as a system powered by energy, and the very different rates at which its processes operate.

## 2.1a The rock cycle and energy transfer

### What you need to know

- Sedimentary, metamorphic and igneous processes and rocks are linked by the rock cycle, powered by energy transfer, over geological time.
- Interpret rock cycle diagrams.

### Explained

Processes and the paths between rock types:

- Igneous rocks form by crystallisation of magma or lava.
- At the surface, any rock is weathered and eroded; sediment is transported and deposited, then buried, compacted and cemented (lithified) into sedimentary rock.
- Buried rocks heated and/or put under pressure undergo metamorphism to form metamorphic rocks.
- At very high temperatures rocks melt to form magma, which can rise and crystallise as new igneous rock.
- Uplift (during mountain building) brings buried igneous, metamorphic and sedimentary rocks back to the surface, where they are weathered and eroded again.

There are many shortcuts: an igneous rock can be metamorphosed without ever being weathered; a sedimentary rock can be uplifted and eroded straight into new sediment; a metamorphic rock can be weathered at the surface.

Energy sources:

- External: energy from the Sun drives the water cycle and the weather, which power weathering, erosion and transport (rain, rivers, wind, waves, ice). Gravity moves sediment downhill and makes it settle.
- Internal: heat from inside the Earth (from radioactive decay and heat left from the Earth's formation) drives mantle convection and plate movements, melting, metamorphism and uplift.

So the rock cycle links to plate tectonics (2.2): subduction carries rocks down to be metamorphosed and melted; collisions uplift mountains that are then eroded.

### Common mistakes

- Drawing the rock cycle as a single circle in one order. There are many possible pathways.
- Saying the Sun's energy causes melting.

## 2.1b Rates of rock cycle processes

### What you need to know

- Rock cycle processes take place at different rates, from seconds to millions of years (catastrophism versus gradualism, for example meteorite impact versus river erosion).
- Distinguish between processes in the rock record that happened at different rates.

### Explained

- Very fast (seconds to days): meteorite impacts, volcanic eruptions, pyroclastic flows, earthquakes and fault movements, landslides, turbidity currents, flash floods.
- Moderate (years to thousands of years): river deposition building floodplains and deltas, dune migration, cooling of lava flows, growth of reefs.
- Very slow (millions of years): deposition of thick sequences of fine mud in the deep sea (perhaps a few millimetres per thousand years), cooling of large plutons, mountain building and regional metamorphism, erosion of mountain ranges, plate movements (a few centimetres per year).

Gradualism (linked to Hutton and Lyell's uniformitarianism, 2.3) says the Earth has been shaped mainly by slow, steady processes acting over vast time. Catastrophism says sudden, violent events have shaped the Earth. Modern geology accepts both: most of the rock record built up slowly, but it is punctuated by rare catastrophic events (such as the K/Pg impact, 2.5 and 3.1).

Evidence in the rock record: a single graded bed or a turbidite was deposited in minutes or hours; a thick sequence of laminated black shale represents a very long time; an impact crater or a layer of ash forms almost instantly; coarse crystals in granite show slow cooling.

### Common mistakes

- Saying all geological processes are slow.

## Check questions

1. Describe how a granite could become a sandstone. [3 marks]
2. Describe how a shale could become a schist. [2 marks]
3. Explain the role of the Sun's energy and of the Earth's internal heat in the rock cycle. [4 marks]
4. Give one rock cycle process that takes seconds and one that takes millions of years. [2 marks]
5. Explain the difference between gradualism and catastrophism. [2 marks]

## Mark scheme

1. Granite uplifted and exposed (1); weathered and eroded into grains (quartz survives) which are transported and deposited (1); buried, compacted and cemented into sandstone (1).
2. Buried / caught in mountain building (1); high temperature and pressure cause regional metamorphism / recrystallisation (1).
3. Sun drives the weather / water cycle (1), powering weathering, erosion and transport (1). Internal heat drives convection / plate movement (1), causing melting, metamorphism and uplift (1).
4. Seconds: meteorite impact / earthquake / landslide / eruption (1). Millions of years: deep sea mud deposition / mountain building / pluton cooling / erosion of a mountain range (1).
5. Gradualism: Earth shaped by slow, continuous processes over long time (1); catastrophism: sudden, violent events shaped the Earth (1).
