# 3.5 The impact of modern technologies

Engineering changes how things are made and how people live. You need to know examples of new and emerging technologies and their effect on production, society and the environment, and the positive and negative effects of engineering industries on social and economic infrastructure. Every point needs examples.

## 3.5 New and emerging technologies

### What you need to know

- The impact of new and emerging technologies on production, on society and on the environment, with examples.

### Explained

| Technology | Impact on production | Impact on society | Impact on the environment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Automation and robotics (robot welding and painting lines, automated warehouses) | Faster, more consistent quality, works 24 hours, safer for dangerous tasks | Fewer low-skilled jobs; new skilled jobs in programming and maintenance; cheaper goods | Less material waste from errors; energy use of machinery |
| CAD/CAM and CNC | Accurate, repeatable parts; designs shared instantly worldwide; quick changes | Products customised to users; design jobs need digital skills | Optimised designs use less material; nesting reduces waste |
| Additive manufacturing (3D printing in polymers and metals) | One-offs and spare parts made on demand; complex lightweight parts; no tooling | Custom medical implants and prosthetics; home and small-business manufacturing | Less waste; lighter aircraft parts save fuel; but many printed plastics are hard to recycle |
| Smart materials and new composites (shape memory alloys, CFRP, graphene research) | New functions without extra parts | Lighter, stronger sports and medical products | Lighter vehicles save fuel; composites are hard to recycle |
| Electric and hybrid vehicles | Battery and motor manufacturing replaces engine plants | Cleaner city air, lower running cost, new charging infrastructure | No tailpipe emissions; battery mining and disposal have impacts |
| Renewable energy engineering (offshore wind, solar, tidal) | New industries and supply chains | Jobs in coastal and rural regions; energy security | Lower carbon emissions; habitat and visual impacts |
| Internet of Things and sensors | Machines report their condition, allowing predictive maintenance | Smart homes, health monitors; concerns about privacy and security | Energy saved by smart control of heating and lighting; more electronic waste |
| Artificial intelligence and machine vision | Automatic inspection, quality control, process optimisation | Changes skills needed; questions of responsibility when systems decide | Optimises energy use; data centres use energy |

Evaluating a technology means weighing both sides and considering who benefits and who loses: owners may benefit from lower costs while some workers lose jobs; users may benefit from cheaper goods while the environment bears more waste.

### Common mistakes

- Giving only benefits. Questions usually expect both positive and negative impacts.
- Being vague ("it is better"). Name a specific technology and a specific effect.

## 3.5 Engineering industries and infrastructure

### What you need to know

- The positive and negative impacts of engineering industries on social and economic infrastructure, with examples.

### Explained

Infrastructure is the basic systems a society runs on: transport (roads, railways, airports), energy (power stations, the grid), water and sewerage, communications (phone and broadband networks), and buildings such as hospitals and schools.

Positive impacts:

- Economic: engineering industries create jobs directly (factories) and indirectly (suppliers, local shops); they bring investment and tax income; exports earn money for the country. A new car plant or offshore wind port can regenerate a whole region.
- Social: better transport links connect people to work and services; reliable power, clean water and sanitation improve health; communications networks allow remote work, learning and healthcare; apprenticeships and training raise skill levels.

Negative impacts:

- Economic: when a large plant closes or moves overseas, whole towns can lose jobs and income; automation can reduce employment; large projects can run over budget.
- Social: construction disrupts communities (noise, traffic); new roads or rail lines may demolish homes or split communities; dependence on one industry makes an area vulnerable.
- Environmental: factories and infrastructure use land, energy and resources, and can pollute air, water and land; traffic increases emissions.

Example: a high-speed rail line shortens journey times, creates construction and operating jobs, and draws investment to the cities it serves, but it costs a very large sum, disrupts communities and habitats along the route during building, and may draw business away from towns it bypasses.

### Common mistakes

- Confusing infrastructure (shared systems such as transport and energy) with a single product.
- Forgetting that the same project can benefit one group and harm another.

## Check questions

1. Give one positive and one negative impact of using robots on a car production line. [2 marks]
2. Explain how additive manufacturing can benefit people who need medical implants. [2 marks]
3. Evaluate the impact on the environment of a switch from petrol cars to electric cars. [4 marks]
4. Describe how sensors connected to the internet can reduce maintenance costs in a factory. [2 marks]
5. A large engineering company opens a factory in a town with high unemployment. Discuss the impacts on the town's social and economic infrastructure. [6 marks]
6. State what is meant by infrastructure and give two examples. [3 marks]
