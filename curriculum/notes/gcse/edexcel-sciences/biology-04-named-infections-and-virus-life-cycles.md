# Named infections and virus life cycles (Edexcel gap file)

Pathogens in general, malaria and HIV are taught in [AQA 8461 4.3.1](../../aqa/gcse/biology-8461/4.3-infection-and-response.md). Edexcel names a different set of example diseases; this file covers the ones AQA does not.

### Board differences

- Edexcel's named diseases: cholera, tuberculosis, Chalara ash dieback, malaria, HIV, and (Biology only) stomach ulcers caused by *Helicobacter* and Ebola. AQA uses measles, TMV, salmonella, gonorrhoea and rose black spot instead.
- Edexcel's named STIs are chlamydia and HIV.
- The lytic and lysogenic pathways (5.7B) are Biology only.
- In 5.5 and 5.6, *Helicobacter* and Ebola are Biology only; the rest are also in Combined Science.

## 5.5 Some common infections

### What you need to know

| Disease | Pathogen type | Main effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cholera | bacterium (*Vibrio cholerae*) | severe watery diarrhoea, causing dehydration |
| Tuberculosis (TB) | bacterium (*Mycobacterium tuberculosis*) | damages lung tissue; coughing, sometimes coughing blood |
| Chalara ash dieback | fungus | ash trees lose leaves and get lesions (dead patches) on the bark |
| Malaria | protist (*Plasmodium*) | damages red blood cells and the liver; recurring fevers |
| HIV | virus | destroys a type of white blood cell (lymphocytes), leading to AIDS |
| Stomach ulcers (Biology only) | bacterium (*Helicobacter pylori*) | open sores in the stomach lining |
| Ebola (Biology only) | virus | haemorrhagic fever: fever with internal and external bleeding |

### Explained

The key skill is linking each disease to its pathogen type, because treatment depends on it. Antibiotics work on bacterial diseases (cholera, TB, stomach ulcers). They do nothing for viral diseases (HIV, Ebola), which are managed with antiviral drugs or supportive care. AIDS is not a separate pathogen: it is the late stage of HIV infection, when the immune system is so weak that other infections take hold.

### Common mistakes

- Calling Chalara a bacterium or a virus. It is a fungus.
- Saying antibiotics treat HIV or Ebola.
- Saying ulcers are caused only by stress or spicy food. Most are caused by *Helicobacter*.

## 5.6 How these pathogens spread and how spread is reduced

### What you need to know

| Disease | How it spreads | Ways to reduce or prevent spread |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cholera | water contaminated with infected faeces | clean, treated drinking water; proper sewage disposal; boil water |
| TB | airborne droplets from coughs and sneezes | isolate infected people; good ventilation; BCG vaccination; finish the full course of antibiotics |
| Chalara ash dieback | airborne fungal spores carried by wind; also moved on infected young trees | remove and burn infected trees; ban imports of ash; replant with resistant varieties |
| Malaria | animal vector: female *Anopheles* mosquitoes | mosquito nets; insect repellent; drain standing water; insecticides; antimalarial drugs |
| Stomach ulcers (Biology only) | oral transmission: contaminated food or water, or saliva | clean water; good hygiene; antibiotics to kill the bacteria |
| Ebola (Biology only) | contact with body fluids (blood, vomit) of an infected person or body | isolate patients; protective clothing for health workers; safe burial; contact tracing |

### Explained

Each control method breaks the chain of transmission at a specific point. For a waterborne disease you cut off the route (clean water). For a vector-borne disease you stop the vector reaching people or breeding. For an airborne disease you reduce contact with droplets or make people immune.

### Common mistakes

- Saying malaria is spread by all mosquitoes, or that the mosquito is the pathogen. The mosquito is the vector; *Plasmodium* is the pathogen.
- Giving "wash your hands" for every disease. Match the method to the route.

## 5.7B The life cycle of a virus: lytic and lysogenic pathways (Biology only)

### What you need to know

- Viruses can only reproduce inside a host cell.
- **Lytic pathway**:
  1. The virus attaches to the host cell and injects its genetic material.
  2. The host cell uses the viral genetic material to make new viral proteins and copies of the viral genetic material.
  3. New viruses are assembled.
  4. The cell bursts (lyses), releasing many new viruses that infect other cells.
- **Lysogenic pathway**:
  1. The viral genetic material is inserted into the host's DNA.
  2. It is copied each time the cell divides, so all daughter cells carry it. The virus is dormant and no new viruses are made.
  3. Later, a trigger (such as a chemical or UV light) makes the viral genetic material leave the host DNA and the virus enters the lytic pathway.

### Explained

The lytic pathway is fast and destructive: lots of new viruses and a dead cell. The lysogenic pathway is a hiding phase: the virus can stay in the cells for a long time with no symptoms. This is why some infections return; for example, the virus that causes cold sores stays dormant in nerve cells and becomes active when the person is run down.

### Common mistakes

- Saying viruses divide by themselves. They need the host cell's machinery.
- Saying the lysogenic pathway makes new viruses. It does not, until it switches to lytic.

## 5.8 Sexually transmitted infections: chlamydia and HIV

### What you need to know

- STIs are spread by sexual contact, through the exchange of body fluids.
- **Chlamydia** is caused by a bacterium. It often has no symptoms but can cause infertility. It can be treated with antibiotics.
- **HIV** is a virus spread by sexual contact and also by sharing needles, from mother to baby at birth or in breast milk, and through blood. It cannot be cured, but antiretroviral drugs slow it down.
- Spread of both can be reduced by: using condoms; screening and testing, then treating people and informing partners; for HIV, not sharing needles (needle exchange schemes) and screening blood for transfusions.

### Explained

Because chlamydia often has no symptoms, people can pass it on without knowing. Screening (especially of young adults) finds infections so they can be treated with antibiotics. Condoms are a physical barrier that stops body fluids passing between partners.

### Common mistakes

- Saying the contraceptive pill protects against STIs. Only barrier methods such as condoms reduce the risk.
- Saying chlamydia is a virus. It is a bacterium, so antibiotics work.

## Check questions

1. Name the type of pathogen that causes (a) cholera, (b) Chalara ash dieback, (c) malaria. (3 marks)
2. Explain why providing clean water reduces cases of cholera but does not reduce cases of TB. (3 marks)
3. Suggest two ways to stop the spread of ash dieback into a new forest. (2 marks)
4. Describe the lytic pathway of a virus. (4 marks)
5. Explain why a person infected by a virus in the lysogenic pathway may have no symptoms for a long time. (2 marks)
6. Explain why screening young adults for chlamydia helps reduce its spread. (2 marks)
7. Give two ways the spread of HIV among people who inject drugs can be reduced. (2 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. (a) Bacterium (1). (b) Fungus (1). (c) Protist (1).
2. Cholera is spread through contaminated water (1); clean water removes the route of transmission (1); TB is airborne/spread in droplets, so water is not its route (1).
3. Any two: remove and burn infected trees; ban import of ash saplings/plants from infected areas; plant resistant ash varieties; clean footwear/vehicles moving between sites (2).
4. Virus attaches to host cell and injects genetic material (1); host cell makes viral proteins and copies of viral genetic material (1); new viruses assembled (1); cell bursts/lyses releasing viruses (1).
5. Viral genetic material is inserted into host DNA and is dormant (1); no new viruses are made / cells are not destroyed until it enters the lytic pathway (1).
6. Chlamydia often has no symptoms so people do not know they have it (1); found cases can be treated with antibiotics so they cannot pass it on (1).
7. Any two: needle exchange/clean needles; do not share needles; condoms; testing and antiretroviral treatment (2).
