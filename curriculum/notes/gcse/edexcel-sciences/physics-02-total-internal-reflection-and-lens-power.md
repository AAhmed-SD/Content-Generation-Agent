# Total internal reflection and lens power (Edexcel gap file)

Reflection, refraction and lenses are in [AQA 8463 4.6](../../aqa/gcse/physics-8463/4.6-waves.md) (4.6.1.3, 4.6.2.2 and 4.6.2.5). Edexcel adds total internal reflection and asks you to link a lens's power to its focal length and shape. Both statements are Physics only (not in Combined Science).

### Board differences

- AQA GCSE does not include total internal reflection or the critical angle. Edexcel expects ray diagrams showing it.

## 5.1P Reflection, refraction and total internal reflection (Physics only)

### What you need to know

- When light goes from a denser medium (such as glass or water) towards a less dense one (air), it speeds up and bends away from the normal.
- As the angle of incidence increases, the refracted ray bends closer to the boundary.
- At the **critical angle**, the refracted ray travels along the boundary (angle of refraction = 90°).
- If the angle of incidence is **greater than** the critical angle, no light leaves: all of it is reflected back inside. This is **total internal reflection** (TIR).
- TIR only happens when light travels from a denser to a less dense medium. For glass to air, the critical angle is about 42°; for water to air, about 49°.

### Explained

Three cases for light inside a glass block hitting the glass–air surface:

| Angle of incidence | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| less than critical angle | most light refracts out, bending away from the normal; a little is reflected |
| equal to critical angle | refracted ray runs along the surface |
| greater than critical angle | all light reflected inside; angle of reflection = angle of incidence |

Uses:

- **Optical fibres**: light enters a thin glass fibre and keeps hitting the sides at angles greater than the critical angle, so it is totally internally reflected all the way along, even round gentle bends. Used in communications and in endoscopes to see inside the body.
- **Prisms** in periscopes and binoculars: a 45° prism reflects light through 90° with almost no loss of brightness.

### Worked example

Light in a glass block (critical angle 42°) hits the surface at 30°, 42° and 55°. Describe each.

- 30°: less than 42°, so the light mostly refracts out into the air, bending away from the normal.
- 42°: at the critical angle, so the ray travels along the surface.
- 55°: greater than 42°, so total internal reflection; the ray reflects back into the glass at 55° to the normal.

### Common mistakes

- Saying TIR happens when light goes from air into glass. It needs light leaving the denser medium.
- Measuring angles from the surface instead of from the normal.
- Drawing the reflected ray at a different angle from the incident ray.

## 5.4P Power of a lens

### What you need to know

- The **focal length** is the distance from the centre of the lens to the principal focus.
- A more powerful lens bends light more, so it has a **shorter** focal length.
- For a lens made of a given material, a **more curved** (fatter) lens is more powerful.
- Lens power is measured in dioptres (D): power = 1 ÷ focal length (in metres). Converging (convex) lenses have positive power; diverging (concave) lenses have negative power.

### Explained

Two convex lenses of the same glass: the one with more strongly curved surfaces brings parallel rays to a focus closer to the lens. Opticians describe glasses by their power: a +2 D lens is converging with focal length 0.5 m; a −4 D lens is diverging with focal length 0.25 m.

### Worked example

A converging lens has focal length 20 cm. Find its power.

- 20 cm = 0.20 m.
- Power = 1 ÷ 0.20 = +5 D.

### Common mistakes

- Using the focal length in cm in the formula. Convert to metres.
- Thinking a longer focal length means a stronger lens. It is the opposite.

## Check questions

1. Explain what is meant by the critical angle. (2 marks)
2. State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection. (2 marks)
3. Light travels inside a water tank and hits the water–air surface at 60°. The critical angle for water is 49°. Describe what happens to the light. (2 marks)
4. Explain how light travels along a curved optical fibre. (3 marks)
5. Lens A has focal length 10 cm and lens B has focal length 25 cm. Both are made of the same glass. Which is more powerful, and which is more curved? Calculate the power of lens A. (3 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. The angle of incidence (1) at which the angle of refraction is 90°/the refracted ray travels along the boundary (1).
2. Light travels from a denser to a less dense medium (1); angle of incidence greater than the critical angle (1).
3. 60° is greater than the critical angle (1); so the light is totally internally reflected back into the water at 60° (1).
4. Light hits the inside surface of the fibre (1) at an angle greater than the critical angle (1); so is totally internally reflected repeatedly along the fibre (1).
5. Lens A is more powerful and more curved (1); power = 1 ÷ 0.10 (1) = 10 D (1).
