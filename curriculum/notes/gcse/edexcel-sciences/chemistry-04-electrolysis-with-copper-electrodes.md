# Electrolysis with copper electrodes (Edexcel gap file)

Electrolysis with inert electrodes, including copper sulfate solution, is in [AQA 8462 4.4.3](../../aqa/gcse/chemistry-8462/4.4-chemical-changes.md). Edexcel also asks what happens when the electrodes are copper, and uses this to purify copper.

### Board differences

- AQA's required practical uses inert electrodes only. Edexcel core practical 3.31 compares inert electrodes with copper electrodes and measures the change in mass of each electrode.

## 3.30 Electrolysis of copper sulfate solution with copper electrodes

### What you need to know

- With **copper** electrodes, the anode itself takes part:
  - At the anode (positive): copper atoms lose electrons and go into solution as copper ions. Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻. The anode loses mass.
  - At the cathode (negative): copper ions gain electrons and are deposited as copper. Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu. The cathode gains mass.
- The concentration of copper ions in the solution stays the same, so the blue colour does not fade.
- This is used to **purify copper**: the impure copper is the anode and a thin strip of pure copper is the cathode. Impurities fall below the anode as "anode sludge".

### Explained

Compare the two set-ups:

| | Inert (graphite or platinum) electrodes | Copper electrodes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cathode | copper deposited; gains mass | copper deposited; gains mass |
| Anode | oxygen gas given off (from hydroxide ions or water); no mass change | copper dissolves; loses mass |
| Solution | blue colour fades as Cu²⁺ is used up; becomes acidic | blue colour stays the same |

With copper electrodes, the mass lost by the anode should equal the mass gained by the cathode (for pure copper), because the same number of electrons flows round the circuit.

### Common mistakes

- Saying oxygen is made at a copper anode. The copper is oxidised instead.
- Saying the solution fades with copper electrodes. Copper ions are added at the anode as fast as they are removed at the cathode.

## 3.31 Core practical: electrolysis of copper sulfate solution with inert and copper electrodes

### What you need to know

- The practical measures how the mass of each copper electrode changes with current (or time), and observes the products with inert electrodes.
- The mass change is directly proportional to the current (for a fixed time) and to the time (for a fixed current).

### Explained

Method with copper electrodes:

1. Clean two copper plates with emery paper, wash with propanone, dry, and weigh each.
2. Put them in copper sulfate solution in a beaker, not touching, and connect them to a d.c. supply with an ammeter and variable resistor in series.
3. Set the current (e.g. 0.2 A) and run for a fixed time (e.g. 20 minutes).
4. Remove the plates, rinse with distilled water then propanone, dry, and reweigh.
5. Repeat with fresh plates at different currents.

With graphite electrodes, look for bubbles of gas at the anode (oxygen relights a glowing splint) and a pink-brown copper coating on the cathode.

### Worked example

| Current (A) | Cathode mass change (g) | Anode mass change (g) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.2 | +0.08 | −0.08 |
| 0.4 | +0.16 | −0.17 |

Doubling the current roughly doubles the mass of copper deposited, because twice as many electrons pass per second, so twice as many copper ions are reduced.

### Common mistakes

- Weighing electrodes wet, which adds mass.
- Rubbing the cathode dry and knocking off loose copper.

### Practical

- Hazards: copper sulfate is harmful and an irritant; propanone is highly flammable (no flames nearby). Use low voltage; wear eye protection.

## Check questions

1. Write the half equations for the reactions at the anode and the cathode when copper sulfate solution is electrolysed with copper electrodes. (2 marks)
2. Explain why the blue colour of the solution fades with graphite electrodes but not with copper electrodes. (3 marks)
3. Explain how electrolysis is used to purify copper. (3 marks)
4. In the core practical, the cathode gains 0.12 g in 30 minutes at 0.3 A. Predict its mass gain in 30 minutes at 0.6 A and explain. (2 marks)
5. Give two steps that make sure the mass change measurement is accurate. (2 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. Anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (1). Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (1).
2. With graphite, Cu²⁺ ions are removed at the cathode and not replaced, so the concentration falls (1); with copper, the anode dissolves forming Cu²⁺ ions (1); at the same rate they are removed at the cathode, so concentration stays constant (1).
3. Impure copper is the anode and pure copper the cathode (1); copper atoms at the anode form ions that move to and are deposited on the cathode (1); impurities fall to the bottom as sludge (1).
4. 0.24 g (1); mass deposited is proportional to current/twice as many electrons flow (1).
5. Any two: clean electrodes before weighing; rinse and dry electrodes before reweighing; use a balance to 0.01 g; keep time constant; do not knock off deposited copper (2).
