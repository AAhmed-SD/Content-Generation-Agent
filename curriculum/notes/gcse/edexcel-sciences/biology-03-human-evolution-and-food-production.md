# Human evolution and food production (Edexcel gap file)

## 4.4 Fossil evidence for human evolution

### What you need to know

- Fossils show that humans evolved from earlier ape-like ancestors over millions of years.
- Three finds to know, from oldest to youngest:
  - **Ardi** (*Ardipithecus ramidus*), about 4.4 million years old.
  - **Lucy** (*Australopithecus afarensis*), about 3.2 million years old.
  - Fossils found by **Richard Leakey's** team, about 1.6 million years old (the "Turkana Boy", *Homo erectus*).
- Comparing them shows trends: walking more upright, longer legs, shorter arms, and a bigger brain.

### Explained

| Feature | Ardi (4.4 mya) | Lucy (3.2 mya) | Leakey's find (1.6 mya) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Brain size | about 350 cm³ (similar to a chimpanzee) | about 450 cm³ | about 850 cm³ |
| Walking | could walk upright, but foot had a grasping big toe for climbing | walked upright; foot more like ours, arched | walked upright, like a modern human |
| Limbs | long arms, short legs | arms and legs between ape and human proportions | short arms, long legs |
| Height | about 1.2 m | about 1.1 m | about 1.6 m |

(Modern humans have a brain of about 1 300 to 1 500 cm³.)

The pattern across these three supports evolution: features change gradually in one direction over time. Brain size roughly doubled between Ardi and the Leakey fossils, and the body moved from part climbing to fully walking on two legs.

### Common mistakes

- Saying humans evolved from chimpanzees. Humans and chimps share a common ancestor.
- Getting the order wrong. Ardi is oldest, then Lucy, then Leakey's find.
- Saying fossils "prove" each one is the direct ancestor of the next. They show the trend, but some may be side branches.

## 4.5 Stone tool evidence for human evolution

### What you need to know

- Stone tools became more complex over time, which suggests that brain size and skill increased.
- Tools can be dated by the layer of rock or sediment they are found in (stratigraphy): deeper layers are usually older.
- Volcanic ash layers above and below a tool can be dated using radioactive isotopes (such as the decay of potassium to argon), and carbon dating can date plant or animal remains found with the tools.

### Explained

A rough timeline of tool types:

| Approximate age | Tools | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2.6 million years | simple pebble tools: a stone with a few flakes knocked off to make a sharp edge (Oldowan) | able to plan simple cutting |
| 1.7 million years | hand axes shaped on both sides into a teardrop (Acheulean) | more planning and skill |
| 300 000 years | smaller flakes shaped from a prepared core, used as points and scrapers | fine control; tools for hunting |
| 40 000 years onward | thin blades, needles, fish hooks, often of bone or antler | complex tool kits, clothing |

Dating from the environment: sediment builds up in layers, so a tool found in a deeper layer was usually left earlier. If a layer of volcanic ash lies above the tool, the tool must be older than that ash.

### Common mistakes

- Saying carbon dating dates the stone. Stone does not contain carbon from a living thing; carbon dating is used on organic remains found with the tools, and only for the last 50 000 years or so.
- Assuming deeper always means older without considering disturbance (such as earthquakes).

## 4.6B The pentadactyl limb (Biology only)

### What you need to know

- A pentadactyl limb is a limb with five digits (fingers or toes).
- Many vertebrates (mammals, birds, reptiles and amphibians) have limbs with the same basic bone plan: one upper bone, two lower bones, wrist or ankle bones, and five digits.
- The limbs are used for very different jobs (grasping, flying, swimming, running), but the same plan suggests these animals evolved from a common ancestor.

### Explained

A human arm, a bat wing, a whale flipper and a horse leg look different, but all contain the humerus, radius and ulna, carpals and digits. If each animal had been designed separately for its job, there is no reason for them to share a plan. The simplest explanation is that they inherited it from a shared ancestor, and natural selection then changed the bones' sizes and shapes to suit each animal's way of life.

### Common mistakes

- Saying the limbs have the same function. They have the same structure but different functions.
- Saying all five digits are always visible. In horses, some digits are reduced, but the basic plan is the same.

## 4.13B Agricultural solutions: fertilisers and biological control (Biology only)

### What you need to know

- A growing human population needs more food. Farmers use fertilisers to increase crop yield and biological control to reduce pests.
- **Fertilisers** add mineral ions (nitrates, phosphates and potassium) to the soil.
- **Biological control** uses a living organism (a predator, parasite or pathogen) to reduce a pest population, instead of chemical pesticides.

### Explained

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fertilisers | Bigger yields from the same land; replace minerals taken out by harvesting | Run-off into rivers causes eutrophication; cost; can harm soil organisms if overused |
| Biological control | No chemical residues on food; can be long-lasting as the control organism breeds; targets the pest | Slow to act; the control organism may become a pest itself or eat native species; can be hard to predict |

Examples of biological control: ladybirds released in greenhouses to eat aphids; a parasitic wasp that lays eggs in whitefly larvae. A famous failure is the cane toad, released in Australia to eat beetles on sugar cane, which instead spread widely and poisoned native predators.

### Common mistakes

- Saying biological control "kills all the pests". It reduces the population; predator and prey numbers then usually settle at a lower level.
- Forgetting that fertiliser run-off, not the fertiliser on the crop, causes eutrophication.

## Check questions

1. Put these in order from oldest to youngest: Lucy, Leakey's fossils, Ardi. (1 mark)
2. Describe two changes shown by the fossils of Ardi, Lucy and Leakey's find that support the idea of human evolution. (2 marks)
3. Explain how scientists could estimate the age of a stone tool found in a cliff with layers of rock and volcanic ash. (3 marks)
4. Explain why stone tools from 40 000 years ago are evidence that brain size had increased compared with 2.6 million years ago. (2 marks)
5. A bat's wing and a human arm have the same bones. Explain how this is evidence for evolution. (2 marks)
6. A farmer has aphids on a greenhouse crop. Evaluate using ladybirds instead of a chemical pesticide. (4 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. Ardi, Lucy, Leakey's fossils (1).
2. Any two: brain size increases; more upright walking/fully bipedal; legs get longer/arms shorter; less adapted for climbing/big toe no longer grasping (2).
3. Tools in deeper layers are older/layers build up over time (1); find which layer the tool is in (1); date volcanic ash layers above/below by radiometric dating to give a range of ages (1).
4. Later tools are more complex/finer/more varied (1); making them needs more planning, skill or intelligence, linked to a bigger brain (1).
5. Same structure/pentadactyl plan used for different functions (1); suggests they share a common ancestor from which both evolved (1).
6. For ladybirds: no chemical residues on the crop; ladybirds keep breeding so control lasts; only target insects they eat. Against: slower; may not remove all aphids; ladybirds may leave. Judgement (4 marks: 1 for each valid point up to 3, 1 for a justified conclusion).
