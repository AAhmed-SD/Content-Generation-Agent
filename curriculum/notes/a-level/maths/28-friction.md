# Friction

This file covers the friction model F ≤ μR, the coefficient of friction, limiting friction, and motion and equilibrium on rough horizontal and inclined surfaces, including connected particles.

## Overview

A-level only, except that OCR H240 puts the basic concept of a frictional force (3.03r) in its AS (Stage 1) content. It builds on Newton's laws and resolving forces (file 27).

### Board differences

- AQA R6; Edexcel Mechanics 8.6; OCR H240 3.03r (AS) and 3.03s to 3.03v (A-level only); OCR MEI H640 MF10 to MF12.
- OCR's 3.03s and MEI's MF10 ask you to see the overall contact force between two rough surfaces as having two components, friction and normal reaction.

## R6 Friction

*Refs: AQA R6 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.6 · OCR 3.03r, 3.03s, 3.03t, 3.03u, 3.03v · MEI MF10, MF11, MF12. Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- Friction acts along the surface, opposing motion (or the tendency to move).
- (A-level only) The friction model:

```latex
F \le \mu R
```

where R is the normal reaction and μ is the coefficient of friction (μ ≥ 0; μ = 0 for a smooth surface).

- (A-level only) When the object is moving, or on the point of moving (limiting equilibrium), F = μR.
- (A-level only) When the object is at rest and not on the point of moving, F takes whatever value is needed for equilibrium, up to μR.
- (A-level only) The contact force from a rough surface is the resultant of F and R.
- (A-level only) Solve problems on rough horizontal surfaces and rough inclined planes, including connected particles.

### Explained

**Finding R first.** Friction depends on R, so always resolve perpendicular to the surface first to find R. R is not always mg: a force pulling upwards at an angle reduces R, and on a slope R = mg cos α plus any perpendicular components of other forces.

**Does it move?** Work out the force trying to move the object and the maximum friction μR. If the driving force is at most μR, the object stays at rest, and friction equals the driving force. If it is more, the object accelerates with F = μR opposing.

**Direction of friction.** Opposite to the direction of motion, or of the motion that would happen without friction. On a slope, a block that would slide down has friction acting up the slope; a block being pushed up has friction acting down the slope.

**Limiting equilibrium on a slope.** A block on a rough slope at angle α is about to slide when mg sin α = μ mg cos α, that is μ = tan α. If tan α ≤ μ it stays put on its own.

### Worked example

(A-level only) A 10 kg box on a rough horizontal floor (μ = 0.3) is pulled by a horizontal force of 50 N. Find its acceleration.

1. R = 10g = 98 N. Maximum friction = 0.3 × 98 = 29.4 N.
2. 50 > 29.4, so it moves. 50 − 29.4 = 10a, so a = 2.06 m s⁻².

(A-level only) An 8 kg sledge on rough horizontal ground (μ = 0.4) is pulled by a rope at 25° above the horizontal with tension 40 N. Find its acceleration.

1. Vertical: R + 40 sin 25° = 8g, so R = 78.4 − 16.90 = 61.50 N.
2. Friction = 0.4 × 61.50 = 24.60 N.
3. Horizontal: 40 cos 25° − 24.60 = 8a, so 36.25 − 24.60 = 8a, a = 1.46 m s⁻².

(A-level only) A 4 kg block slides down a rough slope at 30° (μ = 0.2). Find its acceleration.

1. R = 4g cos 30° = 33.95 N. Friction = 6.79 N up the slope.
2. 4g sin 30° − 6.79 = 4a, so 19.6 − 6.79 = 4a, a = 3.20 m s⁻².

(A-level only) A block rests on a rough slope that is gradually tilted. It starts to slip when the angle reaches 25°. Find μ.

1. In limiting equilibrium, μ = tan 25° = 0.466.

(A-level only) A 6 kg block is on a rough slope at 35° (μ = 0.3). Find the least force parallel to the slope that will stop it sliding down, and the least force that will push it up the slope.

1. Weight component down the slope: 6g sin 35° = 33.73 N. Maximum friction: 0.3 × 6g cos 35° = 14.45 N.
2. To stop it sliding down, friction acts up the slope: P = 33.73 − 14.45 = 19.3 N.
3. To push it up, friction acts down the slope: P = 33.73 + 14.45 = 48.2 N.

(A-level only) A 5 kg block on a rough horizontal table (μ = 0.25) is joined by a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley to a 3 kg mass hanging freely. Find the acceleration and the tension.

1. Friction on the block = 0.25 × 5g = 12.25 N.
2. Hanging mass: 3g − T = 3a. Block: T − 12.25 = 5a.
3. Add: 3g − 12.25 = 8a, so 29.4 − 12.25 = 8a, a = 2.14 m s⁻².
4. T = 3(g − a) = 3(9.8 − 2.144) = 23.0 N.

### Common mistakes

- (A-level only) Using F = μR when the object is at rest and not about to move.
- (A-level only) Using R = mg when another force has a vertical component, or when on a slope.
- (A-level only) Putting friction in the wrong direction.

## Check questions

1. (A-level only) A 20 kg crate on a rough horizontal floor (μ = 0.35) is pushed by a horizontal force of 80 N. Find its acceleration. [3 marks]
2. (A-level only) A 15 kg sled moves at constant speed across rough horizontal snow, pulled by a rope at 20° above the horizontal with tension 60 N. Find μ. [5 marks]
3. (A-level only) A particle is released from rest on a rough slope at 40° (μ = 0.5). Find its acceleration and the distance it travels in 2 s. [5 marks]
4. (A-level only) A 2 kg block is placed at rest on a rough plane at 20° to the horizontal (μ = 0.4). Show that it does not slide, and find the friction force. [4 marks]
5. (A-level only) A particle is projected up a rough slope at 15° (μ = 0.2) with speed 8 m s⁻¹. Find how far up the slope it travels, and decide whether it then slides back down. [6 marks]
6. (A-level only) A 4 kg block on a rough horizontal table (μ = 0.2) is joined by a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley to a 2 kg mass hanging freely. Find the acceleration and the tension. [5 marks]
7. (A-level only) Explain why a friction force can be less than μR. [1 mark]

## Mark scheme

1. R = 20g, Fmax = 0.35 × 196 = 68.6 N (1); 80 − 68.6 = 20a (1); a = 0.57 m s⁻² (1).
2. Constant speed so forces balance (1); horizontally F = 60 cos 20° = 56.4 N (1); vertically R = 15g − 60 sin 20° = 126.5 N (1); μ = 56.4/126.5 (1) = 0.446 (1).
3. R = mg cos 40°, F = 0.5mg cos 40° (1); mg sin 40° − 0.5mg cos 40° = ma (1); a = 2.55 m s⁻² (1); s = ½ × 2.55 × 4 (1) = 5.09 m (1).
4. Component down the slope 2g sin 20° = 6.70 N (1); Fmax = 0.4 × 2g cos 20° = 7.37 N (1); 6.70 < 7.37 so it stays at rest (1); friction = 6.70 N up the slope (1).
5. Deceleration g(sin 15° + 0.2 cos 15°) (2) = 4.43 m s⁻² (1); 0 = 64 − 2(4.43)s, s = 7.22 m (1); tan 15° = 0.268 > 0.2 (1); so it slides back down (1).
6. Friction 0.2 × 4g = 7.84 N (1); 2g − T = 2a and T − 7.84 = 4a (1); a = 1.96 m s⁻² (1); T = 2(9.8 − 1.96) (1) = 15.7 N (1).
7. μR is the maximum; when the object is at rest and not on the point of slipping, friction only needs to balance the other forces (1).
