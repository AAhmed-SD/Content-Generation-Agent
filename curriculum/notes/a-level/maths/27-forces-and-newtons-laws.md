# Forces and Newton's laws

This file covers what a force is, the common types of force, force diagrams, Newton's three laws, weight and g, equilibrium, connected particles and pulleys, resolving forces, and motion in a plane. Friction is in file 28 and moments in file 29.

## Overview

AS: forces and Newton's laws in a straight line, with forces in two perpendicular directions or given as simple 2D vectors; weight; equilibrium of a particle; connected particles and smooth pulleys. A-level only: resolving forces at an angle (including on inclined planes), equilibrium under several coplanar forces, and dynamics in a plane.

### Board differences

- AQA R1 to R5; Edexcel Mechanics 8.1 to 8.5.
- OCR H240: 3.03a to 3.03d, 3.03f to 3.03k, 3.03n (AS); 3.03e, 3.03l, 3.03m, 3.03o to 3.03q (A-level only).
- OCR MEI H640: MF1 to MF5 and Mn1 to Mn6 (AS); MF6 to MF9 and Mn7 (A-level only). MF8 mentions the closed polygon of forces for equilibrium.
- Edexcel's default value of g is 9.8 m s⁻².

## R1 Forces and Newton's first law

*Refs: AQA R1 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.1 · OCR 3.03a, 3.03b, 3.03i, 3.03j · MEI MF1, MF3, Mn1. Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- A force is a push or pull; it is a vector, measured in newtons.
- Types of force: weight, normal reaction (perpendicular to a surface), tension (in a string, pulling), thrust (in a rod, pushing), friction, resistance, driving force.
- Draw force diagrams showing every force on an object.
- Newton's first law: an object stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.

### Explained

A force diagram shows only the forces acting on the object you are studying, each as an arrow from the object with its name or size. Do not show forces the object exerts on other things, and do not draw "ma" as a force.

Constant velocity (including at rest) means the resultant force is zero: the forces balance.

### Worked example

A box rests on a horizontal floor while a horizontal force of 20 N pulls it but it does not move. Draw a force diagram and explain why it does not move.

1. Forces: weight W down, normal reaction R up, 20 N pull, friction F opposing the pull.
2. At rest, so the resultant is zero: F = 20 N and R = W.

### Common mistakes

- Including the force the object exerts on the floor in its own diagram.

## R2 Newton's second law

*Refs: AQA R2 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.2 · OCR 3.03c, 3.03d, 3.03e · MEI Mn2, Mn3, Mn7, MF6. Level: AS & A (part A-level only).*

### What you need to know

- Resultant force = mass × acceleration:

```latex
\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}
```

- Apply it in a straight line with forces in two perpendicular directions, or with forces given as 2D vectors.
- (A-level only) Resolve forces at an angle into perpendicular components, then apply F = ma in each direction.

### Explained

Write the equation of motion in the direction of the acceleration: (sum of forces in the direction of motion) − (sum of forces against it) = ma. Perpendicular to the motion, the forces balance.

**(A-level only) Resolving.** A force F at angle θ to a direction has component F cos θ along it and F sin θ perpendicular to it. On an inclined plane at angle α, the weight mg has components mg sin α down the slope and mg cos α into the slope.

**Vectors.** If forces are given as vectors, add them to get the resultant, then a = F/m.

### Worked example

Forces (3i + 4j) N and (−i + 2j) N act on a particle of mass 2 kg. Find its acceleration and the magnitude of the acceleration.

1. Resultant 2i + 6j N. a = (i + 3j) m s⁻².
2. |a| = √10 = 3.16 m s⁻².

(A-level only) A 5 kg box is pulled up a smooth slope inclined at 20° by a force P parallel to the slope. The box accelerates at 1 m s⁻². Find P and the normal reaction.

1. Parallel to the slope: P − 5g sin 20° = 5 × 1, so P = 5 + 16.76 = 21.8 N.
2. Perpendicular: R = 5g cos 20° = 46.0 N.

### Common mistakes

- Putting ma on the force diagram as if it were a force.
- (A-level only) Using cos for the component down a slope. Down the slope is mg sin α.

## R3 Weight and gravity

*Refs: AQA R3 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.3 · OCR 3.03f, 3.03g · MEI MF2. Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Weight W = mg, acting vertically downwards.
- g is about 9.8 m s⁻² near the Earth's surface. It is not a universal constant: it varies slightly with location, and may be given to different accuracies (9.8, 9.81, 10).
- Motion in a straight line under gravity (see file 25).

### Explained

Mass (kg) is the amount of matter and does not change with location. Weight (N) is the gravitational force and does depend on where you are.

### Worked example

Find the weight of a 70 kg person on Earth (g = 9.8) and on the Moon (g = 1.6).

1. Earth: 686 N. Moon: 112 N.

### Common mistakes

- Writing a mass in newtons or a weight in kilograms.

## R4 Newton's third law, equilibrium and connected particles

*Refs: AQA R4 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.4 · OCR 3.03h, 3.03k, 3.03l, 3.03m, 3.03n, 3.03o · MEI MF5, MF7, MF8, MF9, Mn4, Mn5, Mn6. Level: AS & A (part A-level only).*

### What you need to know

- Newton's third law: if A exerts a force on B, then B exerts an equal and opposite force on A.
- A particle is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero.
- Connected particles: two objects joined by a light inextensible string or rod, or one pushing another, or a lift with a person; objects joined over a smooth pulley.
- (A-level only) Resolve forces in two dimensions; a particle under several coplanar forces is in equilibrium if and only if the components in two perpendicular directions each sum to zero.

### Explained

**Connected particles.** Either treat the whole system as one object (internal forces such as tension cancel), or write an equation for each part. A good method: use the whole system to find the acceleration, then one part to find the tension.

Over a smooth pulley with a light inextensible string, the tension is the same on both sides, and both particles have the same size of acceleration. The force on the pulley is the vector sum of the two tensions (2T if the string hangs vertically on both sides).

**Lifts.** For a person of mass m in a lift with acceleration a upwards, the floor pushes up with R: R − mg = ma.

**(A-level only) Equilibrium with angles.** Resolve horizontally and vertically (or along and perpendicular to a slope) and set each sum to zero. Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle, so the sine rule can also be used on that triangle.

### Worked example

A lift of mass 600 kg carries a person of mass 70 kg and accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s⁻². Find the tension in the cable and the force between the person and the floor.

1. Whole system: T − 670g = 670 × 0.5, so T = 670 × 10.3 = 6901 N.
2. Person: R − 70g = 70 × 0.5, so R = 721 N.

A car of mass 1200 kg tows a trailer of mass 400 kg with a light rigid towbar. The driving force is 2500 N, and resistances are 300 N on the car and 200 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the tension in the towbar.

1. Whole system: 2500 − 300 − 200 = 1600a, so a = 1.25 m s⁻².
2. Trailer: T − 200 = 400 × 1.25, so T = 700 N.

Particles of mass 5 kg and 3 kg are joined by a light inextensible string over a smooth fixed pulley and released from rest. Find the acceleration, the tension, and the force on the pulley.

1. 5 kg: 5g − T = 5a. 3 kg: T − 3g = 3a.
2. Add: 2g = 8a, a = 2.45 m s⁻².
3. T = 3(g + a) = 36.75 N.
4. Force on pulley = 2T = 73.5 N.

A 4 kg block on a smooth horizontal table is joined by a string over a smooth pulley at the edge to a 1 kg mass hanging freely. Find the acceleration and the tension.

1. Hanging: g − T = a. Block: T = 4a.
2. g = 5a, a = 1.96 m s⁻². T = 7.84 N.

(A-level only) A 2 kg particle hangs in equilibrium from two strings at 30° and 60° to the horizontal. Find the tensions.

1. Horizontal: T₁ cos 30° = T₂ cos 60°, so T₂ = √3 T₁.
2. Vertical: T₁ sin 30° + T₂ sin 60° = 2g, so T₁/2 + (3/2)T₁ = 19.6.
3. T₁ = 9.8 N, T₂ = 17.0 N.

### Common mistakes

- Using different tensions on the two sides of a smooth pulley.
- Applying the whole system method to find tension (tension cancels in the whole system).
- (A-level only) Resolving a force in one direction and forgetting its component in the other.

## R5 Resultant forces and dynamics in a plane

*Refs: AQA R5 · Edexcel Mechanics 8.5 · OCR 3.03p, 3.03q · MEI MF4. Level: AS & A (part A-level only).*

### What you need to know

- Add forces to find the resultant, using components.
- (A-level only) The resultant of two or more forces at angles; dynamics of a particle moving in a plane under such forces.

### Explained

Resolve every force into i and j components (or horizontal and vertical), add each set, then find the magnitude √(X² + Y²) and direction tan⁻¹(Y/X) of the resultant. Then F = ma gives the acceleration as a vector, which can be used in the vector suvat formulae (file 25).

### Worked example

(A-level only) Forces of 10 N (along the positive x-axis) and 8 N (at 60° to it) act on a particle. Find the resultant.

1. X = 10 + 8 cos 60° = 14. Y = 8 sin 60° = 6.93.
2. Magnitude √(14² + 6.93²) = 15.6 N, at tan⁻¹(6.93/14) = 26.3° to the x-axis.

(A-level only) A particle of mass 4 kg is acted on by forces (2i − j) N, (pi + 5j) N and (−3i + qj) N, and accelerates at (0.5i + j) m s⁻². Find p and q.

1. Resultant: (p − 1)i + (q + 4)j = 4(0.5i + j) = 2i + 4j.
2. p = 3, q = 0.

### Common mistakes

- (A-level only) Adding magnitudes of forces that act in different directions.
