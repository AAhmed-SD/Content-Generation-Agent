# Practical skills, the Practical Endorsement and fieldwork

Practical skills are examined in all three OCR papers (especially Component 03, Practical skills in geology) and are assessed separately through the Practical Endorsement (pass or not classified). Learners must complete at least 12 assessed practicals covering PAG1 to PAG12 and at least four days of fieldwork, including virtual fieldwork, local fieldwork and fieldwork on unfamiliar outcrops. Eduqas students: see [Eduqas 06](../geology-eduqas-a480qs/06-specified-practicals-and-fieldwork.md).

## 1.1 Practical skills assessed in the written papers

### What you need to know

- Planning (1.1.1): experimental design to solve problems, choosing apparatus and techniques; identifying variables to control; evaluating whether a method fits the aim.
- Implementing (1.1.2): using apparatus correctly; appropriate units; presenting observations and data.
- Analysis (1.1.3): processing qualitative and quantitative results and drawing valid conclusions; maths skills; significant figures; plotting graphs with labelled axes and scales; gradients and intercepts.
- Evaluation (1.1.4): evaluating results and conclusions; anomalies; limitations; precision, accuracy, margins of error, percentage error, uncertainty in apparatus; suggesting improvements.

### Explained

**Planning**: state the independent variable (changed), dependent variable (measured) and control variables (kept constant). In fieldwork many variables cannot be controlled, so choose sites to reduce differences and record conditions.

**Data**: tables with units in headings; consistent significant figures; graphs with the independent variable on the x-axis, a sensible scale filling over half the grid, and a best-fit line. Gradient = Δy ÷ Δx from a large triangle.

**Accuracy and precision**: accurate results are close to the true value; precise results are close together (small spread). Repeat readings improve reliability and allow anomalies to be identified.

**Uncertainty**: half the smallest division for a single reading on a scale (or the stated tolerance); for a difference between two readings, add the uncertainties. Percentage uncertainty = uncertainty ÷ value × 100. For products and quotients, add percentage uncertainties.

### Worked example

Density of a rock: mass 52.4 ± 0.1 g, volume 20 ± 1 cm³. Percentage uncertainties: 0.19% and 5%. Density = 2.62 g cm⁻³ ± 5.2%, so ± 0.14 g cm⁻³. The volume measurement dominates: improve it with a finer measuring cylinder or by weighing in water.

## 1.2 The Practical Endorsement (A level only)

### What you need to know

- 1.2.1: apply investigative approaches; use equipment safely (identify hazards, minimise risk); follow written instructions; make and record observations; keep records; present data scientifically; use software to process data; research with online and offline sources; cite sources; use a wide range of instruments and techniques.
- 1.2.2 apparatus and techniques (asterisked ones must be shown on unfamiliar outcrops):
  - (a)* locate features in the field with traditional navigation without GPS
  - (b)* identify structures in the field and record them in field sketches
  - (c)* use a compass-clinometer to measure dip and strike of planar surfaces and apparent dip of fold limbs
  - (d)* construct graphic logs with appropriate scale and symbols
  - (e)* use sampling techniques in fieldwork
  - (f) use classification systems to identify unknown minerals and fossils
  - (g) produce annotated scientific drawings of fossils or small features from hand samples with a microscope or hand lens
  - (h)* produce full rock descriptions of macro and micro features
  - (i) use photomicrographs to identify minerals and textures
  - (j) use apparatus to measure mass, time, volume, temperature and length
  - (k) physical and chemical tests on minerals, including density and Mohs hardness
  - (l)* increase accuracy, for example timing multiple observations or using a fiducial scale in photographs and sketches
  - (m) use ICT and GIS to compile, process and model geological data.
- The Common Practical Assessment Criteria (CPAC), shared across OCR sciences: follows written procedures; applies investigative approaches when using instruments; uses equipment safely; makes and records observations; researches, references and reports.

## 1.3 Fieldwork skills (AS and A level)

### What you need to know

- (a) Measure and describe diagnostic properties of rocks in the field: colour, composition, grain or crystal size and shape, sorting, igneous texture, metamorphic fabric.
- (b) Collect valid data on the processes that formed the rocks: random, stratified and systematic sampling; sampling validity.
- (c) Measure and describe deformation in the field, with valid sampling.
- (d) Use geochronological principles in the field to place events in relative time order.

### Explained

**Field description**: describe from a fresh surface; use a hand lens and grain-size card (comparator); measure crystal and grain sizes against a scale; test with acid where appropriate; note structures and fossils; take photographs with a scale (coin, hammer, scale bar).

**Sampling**:

- Random: every location has an equal chance of selection (random numbers on a grid); avoids bias.
- Systematic: measurements at regular intervals (every metre along a traverse or log).
- Stratified: divide the outcrop into units (beds, zones) and sample each in proportion.
- Validity: enough measurements (for example many dip readings to average, since bedding surfaces are uneven); sample representative parts of the outcrop; record locations by grid reference.

**Deformation**: measure many dip and strike readings on each fold limb; record fault displacements, joint orientations, cleavage; plot data (stereonets, rose diagrams).

**Relative dating in the field**: apply superposition, way-up criteria, cross-cutting relationships, included fragments and unconformities ([06](06-geological-time-and-dating.md)).

**Navigation without GPS**: use a topographic map, compass bearings, pacing, and six-figure grid references.

**Field sketches**: title, location (grid reference), orientation (direction of view), scale, labels and interpretation; show the geology, not every detail of vegetation.

**Safety and conduct**: risk assessment (cliffs, tides, quarries, weather); hard hats near faces; goggles for hammering; minimal hammering and sample collection (follow the geological fieldwork code; respect SSSIs and landowners).

## Maths skills (summary)

Units and prefixes; standard form; ratios, fractions, percentages; significant figures; mean, median, mode, standard deviation; frequency tables, histograms, cumulative frequency; scatter graphs; probability; logarithms (phi scale, magnitude); rearranging equations; y = mx + c, gradients and intercepts; rates of change; areas and volumes; trigonometry for dip and thickness; reading circular data (rose diagrams).

### Common mistakes

- Taking one dip reading and treating it as representative.
- Field sketches with no scale or orientation.
- Confusing precision with accuracy.

## Check questions

1. Distinguish between accuracy and precision. (2)
2. A student measured the dip of one bed at five points: 22°, 25°, 21°, 34°, 23°. Identify the anomaly, suggest a cause and calculate the mean of the remaining values. (3)
3. Explain why stratified sampling may be better than random sampling on a cliff of alternating sandstone and shale. (2)
4. A grain is 0.8 ± 0.05 mm and a second 1.6 ± 0.05 mm. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in each and comment. (3)
5. List four things that must appear on a field sketch. (4)
6. Describe how to measure the strike and dip of a bedding plane with a compass-clinometer. (4)
7. Plan a field investigation to test whether grain size changes upwards through a sandstone sequence. (6)

## Mark scheme

1. Accuracy: closeness to the true value (1); precision: closeness of repeated readings to each other (1).
2. 34° anomalous (1); uneven surface / misread / measured a cross-bed not bedding (1); mean = (22 + 25 + 21 + 23) ÷ 4 = 22.75 ≈ 23° (1).
3. Ensures both rock types are sampled in proportion (1); random may miss thin beds / one lithology (1).
4. 6.25% (1); 3.1% (1); smaller measurements have larger percentage uncertainty / use magnification or a finer scale (1).
5. Any four: title; grid reference / location; direction of view; scale; labels; interpretation; date.
6. Place compass edge horizontally on the bedding plane / level with spirit level and read strike bearing (1); rotate 90° to point down the steepest slope (1); read dip angle with the clinometer (1); record dip direction; repeat and average (1).
7. Level-marked: systematic sampling at measured intervals up the log (1); use grain-size comparator / collect samples for sieving (1); many grains / repeat measurements at each level (1); control: same bed type / avoid weathered surfaces (1); plot grain size against height; Spearman's rank (1); risk assessment and conduct (1).
