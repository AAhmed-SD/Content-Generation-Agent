# Oil and gas basins

An oil or gas field needs a petroleum system: a source rock, burial to the right temperature, a migration route, a porous and permeable reservoir, a seal (cap rock) and a trap, in the right order of time. The North Sea Basin is the standard British example. Porosity, permeability and Darcy's law are in [14](14-fluids-in-rocks-and-hydrogeology.md); coal for Eduqas in [Eduqas 01](../geology-eduqas-a480qs/01-economic-minerals-coal-and-bulk-materials.md).

## Source rocks and maturation (OCR 7.2.2(a); Eduqas G4.1d)

### What you need to know

- Settings and conditions that led to oil and gas in the North Sea Basin; burial history curves.
- Palaeoenvironments of source, reservoir and cap rocks.
- Maturation in the source rock: temperature and pressure with burial; oil and gas windows.

### Explained

**Source rocks** are organic-rich mudstones. Plankton (algae) settle in quiet water where the bottom is anoxic, so the organic matter is not destroyed and is buried. In the North Sea the main oil source is the Upper Jurassic Kimmeridge Clay, deposited in deep, poorly oxygenated rift basins. Gas comes mainly from Carboniferous coals (southern North Sea), formed from land plants.

**Maturation**: with burial, temperature rises (about 25 to 30 °C km⁻¹).

1. Organic matter becomes kerogen (insoluble organic material) during shallow burial.
2. **Oil window**: about 60 to 120 °C (roughly 2 to 4 km): kerogen breaks down (cracks) into liquid oil.
3. **Gas window**: about 120 to 225 °C: oil and kerogen crack to gas (methane). Above that, organic matter is overcooked (graphite).
4. Algal (marine) kerogen produces mainly oil; woody (land plant) kerogen and coal produce mainly gas.

**Burial history curves** plot depth of a layer against time. Combined with the geothermal gradient, they show when the source rock entered the oil window (for the Kimmeridge Clay, mainly in the Cretaceous and Cenozoic). Traps must exist before oil is generated, or the oil escapes.

### Worked example

A source rock lies at 3200 m. Surface temperature 10 °C, gradient 30 °C km⁻¹. Temperature = 10 + 3.2 × 30 = 106 °C, so it is in the oil window. At 4500 m it would be 145 °C, in the gas window.

**Palaeoenvironments (North Sea)**: source rock (Kimmeridge Clay): deep anoxic marine rifts; reservoirs: Jurassic deltaic and shallow marine sandstones (the Brent Group), Permian desert sandstones (Rotliegend, southern gas fields), Palaeogene turbidite sandstones, Cretaceous chalk (fractured); cap rocks: marine mudstones (Kimmeridge Clay itself, Cretaceous clays) and Permian Zechstein evaporites (salt).

## Migration and traps (OCR 7.2.2(b); Eduqas G4.2c)

### What you need to know

- Migration from source to reservoir under a cap rock; porosity and permeability of reservoirs; suitable cap rocks; controls on migration (buoyancy).
- Rifting and synsedimentary faulting in the North Sea Basin forming traps.
- Traps: anticline, fault, salt dome, unconformity and lithological (stratigraphic) traps; how oil and gas may be lost from traps.

### Explained

**Migration**: as the source rock matures, oil and gas are squeezed out (primary migration) into permeable carrier beds and faults. Oil and gas are less dense than water, so they rise buoyantly through water-filled pores (secondary migration) until stopped by an impermeable layer. Gas rises above oil, and oil above water, in the trap.

**Reservoir rock**: porous (to store) and permeable (to flow): well sorted, poorly cemented sandstones; fractured limestones and chalk. **Cap rock (seal)**: impermeable: mudstone, shale, evaporites (salt is ideal because it flows and seals fractures).

**Traps**:

| Trap | How it forms |
| --- | --- |
| Anticline | Fold arch; hydrocarbons rise to the crest under the cap rock |
| Fault trap | A fault places impermeable rock against the reservoir, sealing it |
| Salt dome | Salt rises buoyantly (diapir), arching and faulting overlying beds and sealing them at its flanks |
| Unconformity trap | Tilted reservoir truncated by an erosion surface and sealed by impermeable rock above |
| Lithological (stratigraphic) trap | Reservoir pinches out or changes laterally into impermeable rock (for example a channel sandstone within mudstone, or a reef) |

**North Sea rifting**: in the Jurassic the North Sea area was stretched, forming rift basins (the Viking and Central Grabens) bounded by normal faults. Faulting during deposition (synsedimentary) tilted fault blocks, so Brent sandstones lie in the raised crests of rotated blocks sealed by the Kimmeridge Clay and later Cretaceous mudstones (for example the Brent field). The deep grabens buried the source rock deep enough to mature.

**Loss of oil and gas**: faults or erosion breaching the trap; cap rock fractured; uplift bringing the trap near the surface where oil is degraded by bacteria and water (biodegradation); overheating cracking oil to gas.

## Exploration (OCR 7.2.2(c); Eduqas G4.3a, G4.3b)

### What you need to know

- Geophysics: seismic reflection and gravity surveys to interpret structure and locate reserves.
- Exploration drilling and downhole logging: porosity, gamma ray and resistivity logs; primary and secondary recovery; planning field development.
- Microfossils in biostratigraphy and palaeoenvironmental analysis: foraminifera, coccolithophores, palynomorphs (pollen, spores, dinoflagellates).
- Eduqas: seismic reflection sections; cross-sections from borehole data with dating and correlation by microfossils.

### Explained

**Seismic reflection**: sound pulses (from air guns at sea or vibrator trucks on land) travel into the ground and reflect from boundaries between layers of different acoustic impedance (density × velocity). Recording travel times builds a cross-section image of the layers. Geologists identify anticlines, faults, salt domes and unconformities. Bright spots (strong reflections) can indicate gas. 3D seismic surveys image whole fields.

**Gravity surveys**: negative anomalies over thick low-density sediments (basins) and salt domes; positive over basement highs.

**Drilling and logs**: an exploration well tests a prospect. Downhole tools measure properties along the hole:

| Log | Measures | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gamma ray | Natural radioactivity (mainly K, Th, U in clays) | High in shales, low in clean sandstones and limestones: identifies lithology and correlates wells |
| Resistivity | Electrical resistance | Salty water conducts (low resistivity); oil and gas do not (high resistivity) in a porous bed |
| Porosity (neutron, density, sonic) | Pore space | Identifies good reservoir; neutron-density crossover flags gas |

Cores and cuttings are examined for lithology and fossils.

**Recovery**: primary recovery uses the natural pressure in the reservoir; secondary recovery injects water or gas to maintain pressure and sweep oil to producing wells; enhanced methods follow (see [Environmental Science 13](../environmental-science-aqa-7447/13-securing-future-energy-supplies.md)). Field development planning uses all data to place production and injection wells.

**Microfossils**: in drill cuttings (small chips), large fossils are destroyed but microfossils survive in large numbers. Foraminifera (marine single-celled organisms with chambered shells), coccolithophores (calcareous plankton plates), and palynomorphs (organic-walled: pollen and spores from land plants, and dinoflagellate cysts) evolve quickly and are widespread, so they date and correlate rocks between wells (biostratigraphy). They also indicate environment (marine or terrestrial, water depth, salinity), and the colour of palynomorphs shows thermal maturity.

### Practical

**OCR PAG5 and PAG10**: interpret graphic logs and well logs; correlate boreholes using gamma ray signatures and microfossil zones; locate traps on seismic sections. **OCR PAG8**: model migration with oil and water in sand columns.

## Onshore basins (OCR 7.2.2(d))

### What you need to know

How the same principles apply to onshore basins, including directional drilling and hydraulic fracturing (fracking).

### Explained

Onshore Britain has small conventional oil fields (Wytch Farm in Dorset, the largest onshore field in western Europe, in a Triassic sandstone reservoir; East Midlands Carboniferous fields) and shale gas potential in Carboniferous shales (Bowland Shale in northern England). Directional (extended-reach and horizontal) drilling allows wells from one site to reach far beneath sensitive areas (from land under Poole Harbour at Wytch Farm). Fracking opens fractures in tight shale to release gas; it caused small earthquakes in Lancashire in 2011 and 2019, and a moratorium in England followed in 2019. Concerns: induced seismicity, groundwater contamination, water use, traffic, methane leakage and climate impact.

### Common mistakes

- Saying oil forms from dinosaurs. Mostly from marine plankton.
- Saying oil is stored in underground lakes. It fills pore spaces.
- Forgetting timing: trap must form before migration.
