# Lagerstätten

A Lagerstätte (plural Lagerstätten) is a deposit with exceptionally complete preservation of fossils, often including soft tissues that normally decay. Because the usual fossil record is so biased towards hard parts ([05](05-fossils-and-the-fossil-record.md)), Lagerstätten give rare views of whole ecosystems. OCR studies two Cambrian examples and one Jurassic one.

## Conditions for exceptional preservation

### What you need to know

Key rock types and sedimentary structures that show rapid burial; how conditions and early diagenesis control the level of detail preserved.

### Explained

Soft tissues survive only if decay, scavenging and burrowing are stopped quickly. Typical conditions:

- **Rapid burial** by fine sediment (mudflows, storm deposits, turbidity currents), which also prevents scavenging.
- **Anoxic** or otherwise hostile bottom water (no oxygen, high salinity), so no scavengers or burrowers and slow bacterial decay.
- **Fine grain size** (clay, lime mud) that records fine detail.
- **Early mineralisation**: soft tissues replaced or coated by minerals (clay films, pyrite, phosphate, carbon films) before they decay.

Evidence in the rocks: fine laminations undisturbed by burrows (no bioturbation); graded beds or event layers showing sudden deposition; dark colour and pyrite showing anoxia; articulated (joined-up) skeletons.

## Burgess Shale and Chengjiang (OCR 7.2.1(a))

### What you need to know

- Geological settings and sedimentary conditions of the Middle Cambrian Burgess Shale (Canada) and the Lower Cambrian Chengjiang Formation (China).
- How hard and soft tissues were preserved.
- How their fossil assemblages provide evidence for the Cambrian explosion and their depositional palaeoenvironments.

### Explained

**Burgess Shale** (British Columbia, about 508 Ma):

- **Setting**: fine mudstones deposited in deep water at the foot of a steep submarine cliff at the edge of a carbonate reef platform.
- **Preservation**: animals living near the base of the cliff were swept down by mudflows (slurries of fine sediment) into deeper, oxygen-poor water and buried instantly. Soft tissues were preserved as thin carbon films with clay minerals coating them, forming silvery films on the bedding planes. Later the rocks were mildly metamorphosed during mountain building but detail survived.
- **Fossils**: arthropods dominate (trilobites, but mostly soft-bodied arthropods such as *Marrella*), sponges, worms, early chordates (*Pikaia*), and strange forms such as *Opabinia* (five eyes, a frontal grasping nozzle), *Anomalocaris* (a large predator with grasping appendages), *Hallucigenia* (a spiny lobopodian).
- **Significance**: most animals in the community were soft-bodied, so the normal fossil record misses the majority of Cambrian life. It shows a complex food web with predators already present, supporting the rapid diversification of body plans.

**Chengjiang Formation** (Yunnan, China, about 518 Ma):

- **Setting**: shallow marine shelf with fine muds, below normal wave base but affected by storms.
- **Preservation**: event layers from storm-driven or flood-driven mud flows buried organisms rapidly; soft parts preserved as films, often with iron oxides that replaced original pyrite.
- **Fossils**: arthropods, sponges, worms, the earliest known chordates and fish-like vertebrates (*Haikouichthys*, *Myllokunmingia*), lobopodians, and comb jellies.
- **Significance**: it is about 10 million years older than the Burgess Shale, showing that diverse animal body plans, including early vertebrates, were already present early in the Cambrian, strengthening the case for a rapid "explosion".

**Interpreting palaeoenvironments**: the assemblages include filter-feeders (sponges), burrowers, swimmers and predators, showing a normal marine environment with oxygenated water where the animals lived; the burial site was anoxic. Lack of bioturbation in the event beds and fine laminae show quiet bottom conditions after burial.

## Solnhofen Limestone (OCR 7.2.1(b))

### What you need to know

- Setting and conditions of the Jurassic Solnhofen Limestone (Germany): changes in salinity in a shallow lagoon.
- Preservation of feathers and of hard and soft tissues in a range of organisms.
- Evidence for the evolution of *Archaeopteryx*: similarities to both dinosaurs and birds.

### Explained

- **Setting** (Late Jurassic, about 150 Ma): a group of shallow lagoons behind sponge and coral reefs, in a hot, arid climate near the Tethys Ocean.
- **Conditions**: restricted circulation and high evaporation made the bottom water hypersaline and anoxic, so few organisms could live on the floor, there were no scavengers or burrowers, and decay was slow. Animals that were washed in from the open sea, or fell in from the air or nearby land, died and sank. Storms periodically brought fine lime mud that buried them.
- **Rock**: very fine, evenly laminated limestone (lithographic limestone, once used for printing) that splits along bedding planes.
- **Preservation**: complete skeletons, impressions of soft tissues (jellyfish, wing membranes of pterosaurs), and even feathers and the death tracks of horseshoe crabs that crawled briefly before dying.
- **Fossils**: fish, ammonites, crustaceans, horseshoe crabs, insects, pterosaurs, the small theropod dinosaur *Compsognathus*, and *Archaeopteryx*.

**Archaeopteryx**:

| Bird-like features | Dinosaur (reptile) features |
| --- | --- |
| Feathers, including asymmetric flight feathers | Teeth in jaws |
| Wings | Long bony tail |
| Wishbone (furcula) | Clawed fingers on wings |
| | Unfused hand bones, no large keeled breastbone, theropod-like skull and hips |

It is a transitional fossil supporting the evolution of birds from small theropod dinosaurs ([23](23-evolution-and-applied-palaeontology.md)). Later discoveries of feathered dinosaurs in China (in other Lagerstätten) strengthened the link.

### Practical

**OCR PAG5**: study images and replicas of Lagerstätten fossils; draw and label *Archaeopteryx* features.

### Common mistakes

- Saying Lagerstätten form where life was abundant. They form where conditions stop decay; organisms often lived elsewhere and were transported.
- Saying *Archaeopteryx* proves birds came from pterosaurs.

1. A deposit with exceptional preservation of fossils, including soft tissues / complete organisms (1).
2. Any three: rapid burial; anoxic / hypersaline bottom water; no scavengers / burrowers; fine sediment; early mineralisation.
3. Animals living near the base of a submarine cliff (1); swept away by mudflows (1); buried rapidly in deep anoxic water (1); soft tissues preserved as carbon films coated with clay minerals (1).
4. Most organisms soft-bodied (1); so the normal record misses most species (1); shows diverse body plans / complex food webs including predators (1).
5. About 10 million years older than Burgess (1); shows diverse animals including early vertebrates already present early in the Cambrian (1).
6. Restricted lagoon with high evaporation (1); hypersaline, anoxic bottom water (1); no scavengers or burrowers / slow decay (1); fine lime mud from storms buried organisms (1).
7. Bird: feathers / wings / wishbone (any two, 2); dinosaur: teeth / long bony tail / clawed fingers (any two, 2).
