# Specified practicals, fieldwork and practical techniques

Eduqas requires at least four days of fieldwork (with a fieldwork statement sent to WJEC by 15 May of the year of the award) and the twenty specified practical activities (SP1 to SP20) for the Practical Endorsement. Fieldwork must include virtual fieldwork, local fieldwork and work on unfamiliar outcrops, and should be investigative (answering geological questions such as the relative order of events, changes in depositional environment, or tectonic history). Practical skills are also examined in Components 1 to 3, especially the Component 1 practical investigation with hand specimens and a simplified map. OCR candidates: see [OCR 28](../geology-ocr-h414/28-practical-skills-fieldwork-and-pags.md), which covers the same apparatus and techniques list in more detail, including sampling, uncertainty and field sketching.

## Field location and mapping (SP12, SP13)

### Explained

**Six-figure grid references**: on an Ordnance Survey map, read eastings (along the bottom) first, then northings (up the side). Each grid square is 1 km; estimate tenths within a square. For example 345 782 means 4.5 squares east of line 34 and 2 tenths north of line 78, to the nearest 100 m. Navigate without GPS using the map, a compass bearing, pacing and recognisable landmarks.

**Base maps**: mark each locality by grid reference; plot boundaries between rock units where seen (solid line) and inferred (dashed line); add dip and strike symbols (a long line along strike with a short tick pointing down-dip and the angle written beside it); mark faults, fold axes and sample points; include a key, north arrow and scale.

## Field sketches (SP14)

A field sketch records geology, not scenery: include a title, grid reference, direction of view, scale (a scale bar or a labelled object of known size), labelled rock units, structures (bedding, cleavage, faults, folds, unconformities), annotations of interpretation and the date. Draw clean outlines; omit vegetation unless relevant.

## Dip and strike measurement (SP15)

Measure strike with the compass held horizontally along the bedding plane; measure dip with the clinometer perpendicular to strike, down the steepest slope; record as dip/dip direction (for example 24/135). Valid sampling: take several readings across the surface and average; avoid cross-bedding surfaces and loose blocks; note anomalies. Plot directions on rose diagrams or poles on stereonets.

## Earthquake data investigation (SP19)

Download earthquake data (location, depth, magnitude) for a region crossing a subduction zone; measure each event's distance from the trench or plate boundary in Google Earth; plot focal depth against distance (a scatter diagram); calculate a correlation coefficient (Spearman's rank). A dipping line of foci reveals the Benioff zone; the slope gives the dip of the subducting slab: tan(dip) = depth ÷ horizontal distance. Magnitude may show no clear relationship with distance.

### Worked example

Foci at 100 km depth lie 160 km from the trench, and at 300 km depth lie 470 km from it. Slope = (300 − 100) ÷ (470 − 160) = 200 ÷ 310 = 0.645; dip ≈ 33°.

## Metamorphic aureole simulation (SP20)

A model represents a hot intrusion surrounded by country rock (for example a heated block or container in a tray of material, with temperature probes or thermochromic indicators at measured distances). Record temperature against distance and time. Plot maximum temperature against distance: temperature falls away from the "intrusion", matching the zones of an aureole (inner hornfels, outer spotted rock). Variables to explore: size or temperature of the heat source; material conductivity; time. Limitations: real aureoles also depend on fluids, rock composition and the shape and dip of the contact.

## Appendix A: practical skills and the CPAC

Practical skills are assessed indirectly in the written papers (planning, implementing, analysing, evaluating) and directly in the Practical Endorsement against the Common Practical Assessment Criteria: following written procedures; applying investigative approaches with instruments and equipment; using equipment safely with risk assessment; making and recording observations; researching, referencing and reporting.

Apparatus and techniques (Part 3) match the OCR list exactly: field navigation without GPS, field sketches, compass-clinometer, graphic logs, sampling, classification of minerals and fossils, scientific drawings, full rock descriptions, photomicrographs, quantitative measurement (mass, time, volume, temperature, length), density and Mohs hardness tests, improving accuracy (repeat timing, fiducial scales), and ICT and GIS. Fieldwork skills must be shown at unfamiliar exposures. See [OCR 28](../geology-ocr-h414/28-practical-skills-fieldwork-and-pags.md) for detail on sampling, precision and uncertainty.

## Appendix C: mathematical skills (summary)

Units and standard form; ratios, fractions, percentages; estimation; significant figures; arithmetic means; frequency tables, bar charts, histograms; normal and skewed distributions; mean, median, mode; standard deviation and interquartile range; selecting and using a statistical test; scatter diagrams and correlation; sampling principles; uncertainty and percentage error; logarithms (logarithmic plots, phi scale, magnitude); changing the subject and solving equations; y = mx + c, gradient and intercept, rate of change; plotting circular data (rose diagrams, stereonets); trigonometry (sin, cos, tan) for dips and thicknesses; circumference, area and volume (ore and coal tonnage); magnification.

### Common mistakes

- Reading northings before eastings.
- Field sketches without scale or orientation.
- Recording one dip reading as if it were representative.

## Mark scheme

1. Eastings then northings, three figures each (1); locates a point to within 100 m (1).
2. Long line along strike (NE-SW) with a short tick pointing SE, labelled 30 (1); tick shows dip direction, number shows angle (1).
3. Any five: title; grid reference; direction of view; scale; labels of units; structures; interpretation; date.
4. Compass level along bedding for strike (1); clinometer down the steepest slope perpendicular to strike for dip (1); several readings across the surface averaged (1); avoid cross-beds / loose blocks; record location (1).
5. tan θ = 200 ÷ 320 = 0.625 (1); θ = 32° (1).
6. Heat source represents intrusion; temperature measured at distances over time (1); maximum temperature falls with distance like grade zones (1); limitations: no fluids / composition effects (1); simplified geometry / conductivity differs (1).
7. Tests ability to apply knowledge independently (1); real geology is not pre-interpreted, as in exams with unseen maps and specimens (1).
