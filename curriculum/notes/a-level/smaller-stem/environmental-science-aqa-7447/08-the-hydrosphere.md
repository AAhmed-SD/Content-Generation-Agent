# 3.2.2 The hydrosphere

The hydrosphere is all the water on Earth. Fresh water is a renewable resource only if it is used no faster than it is replenished. This file covers the water cycle and human effects on it, sustainable management, the thermohaline circulation, water treatment and new sources. Aquifers and groundwater flow are covered in more geological depth in [Geology 14](../geology-ocr-h414/14-fluids-in-rocks-and-hydrogeology.md).

## 3.2.2.1 The hydrological cycle and unsustainable exploitation

### What you need to know

- The hydrological cycle is a dynamic equilibrium of stores (reservoirs) and flows.
- Human activities change how much water is in each store and how long it stays (residence time).

### Explained

**Stores**: oceans (about 97% of all water), ice caps and glaciers, groundwater, lakes, soil moisture, rivers, atmosphere, living things.

**Flows**: evaporation, transpiration, condensation, precipitation, infiltration, percolation, surface run-off, throughflow, groundwater flow.

**Residence time** = volume of store ÷ rate of flow through it. Water stays about 9 days in the atmosphere, weeks in rivers, years to decades in lakes, up to thousands of years in deep groundwater and ice sheets.

In dynamic equilibrium, inputs and outputs of each store balance on average. Human activities upset this:

| Activity | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| Pumping groundwater faster than recharge | Water table falls; wells dry; land subsides; salt water enters coastal aquifers |
| Abstraction from rivers | Lower flow; less water downstream; wetlands dry (for example the Aral Sea shrank when its feeder rivers were diverted for cotton irrigation) |
| Deforestation | Less interception and transpiration; faster run-off; more flooding, less infiltration |
| Urbanisation | Impermeable surfaces; rapid run-off; less groundwater recharge |
| Reservoirs | Water held longer; more evaporation in hot climates |
| Climate change | Glaciers shrink, reducing dry-season meltwater; changing rainfall patterns |

### Worked example

A lake holds 2.4 × 10⁸ m³ and its outflow is 4.0 × 10⁶ m³ per day. Residence time = 2.4 × 10⁸ ÷ 4.0 × 10⁶ = 60 days. If a pollutant enters the lake, it will take months to flush out.

## 3.2.2.2 Sustainable management of water

### What you need to know

Strategies for sustainable management, using examples of exploited water resources.

### Explained

Sustainable use keeps abstraction below recharge and protects water quality. Strategies:

- abstraction licences that limit how much each user can take, based on recharge
- catchment management: planting trees and restoring wetlands to slow run-off and raise infiltration
- managed aquifer recharge: deliberately putting excess winter water back into aquifers
- efficient irrigation (drip instead of flood irrigation)
- water pricing to reduce waste
- protecting recharge areas from pollution.

## 3.2.2.3 Thermohaline circulation

### What you need to know

How the thermohaline circulation redistributes heat and affects climate, including in the UK and North Atlantic.

### Explained

"Thermo" means temperature, "haline" means salt. Both control seawater density.

1. The Gulf Stream and North Atlantic Drift carry warm surface water north-east from the tropics.
2. It releases heat to the air, warming western Europe (the UK is several degrees warmer than places at the same latitude in Canada).
3. The water becomes cold, and sea-ice formation and evaporation make it saltier, so it is dense and sinks near Greenland and Iceland (North Atlantic Deep Water).
4. Deep water flows south and around the globe, rising in other oceans and returning at the surface. A full circuit takes about a thousand years.

**Climate change risk**: melting Greenland ice and higher rainfall add fresh water, making the surface water less dense, so less sinks. A weaker circulation would carry less heat north, causing relative cooling of north-west Europe, changes in rainfall, and possibly faster sea-level rise on the US east coast.

## 3.2.2.4 Treating contaminated water

### What you need to know

How litter, suspended solids, metals and odours, organic pollutants, salt and pathogens are removed.

### Explained

| Contaminant | Method |
| --- | --- |
| Litter and large objects | Screens (metal bars) |
| Suspended solids | Settlement tanks; flocculation (adding aluminium or iron salts to make particles clump); sand filtration |
| Metals and odours | Oxidation by aeration; activated carbon adsorption; precipitation |
| Organic pollutants | Aerobic decomposition by microorganisms; activated carbon |
| Salt | Desalination: reverse osmosis (pushing water through a membrane at high pressure) or distillation; both need a lot of energy |
| Pathogens | Chlorination, ozone or UV light |

Sewage treatment stages are in [17](17-oil-pesticides-nutrients-and-metals.md).

## 3.2.2.5 Economical use and new sources

### What you need to know

- Economical use: metering, low water-use appliances, grey water.
- New sources: rainwater catchment, new reservoirs or estuary barrages, unexploited aquifers, inter-basin transfers.

### Explained

**Economical use**:

- Metering: paying for what you use encourages saving; also helps find leaks.
- Low water-use appliances: dual-flush toilets, aerating taps, low-flow showers, efficient washing machines.
- Grey water: reusing lightly used water from basins and showers to flush toilets or water gardens.

**New sources**:

| Source | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rainwater catchment (roofs into tanks) | Cheap, local | Depends on rainfall; storage space |
| New reservoir | Large, reliable store | Floods land and habitats; displaces people; sediment fills it |
| Estuary barrage | Stores fresh river water | Destroys mudflat habitat; blocks fish migration |
| Unexploited aquifers | Often clean, large | Risk of overuse; may be slow to recharge or saline |
| Inter-basin transfer (pipes or canals between catchments) | Moves water from wet to dry areas | Expensive; harms donor river; may spread invasive species |
| Desalination | Unlimited sea water | High energy and cost; brine waste |

### Common mistakes

- Treating groundwater as unlimited. Deep aquifers may take thousands of years to refill ("fossil water").
- Saying the thermohaline circulation is driven by wind. Surface currents are wind-driven; the deep circulation is driven by density.
