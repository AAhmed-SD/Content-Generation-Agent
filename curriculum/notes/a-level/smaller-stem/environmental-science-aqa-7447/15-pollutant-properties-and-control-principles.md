# Pollutant properties, environmental factors and control principles

Pollution is the release of a substance or energy that causes harm. How harmful a pollutant is depends on its own properties and on the environment it enters. This file gives the general ideas used for every pollutant in files [16](16-air-and-thermal-pollution.md), [17](17-oil-pesticides-nutrients-and-metals.md) and [18](18-solid-waste-noise-and-ionising-radiation.md).

## 3.4.1 Properties of pollutants

### What you need to know

State of matter; form of energy; density; persistence and degradability; toxicity; reactivity; adsorption; solubility in lipids and in water; bioaccumulation; biomagnification; synergism; mutagenic, carcinogenic and teratogenic effects.

### Explained

| Property | Meaning and why it matters | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| State | Gases and fine particles disperse widely in air; liquids flow and soak into soil; solids stay put | SO₂ gas spreads far; spoil stays local |
| Energy form | Pollution can be energy rather than material | Noise, heat, ionising radiation, light |
| Density | Decides whether it sinks, floats or rises | Oil floats; dense gases collect in hollows |
| Persistence / degradability | How long it lasts before breaking down; persistent pollutants build up | DDT, CFCs, plastics, heavy metals persist |
| Toxicity | The dose that causes harm; often given as LD50 (dose killing 50% of a test population) | Mercury compounds very toxic |
| Reactivity | Reactive pollutants change into other substances, sometimes worse ones (secondary pollutants) | NOx forms ozone in sunlight |
| Adsorption | Sticking to surfaces of particles such as clay or organic matter; reduces mobility | Metal ions on clay |
| Lipid solubility | Dissolves in fats, so stored in fatty tissue instead of excreted | DDT, methylmercury |
| Water solubility | Dissolves in water, so spreads in rivers and groundwater; usually excreted | Nitrate |
| Bioaccumulation | Concentration builds up in an organism over its life because intake exceeds loss | Mercury in fish |
| Biomagnification | Concentration increases at each trophic level along a food chain | DDT in birds of prey |
| Synergism | Two pollutants together cause more harm than the sum of their separate effects | SO₂ and particulates in smog |
| Mutagenic | Causes mutations in DNA | Ionising radiation, benzene |
| Carcinogenic | Causes cancer | Asbestos, some hydrocarbons |
| Teratogenic | Causes birth defects in developing embryos | Methylmercury |

### Worked example

Biomagnification: water contains 0.00005 ppm of a pesticide; zooplankton 0.04 ppm; small fish 0.5 ppm; large fish 2.0 ppm; fish-eating birds 25 ppm. Concentration factor from water to birds = 25 ÷ 0.00005 = 500 000. From large fish to birds the factor is 12.5.

## 3.4.2.1 Dispersal

### What you need to know

Point sources and diffuse sources; the speed and direction of air and water currents.

### Explained

- **Point source**: a single identifiable outlet, such as a factory chimney or sewage pipe. Easier to monitor and control.
- **Diffuse (non-point) source**: many small sources spread over a large area, such as fertiliser run-off from farms or vehicle exhausts. Harder to control.
- Strong winds and fast currents dilute and carry pollutants away (lower concentration locally but spread further). Still air and slow water let concentrations build up. Prevailing wind direction decides who is downwind.

## 3.4.2.2 Degradation and other environmental factors

### What you need to know

How severity is affected by temperature, light, oxygen, pH, other chemicals, temperature inversions and adsorbent materials.

### Explained

| Factor | Effect | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Temperature | Higher temperature speeds microbial decomposition | Sewage breaks down faster in summer |
| Light | Drives photochemical reactions; can break pollutants down | Photochemical smog forms; some pesticides are broken down by UV |
| Oxygen | Needed for aerobic decay; low oxygen slows breakdown | Organic waste persists in stagnant water |
| pH | Alkaline rocks neutralise acids | Limestone areas are less affected by acid rain |
| Other chemicals | Reactions form secondary pollutants | Ozone oxidises SO₂ and NO₂ to acids |
| Temperature inversion | A layer of warm air over cold air stops vertical mixing, trapping pollutants near the ground | Winter smogs in valleys |
| Adsorbent materials | Clay and organic matter bind pollutants, reducing mobility and toxicity | Clay binds heavy metal ions |

**Temperature inversion**: normally air cools with height, so warm surface air rises and carries pollutants up. On still, clear nights the ground loses heat fast, cooling the air next to it; or cold air drains into a valley. Warmer air above acts as a lid. Pollutants build up until the Sun heats the ground or wind breaks the inversion.

## 3.4.3.1 Principles of control

### What you need to know

- Critical Pathway Analysis (CPA): predicting how a pollutant moves through the environment.
- Critical Group Monitoring (CGM): monitoring the people most at risk.
- Location of emissions (avoiding valleys and enclosed water bodies).
- Timing of emissions (restricting activity during temperature inversions).

### Explained

- **CPA**: trace the likely route of a pollutant from source to people and wildlife (air, water, soil, food chains) and identify the routes giving the highest exposure. Monitoring and controls then focus there. Example: a radioactive discharge into the sea may concentrate in seaweed and shellfish eaten by local people.
- **CGM**: identify and monitor the group with the greatest exposure (for example people who eat a lot of locally caught shellfish). If they are safe, everyone else should be safer.
- **Location**: release pollutants where they disperse well: tall chimneys above inversion layers; outfalls into fast-flowing water or open sea, not enclosed lakes or valleys.
- **Timing**: restrict burning, traffic or industrial releases during inversions and still weather; release effluent on the ebb tide so it is carried out to sea.

### Common mistakes

- Using bioaccumulation and biomagnification interchangeably. One organism over time versus increasing concentration up food chains.
- Saying tall chimneys remove pollution. They disperse it, sometimes moving the problem elsewhere (acid rain downwind).
