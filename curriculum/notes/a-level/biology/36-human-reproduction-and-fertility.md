# Human reproduction, fertility and ageing of the reproductive system

This file covers the human reproductive systems, how sperm and eggs are made and how they are adapted, hormonal control of gametogenesis and the menstrual cycle, fertilisation, early development and the placenta, infertility and assisted reproduction, and the effects of ageing.

## The reproductive systems and gametogenesis

*Refs: OCR B 4.2.1(a) to (e) · Edexcel A 3.6 · Edexcel B 2.4 i · Eduqas C2.3(a), (b). Level: AS & A (Edexcel); A-level only (OCR B, Eduqas).*

### What you need to know

- The structure and functions of the male and female reproductive systems.
- The histology of the testis and ovary.
- Spermatogenesis and oogenesis.
- The structure of the sperm and the secondary oocyte and how they are adapted.
- The role of hormones in gametogenesis.

### Explained

**Male system.** Testes (in the scrotum, kept about 2 to 3 °C below body temperature, which suits sperm production) make sperm and testosterone. Epididymis (sperm mature and are stored); sperm duct (vas deferens); seminal vesicles and prostate gland (add fluid with fructose for respiration and alkaline substances); urethra (shared with the urinary system, hence "urinogenital").

**Female system.** Ovaries (make eggs, oestrogen and progesterone); oviducts (Fallopian tubes: fertilisation happens here; cilia move the egg); uterus (thick muscular wall; lining, the endometrium, receives the embryo); cervix; vagina.

**Testis histology.** The testis is packed with **seminiferous tubules**. Around the outside of each tubule are spermatogonia; cells divide and move inwards as they develop, so the most mature sperm are near the lumen. **Sertoli cells** in the tubule wall nourish developing sperm. Between the tubules are **interstitial (Leydig) cells**, which make testosterone.

**Ovary histology.** Follicles at different stages: primary follicles (an oocyte with one layer of cells) near the edge; developing follicles with a fluid-filled space; a mature (Graafian) follicle that bulges at the surface; after ovulation, the empty follicle becomes the **corpus luteum**.

**Spermatogenesis** (in seminiferous tubules, continuous from puberty).

1. Spermatogonia (diploid) divide by mitosis to make more cells.
2. Some grow into primary spermatocytes (diploid).
3. Meiosis I gives two secondary spermatocytes (haploid).
4. Meiosis II gives four spermatids (haploid).
5. Spermatids differentiate into spermatozoa (developing a tail, mitochondria, acrosome).

**Oogenesis.**

1. Before birth, oogonia divide by mitosis and grow into primary oocytes, which begin meiosis I and stop in prophase I. A girl is born with all her primary oocytes.
2. From puberty, each month a few follicles develop; one primary oocyte completes meiosis I, producing a large secondary oocyte and a small polar body (most cytoplasm goes to the oocyte).
3. The secondary oocyte begins meiosis II and stops at metaphase II. It is released at ovulation.
4. Meiosis II completes only if a sperm enters, producing the ovum and a second polar body.

| | Spermatogenesis | Oogenesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number from one cell | Four sperm | One egg (plus polar bodies) |
| Timing | Continuous from puberty | Starts before birth; pauses; completes one at a time from puberty to menopause |
| Size | Small, little cytoplasm | Large, much cytoplasm |

**Gamete adaptations** (Edexcel A 3.6).

- *Sperm:* head with a haploid nucleus and an acrosome (a modified lysosome with enzymes to digest the egg's coverings); midpiece packed with mitochondria (ATP for the tail); tail (flagellum) for swimming.
- *Secondary oocyte:* large, with much cytoplasm containing nutrients for the early embryo; haploid nucleus; surrounded by the **zona pellucida** (a glycoprotein layer) and outer follicle cells (corona radiata); cortical granules under its membrane.

**Hormones in gametogenesis.** The hypothalamus releases GnRH, which makes the anterior pituitary release **FSH** and **LH**. In males, FSH stimulates Sertoli cells to support spermatogenesis; LH stimulates interstitial cells to make testosterone, which is needed for spermatogenesis and male secondary sexual characteristics. Testosterone inhibits GnRH and LH (negative feedback).

## The menstrual cycle, fertilisation and the placenta

*Refs: OCR B 4.2.1(f), (g) · Edexcel A 3.7 · Edexcel B 2.4 ii, iii · Eduqas C2.3(c), (d). Level: as above.*

### What you need to know

- The hormonal control of the menstrual cycle.
- The events of fertilisation: from first contact to the fusion of nuclei, including the acrosome reaction and the cortical reaction.
- Early development to the blastocyst; implantation.
- The role of the placenta, including hormones.

### Explained

**The menstrual cycle** (about 28 days).

| Days | Ovary | Uterus | Hormones |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | Follicles start to develop | Lining shed (menstruation) | Low progesterone; FSH rising |
| 6 to 13 | A follicle matures (follicular phase) | Lining rebuilds and thickens | FSH stimulates follicle growth; follicle makes oestrogen; oestrogen repairs lining and at first inhibits FSH and LH |
| About 14 | Ovulation | | High oestrogen now stimulates (positive feedback) a surge of LH (and FSH); the LH surge causes ovulation |
| 15 to 28 | Corpus luteum forms (luteal phase) | Lining maintained, glands and blood vessels develop | LH stimulates the corpus luteum to make progesterone (and oestrogen); progesterone inhibits FSH and LH |
| End | If no pregnancy, corpus luteum breaks down | Lining is shed | Progesterone falls; FSH and LH rise again; new cycle |

**Fertilisation.**

1. Sperm swim through the cervix and uterus to the oviduct. **Capacitation:** in the female tract, changes to the sperm's membrane make it able to fertilise.
2. The sperm passes between the follicle cells and binds to receptors on the zona pellucida.
3. **Acrosome reaction:** the acrosome releases enzymes (proteases) that digest a path through the zona pellucida.
4. The sperm's membrane fuses with the oocyte's membrane; the sperm nucleus enters.
5. **Cortical reaction:** cortical granules release their contents by exocytosis into the zona pellucida, which hardens it and changes its receptors, so no more sperm can enter (preventing polyspermy).
6. The secondary oocyte completes meiosis II.
7. The sperm and egg nuclei fuse, forming a diploid zygote.

**Early development** (Edexcel B 2.4 iii). The zygote divides by mitosis as it moves down the oviduct (cleavage): 2, 4, 8 cells, then a solid ball (morula). After about 5 days it is a **blastocyst**: a hollow ball with an outer layer (trophoblast, which forms the placenta) and an inner cell mass (pluripotent cells that form the embryo). The blastocyst implants in the endometrium.

**The placenta** (Eduqas C2.3(d)). Formed from embryonic (chorion) and maternal tissue; villi give a large surface area; fetal blood in capillaries in the villi is very close to maternal blood in spaces around them, but the two do not mix.

- Exchange: oxygen, glucose, amino acids, antibodies (passive immunity) pass to the fetus; CO₂ and urea pass to the mother. Fetal haemoglobin's higher affinity helps (file 10).
- Barrier: blocks many bacteria and large molecules, though some viruses, drugs and alcohol cross.
- Hormones: the embryo first makes **hCG**, which keeps the corpus luteum making progesterone, so the lining is not shed. Later the placenta itself makes progesterone and oestrogen (maintaining the endometrium and inhibiting FSH and LH, so no more ovulation) and human placental lactogen.
- Oxytocin (from the posterior pituitary) causes contractions at birth; prolactin causes milk production.

## Infertility, assisted reproduction and ageing

*Refs: OCR B 4.2.1(h) to (j), 4.2.2(a) to (c). Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- The biological and ethical issues surrounding infertility.
- Assisted reproduction, including IVF.
- The effects of ageing on the female and male reproductive systems and how the effects are managed.

### Explained

**Causes of infertility.** In women: no ovulation (hormone imbalance), blocked oviducts (after infection), endometriosis. In men: low sperm count or poor motility. Age reduces fertility.

**Assisted reproduction.**

- *Fertility drugs:* FSH and LH (or drugs that increase them, such as clomifene) stimulate follicle development and ovulation.
- *In vitro fertilisation (IVF):* hormones stimulate many follicles; eggs are collected with a needle; eggs and sperm are mixed in a dish (or a single sperm is injected into an egg, ICSI, when sperm are few or weak); embryos grow for 2 to 5 days; one or two are placed in the uterus. Success rates fall with the woman's age. Spare embryos can be frozen. Pre-implantation genetic diagnosis can test embryos for serious inherited disorders.
- Issues: cost and who should receive NHS-funded treatment; multiple pregnancies (higher risk); what happens to unused embryos; the use of donated eggs or sperm and the child's right to know their genetic parent; age limits; the emotional effect of failed cycles; HFEA regulation in the UK. Religious and personal views differ on which treatments are acceptable.

**Ageing: women.** The **menopause** (usually between 45 and 55): the supply of follicles runs out; the ovaries stop responding; oestrogen and progesterone fall; menstruation and ovulation stop. FSH and LH rise (less negative feedback). Effects: hot flushes, mood changes, thinning of the vaginal lining; loss of bone density (osteoporosis risk, file 47); higher risk of heart disease. **Hormone replacement therapy (HRT)** gives oestrogen (with progesterone if the woman has a uterus) to relieve symptoms and protect bone; risks for some women include blood clots and a small increase in some cancers, so benefits and risks are weighed individually.

**Ageing: men.** Testosterone falls gradually; sperm production continues but declines; the **prostate gland enlarges** (benign prostatic hyperplasia), pressing on the urethra and causing difficulty urinating; prostate cancer becomes more common (detected with PSA blood tests and examination). Erectile problems are more common; treatment with drugs that increase blood flow.

### Common mistakes

- Saying ovulation releases a mature ovum (it releases a secondary oocyte).
- Saying LH makes the follicle grow (FSH does; LH triggers ovulation).
- Saying the placenta lets maternal and fetal blood mix.
- Saying the cortical reaction digests the zona pellucida (the acrosome reaction does).
