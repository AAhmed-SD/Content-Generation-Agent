# Organic synthesis: routes, nitriles, C–C bond formation and practical techniques

Synthesis puts together the reactions from all the organic files to plan a route from a starting material to a target. This file gathers the reactions into maps, covers ways to lengthen a carbon chain (nitriles, hydroxynitriles, Grignard reagents, Friedel–Crafts), the reactions of nitriles, the mechanisms used across the course, and the practical techniques for making and purifying products.

Mostly A-level. Every reaction here is explained in its own file (17 to 28); this file is for linking them.

## 3.3.14 Organic synthesis

*Refs: AQA 3.3.14 · Edexcel 18.18 to 18.22, CP16 · OCR A 4.2.3(b), (c), 6.2.4(a) to (c), 6.2.5(a) to (c) · OCR B WM(e), WM(f), CD(j), CD(l) · Eduqas OA2.3(h) to (j), OA4(a), (b), (e). Level: A-level only (OCR A 4.2.3, OCR B WM AS).*

### What you need to know

- Reagents, conditions and equations for every conversion in the course.
- Plan multi-step routes, aliphatic and aromatic.
- Methods to increase chain length: KCN with halogenoalkanes, HCN with carbonyls, Grignard reagents (Edexcel), Friedel–Crafts (OCR A).
- Reactions of nitriles: hydrolysis to acids, reduction to amines.
- Practical techniques: reflux, distillation, separating funnel, drying, filtration under reduced pressure, recrystallisation, melting and boiling points.
- Chemists aim for fewer steps, high yield and atom economy, and safe, non-hazardous reagents and solvents.

**Nitriles.**

- Made from halogenoalkanes and KCN in ethanol (adds one carbon), or from carbonyls and HCN (hydroxynitriles).
- **Hydrolysis** by refluxing with dilute acid: CH₃CH₂CN + 2H₂O + H⁺ → CH₃CH₂COOH + NH₄⁺. With alkali, the carboxylate salt and NH₃ form.
- **Reduction** with LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni: CH₃CH₂CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
- Hydroxynitriles give 2-hydroxy acids on hydrolysis: CH₃CH(OH)CN → CH₃CH(OH)COOH (lactic acid).

**Grignard reagents (Edexcel 18.20).** Made by reacting a halogenoalkane with magnesium in dry ether: CH₃CH₂Br + Mg → CH₃CH₂MgBr. The carbon bonded to Mg is δ−, so it acts as a nucleophile and forms a new C–C bond. Conditions must be dry (water destroys the reagent). After reaction, dilute acid is added.

- With CO₂ (dry ice): carboxylic acid with one more carbon. CH₃CH₂MgBr + CO₂ → CH₃CH₂COOMgBr; then acid → CH₃CH₂COOH.
- With methanal: primary alcohol (one more carbon).
- With other aldehydes: secondary alcohol.
- With ketones: tertiary alcohol.

**Planning routes.** Work backwards from the target: which functional group is it, and what makes it? Count carbons: if the chain grows, a nitrile, hydroxynitrile, Grignard or Friedel–Crafts step is needed. Watch for groups that react with your reagents (protect or reorder).

**Choosing a route.** Fewer steps give higher overall yield (yields multiply: 80% × 80% × 80% = 51%). Prefer high atom economy, renewable starting materials, safe solvents and reagents, low temperatures, and catalysts (file 35).

**Deducing formulae (Edexcel 18.18).** Combine combustion data (empirical formula, file 2), Mᵣ from the mass spectrum, functional group tests and spectra.

### Worked example

1. Plan a synthesis of propanoic acid from ethene.

   Ethene + HBr → bromoethane. Bromoethane + KCN (ethanol, reflux) → propanenitrile. Propanenitrile + dilute HCl, reflux → propanoic acid.

2. Plan a synthesis of 2-hydroxy-2-methylbutanoic acid from butan-2-ol.

   Oxidise butan-2-ol (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux) to butanone. Add HCN (KCN + acid) to give 2-hydroxy-2-methylbutanenitrile. Hydrolyse (dilute HCl, reflux) to the acid.

3. A route has three steps with yields 75%, 60% and 90%. What is the overall yield?

   0.75 × 0.60 × 0.90 = 0.405, so 40.5%.

### Practical

**Making and purifying a liquid (OCR B WM(f), Eduqas):** reflux; distil; wash in a separating funnel (sodium hydrogencarbonate to remove acid, then water); dry with an anhydrous salt; redistil, collecting at the boiling point (file 21).

**Making and purifying a solid (OCR A 6.2.5(a), OCR B WM(e), Edexcel 18.22, Eduqas):** react; cool to crystallise; filter under reduced pressure with a Büchner funnel (faster and drier than gravity filtration); recrystallise from the minimum volume of hot solvent; dry; measure the melting point (file 24).

**Two-step synthesis (Eduqas):** for example, nitrobenzene to phenylamine to N-phenylethanamide, purifying and checking the melting point at the end.

**Choosing a solvent for recrystallisation:** the product must be very soluble in the hot solvent and much less soluble when cold; impurities should either be very soluble (stay in solution) or insoluble (removed by hot filtration).

### Common mistakes

- Missing a step that adds a carbon.
- Giving reagents without conditions (for example "KOH" without saying aqueous or ethanolic).
- Using NaBH₄ to reduce a carboxylic acid (it cannot; use LiAlH₄).
- Adding yields instead of multiplying them.
