# Group 7: the halogens, halides and tests for ions

The halogens are reactive non-metals that act as oxidising agents. This file covers trends in their physical properties, electronegativity and oxidising power, displacement reactions, the reducing power of halide ions with sulfuric acid, silver halides, chlorine in water treatment and bleach, and the test-tube tests for common ions. It includes AQA required practical 4.

## Overview

AS content. You need redox and oxidation states from file 7 and intermolecular forces from file 3. Halogenoalkanes are in file 19.

## 3.2.3.1 Trends in properties

*Refs: AQA 3.2.3.1 · Edexcel 4.9 to 4.11, 4.13 to 4.15 · OCR A 3.1.3(a) to (d), (g) · OCR B ES(h) to (m) · Eduqas C1.6(l) to (p), PI2.1(l). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Trends in electronegativity and boiling point down Group 7, and their explanations.
- Trend in oxidising ability (F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂), shown by displacement reactions.
- Trend in reducing ability of halide ions (I⁻ > Br⁻ > Cl⁻ > F⁻), shown by reactions of solid sodium halides with concentrated sulfuric acid.
- Silver nitrate test for halide ions, and the solubility of the silver halides in ammonia.

### Explained

**Physical properties.**

| Halogen | State at room temperature | Colour | In water | In hexane (organic) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| F₂ | Gas | Pale yellow | Reacts | — |
| Cl₂ | Gas | Pale green | Pale green | Pale green |
| Br₂ | Liquid | Red-brown | Orange / yellow | Orange / red |
| I₂ | Solid (shiny grey-black; purple vapour) | Grey-black | Brown (slightly soluble) | Violet / purple |

**Boiling point increases** down the group. The molecules get bigger with more electrons, so stronger London forces between them.

**Electronegativity decreases** down the group. The atoms get larger with more shielding, so the bonding pair is further from the nucleus and less attracted.

**Oxidising ability decreases** down the group. A halogen oxidises by gaining an electron. Down the group the atom is larger, the incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded, so it is attracted less strongly.

**Displacement reactions.** A more reactive halogen oxidises the halide ion of a less reactive one.

| Added | Cl⁻(aq) | Br⁻(aq) | I⁻(aq) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cl₂(aq) | — | Orange solution (Br₂) | Brown solution (I₂) |
| Br₂(aq) | No change | — | Brown solution (I₂) |
| I₂(aq) | No change | No change | — |

Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂. Half-equations: Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻ (reduction); 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻ (oxidation).

Shaking with hexane makes the colours clearer: bromine orange-red, iodine purple in the upper organic layer.

**Halides with concentrated sulfuric acid (reducing ability).** The halide ions get better at losing electrons (stronger reducing agents) down the group, because they are larger and the outer electron is further from the nucleus.

| Solid | Products and observations | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| NaF / NaCl | Misty white fumes of HF / HCl | Acid–base only: NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl. Cl⁻ cannot reduce H₂SO₄. |
| NaBr | Misty fumes (HBr), orange-brown fumes (Br₂), colourless choking gas (SO₂) | NaBr + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HBr; then 2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O. S goes +6 → +4. |
| NaI | Misty fumes (HI), purple fumes / black solid (I₂), SO₂, yellow solid (S), rotten-egg smell (H₂S) | 2HI + H₂SO₄ → I₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O; 6HI + H₂SO₄ → 3I₂ + S + 4H₂O; 8HI + H₂SO₄ → 4I₂ + H₂S + 4H₂O. S goes to +4, 0 and −2. |

Half-equations for iodide: 2I⁻ → I₂ + 2e⁻; H₂SO₄ + 8H⁺ + 8e⁻ → H₂S + 4H₂O.

**Hydrogen halides (OCR B, Edexcel).**

- Preparing: HCl from NaCl and concentrated sulfuric acid. HBr and HI cannot be made this way because they reduce the sulfuric acid; instead use the halide with concentrated phosphoric acid, which is not an oxidising agent.
- All are colourless gases that fume in moist air, dissolve in water to form strong acids (HCl, HBr, HI) and react with ammonia gas to form white smoke of ammonium halide: NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl.
- Thermal stability decreases from HF to HI, because the H–X bond gets longer and weaker. A hot wire plunged into HI gives purple iodine vapour; HCl does not decompose.

**Silver nitrate test.** Acidify the solution with dilute nitric acid (removes carbonate or hydroxide ions, which would also give precipitates), then add silver nitrate.

| Halide | Precipitate | Dilute NH₃ | Concentrated NH₃ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cl⁻ | White AgCl | Dissolves | Dissolves |
| Br⁻ | Cream AgBr | Does not dissolve | Dissolves |
| I⁻ | Yellow AgI | Does not dissolve | Does not dissolve |
| F⁻ | No precipitate (AgF is soluble) | — | — |

Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s). The precipitate dissolves when ammonia forms [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. Do not acidify with HCl (it adds Cl⁻).

**Predicting fluorine and astatine (Edexcel 4.14).** Fluorine is a pale yellow gas, the strongest oxidising agent, and AgF is soluble. Astatine would be a black solid, the weakest oxidising agent, and AgAt would be insoluble even in concentrated ammonia.

### Common mistakes

- Saying bromine "displaces" chloride. A halogen displaces a less reactive halide.
- Explaining oxidising power with "more shells, so more electrons". Say the incoming electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded.
- Forgetting to add nitric acid first in the silver nitrate test, or adding hydrochloric acid instead.
- Saying the sulfuric acid oxidises Cl⁻. It does not; only an acid–base reaction happens.

## 3.2.3.2 Uses of chlorine and chlorate(I)

*Refs: AQA 3.2.3.2, RP4 · Edexcel 4.12, 4.15, CP7 · OCR A 3.1.3(e), (f), 3.1.4(a) · OCR B ES(n) · Eduqas C1.6(q), PI2.1(j), (k). Level: AS & A (Eduqas PI2.1 A-level).*

### What you need to know

- Reaction of chlorine with water, and with cold dilute sodium hydroxide (both disproportionations).
- Chlorine in water treatment: benefits and risks; chlorine in sunlight.
- Sodium chlorate(I) as bleach.
- Chlorine with hot alkali (Edexcel, Eduqas).
- Test-tube tests for ions (AQA RP4; OCR A 3.1.4).

### Explained

**Chlorine with water.**

Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO. Chlorine goes from 0 to −1 and +1: disproportionation. Chloric(I) acid (HClO) kills bacteria. Universal indicator turns red then colourless (HClO bleaches it).

In bright sunlight chlorine water decomposes: 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O → 4HCl + O₂.

**Water treatment.** Small amounts of chlorine kill bacteria in drinking water and swimming pools, preventing diseases such as cholera and typhoid. Benefits outweigh the risks, but:

- chlorine is toxic, so the dose must be controlled;
- chlorine can react with organic matter to form chlorinated hydrocarbons, some of which may cause cancer.

Alternatives such as ozone or UV light have no lasting effect in the pipes. Fluoride ions are added to some water supplies to reduce tooth decay (Eduqas C1.6(q)), which raises questions about mass medication.

**Storage and transport (OCR B ES(n)).** Chlorine is a toxic gas, transported as a liquid under pressure. A leak can cause serious harm. Its uses (sterilising water, bleaching, making PVC and other chemicals) must be weighed against these risks.

**Chlorine with cold dilute sodium hydroxide.**

Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O. The solution of sodium chlorate(I) is bleach.

**Chlorine with hot concentrated sodium hydroxide (Edexcel, Eduqas).**

3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O. Chlorine goes 0 → −1 and 0 → +5. The chlorate(I) disproportionates when warm: 3ClO⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻.

**Bleaching and bactericidal action (Eduqas PI2.1(k)).** ClO⁻ and HClO are oxidising agents. They oxidise coloured dyes to colourless products and destroy enzymes and cell components in microbes.

**Tests for ions (AQA RP4, OCR A 3.1.4, Edexcel CP7, Eduqas).**

| Ion | Test | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group 2 ions | Add NaOH(aq) dropwise, then excess; add dilute H₂SO₄ | Mg²⁺ white ppt with OH⁻; Ba²⁺ white ppt with SO₄²⁻; Ca²⁺ slight ppt with both |
| NH₄⁺ | Warm with NaOH(aq) | Gas turns damp red litmus blue (NH₃): NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃ + H₂O |
| OH⁻ | Red litmus / indicator | Turns blue; pH above 7 |
| CO₃²⁻ | Add dilute acid; bubble gas through limewater | Effervescence; limewater goes cloudy |
| SO₄²⁻ | Add dilute HCl, then BaCl₂(aq) | White precipitate of BaSO₄ |
| Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻ | Add dilute HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq), then NH₃ | White, cream, yellow precipitates (see above) |

**Order of tests (OCR A).** When testing for several anions in one solution, do carbonate first (add acid; any CO₃²⁻ is removed as CO₂), then sulfate (BaCO₃ would also be white, so carbonate must be gone first), then halides (Ag₂SO₄ can precipitate, so sulfate test comes before). Use HCl for the carbonate test only if you will not then test for chloride; use nitric acid otherwise.

### Practical

**AQA RP4: test-tube reactions to identify cations (Group 2, NH₄⁺) and anions (Group 7 halides, OH⁻, CO₃²⁻, SO₄²⁻).** Use a few drops of each solution in a test tube, add the reagent dropwise and record observations including colours and whether a precipitate dissolves in excess. Carry out the ammonium test with warm (not boiling) mixture and hold damp red litmus at the mouth of the tube without touching the glass.

**Edexcel CP7 and Eduqas: analysis of unknowns.** Plan a sequence of these tests to identify an unknown salt, recording positive and negative results.

## Mark scheme

1. Boiling point increases (1); molecules larger with more electrons (1); stronger London forces need more energy to overcome (1).
2. Chlorine atom smaller (1); incoming electron closer to nucleus / less shielding (1); stronger attraction so electron gained more easily (1).
3. Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (1); solution turns orange / yellow (1).
4. Any two of: misty white fumes, orange / brown fumes, colourless choking gas (2); 2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O (1).
5. I⁻ larger / outer electrons further from nucleus and more shielded (1); so electrons lost more easily / stronger reducing agent (1).
6. Add dilute HNO₃ to remove carbonate / other ions that would precipitate (1); add AgNO₃: chloride white ppt, bromide cream ppt (1); add dilute NH₃ (1); AgCl dissolves, AgBr does not (1).
7. Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HClO (1); Cl goes from 0 to −1 (reduced) (1) and 0 to +1 (oxidised) (1).
8. Benefit: kills bacteria / prevents disease (1). Risk: toxic gas / forms chlorinated hydrocarbons that may cause cancer (1).
9. Warm with NaOH(aq) (1); gas turns damp red litmus blue (1).
10. Add dilute nitric (or hydrochloric) acid: effervescence, gas turns limewater cloudy shows carbonate (1); continue until no more fizzing so carbonate removed (1); then add BaCl₂ / Ba(NO₃)₂: white precipitate shows sulfate (1).
