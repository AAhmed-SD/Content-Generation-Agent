# Condensation polymers, biodegradability and disposal

Condensation polymers form when monomers join and a small molecule, usually water or HCl, is lost at each link. This file covers polyesters and polyamides (Terylene, nylon 6,6, Kevlar, poly(lactic acid)), drawing repeat units and monomers, hydrolysis, the forces between chains, and biodegradability and disposal compared with addition polymers.

## Overview

A-level only. Addition polymers are in file 20; esters and amides in file 24; proteins (natural polyamides) in file 28.

## 3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers

*Refs: AQA 3.3.12.1 · Edexcel 17.16, 18.14, 18.15 · OCR A 6.2.3(a) to (c) · OCR B PL(o), PL(p) · Eduqas OA4(c), (d). Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- Condensation polymers form from monomers with two functional groups, losing a small molecule at each link.
- Polyesters: from a diol and a dicarboxylic acid (or diacyl chloride), or from a hydroxycarboxylic acid.
- Polyamides: from a diamine and a dicarboxylic acid (or diacyl chloride), or from an amino acid.
- Draw repeat units from monomers, and monomers from a section of polymer.
- Examples: Terylene (PET), nylon 6,6, Kevlar, poly(lactic acid).
- Hydrogen bonding between polyamide chains; the strength of Kevlar.

### Explained

**Addition compared with condensation.**

| | Addition | Condensation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Monomer | Contains C=C | Two functional groups (or two different monomers each with two) |
| By-product | None | Small molecule (H₂O or HCl) |
| Link | C–C | Ester (–COO–) or amide (–CONH–) |
| Backbone | Only C | Contains O or N |

**Terylene (PET).** Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid + ethane-1,2-diol:

n HOOC–C₆H₄–COOH + n HOCH₂CH₂OH → –[OC–C₆H₄–COO–CH₂CH₂–O]ₙ– + 2n H₂O

Repeat unit: –CO–C₆H₄–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O–. Uses: bottles, clothing fibres.

**Nylon 6,6.** Hexane-1,6-diamine + hexanedioic acid (each monomer has 6 carbons):

n H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂ + n HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH → –[NH(CH₂)₆NH–CO(CH₂)₄CO]ₙ– + 2n H₂O

Uses: clothing, ropes, carpets. Using hexanedioyl dichloride, ClOC(CH₂)₄COCl, instead of the acid works faster at room temperature and gives HCl.

**Kevlar.** Benzene-1,4-diamine + benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (or its dichloride): –[NH–C₆H₄–NH–CO–C₆H₄–CO]ₙ–. The flat, rigid chains line up in parallel, held by many hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H groups on neighbouring chains. This makes Kevlar very strong and light: used in bulletproof vests, helmets, ropes and tyres.

**From one monomer.**

- 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid) forms poly(lactic acid), PLA: –[O–CH(CH₃)–CO]ₙ–. Made from corn starch; biodegradable; used in packaging, cups and dissolving stitches.
- Amino acids form polypeptides (file 28).

**Drawing repeat units.** Join the –COOH of one monomer to the –OH or –NH₂ of the next by removing H₂O (OH from the acid, H from the alcohol or amine). Show the link with open bonds at each end.

**Finding monomers from a polymer.** Break each ester or amide link: add –OH to the C=O side and –H to the O or N side.

**Hydrogen bonding.** In polyamides, the N–H of one chain hydrogen bonds to the C=O of another. Polyesters have only permanent dipole–dipole and London forces between chains.

### Practical

**Making nylon (AQA).** Carefully pour a solution of hexanedioyl dichloride in cyclohexane on to an aqueous solution of hexane-1,6-diamine (with a little NaOH to neutralise the HCl). Nylon forms at the interface; use tweezers to pull a continuous thread and wind it on to a rod. Wear gloves and goggles: acyl chlorides are corrosive, the diamine is an irritant.

### Common mistakes

- Including the H₂O in the repeat unit, or leaving an –OH or –H on the ends.
- Drawing two repeat units when asked for one.
- Getting the number of carbons in nylon 6,6 monomers wrong (the acid has 6 including both COOH carbons).

## 3.3.12.2 Biodegradability and disposal of polymers

*Refs: AQA 3.3.12.2 · OCR A 6.2.3(b) · OCR B PL(m). Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- Polyalkenes are chemically inert, so non-biodegradable.
- Polyesters and polyamides can be broken down by hydrolysis, so they biodegrade (slowly).
- Acid and base hydrolysis equations.
- Advantages and disadvantages of landfill, incineration and recycling.

### Explained

**Why polyalkenes are inert.** Non-polar C–C and C–H bonds; no site for nucleophiles to attack.

**Why condensation polymers biodegrade.** The ester and amide links are polar; the δ+ carbon of C=O is attacked by water (catalysed by acid, alkali or enzymes in the environment). This is still slow in landfill.

**Hydrolysis.**

- Polyester with NaOH(aq): gives the diol and the sodium salt of the dicarboxylic acid.
- Polyester with acid: gives the diol and the dicarboxylic acid.
- Polyamide with HCl(aq): gives the dicarboxylic acid and the ammonium salt of the diamine (H₃N⁺(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺ 2Cl⁻).
- Polyamide with NaOH(aq): gives the sodium dicarboxylate and the free diamine.

**Disposal.**

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Landfill | Cheap, easy; little sorting | Takes up land; polymers last for centuries; leachate can pollute |
| Incineration | Energy recovered for electricity; reduces volume | CO₂ (greenhouse gas); toxic gases (HCl from PVC, which must be removed) |
| Recycling (mechanical) | Saves crude oil and energy; reduces landfill and CO₂ | Collection and sorting costly; quality can be lower; only limited times |
| Chemical recycling (hydrolysis or cracking) | Back to monomers or feedstock | Energy-intensive |

### Common mistakes

- Saying condensation polymers biodegrade quickly. They can be hydrolysed but are still slow to break down in landfill.
- Writing the free diamine as the product of acid hydrolysis of a polyamide; in acid it is protonated.

## Mark scheme

1. Any two: addition monomers have C=C, condensation have two functional groups; condensation loses a small molecule (H₂O / HCl), addition does not; condensation forms ester / amide links.
2. –O–CH₂CH₂CH₂–O–CO–CH₂CH₂–CO– with open bonds (2: ester link 1, correct structure 1).
3. H₂N(CH₂)₄NH₂ (1); HOOC(CH₂)₈COOH or ClOC(CH₂)₈COCl (1).
4. –NH–CH₂CH₂–CO– (1).
5. Rigid, flat chains line up parallel (1); hydrogen bonds between C=O and N–H of neighbouring chains (1); many strong intermolecular forces (1).
6. Hexanedioic acid (1); hexane-1,6-diammonium chloride / ClH₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃Cl (1).
7. Ester link polar / δ+ carbon (1); can be hydrolysed (by water / enzymes) (1); poly(ethene) has non-polar C–C and C–H bonds, unreactive (1).
8. Advantage: saves raw materials / crude oil / energy / landfill space (1). Disadvantage: sorting / collection costs / lower quality product (1).
