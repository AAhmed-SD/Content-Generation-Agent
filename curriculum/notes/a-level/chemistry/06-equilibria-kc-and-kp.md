# Chemical equilibria, Le Chatelier's principle, Kc and Kp

Many reactions do not go to completion: they reach a balance where the forward and reverse reactions run at the same rate. This file covers dynamic equilibrium, Le Chatelier's principle, industrial compromise, the equilibrium constant Kc and, at A-level, Kp.

## Overview

The first two sections are AS. The Kp section and the effect of temperature on K are A-level only. Acid dissociation constants Ka are in file 11; ΔG and K are linked in file 8.

## 3.1.6.1 Chemical equilibria and Le Chatelier's principle

*Refs: AQA 3.1.6.1 · Edexcel 10.1 to 10.3 · OCR A 3.2.3(a) to (e) · OCR B ES(o), ES(q), CI(f), CI(g) · Eduqas C2.1(a), (b). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Many reactions are reversible. In a closed system they reach dynamic equilibrium: forward and reverse rates are equal, and concentrations stay constant.
- Le Chatelier's principle: if a factor affecting an equilibrium is changed, the position of equilibrium shifts to oppose the change.
- Predict the effects of changing concentration, pressure and temperature, and of adding a catalyst.
- Industrial processes use compromise conditions.

### Explained

"Dynamic" means both reactions are still happening. "Equilibrium" means no overall change. Concentrations are constant, not equal.

| Change | Shift | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Increase concentration of a reactant | Right, towards products | Uses up the added reactant |
| Remove a product | Right | Replaces the product |
| Increase pressure (gases) | Towards the side with fewer gas moles | Reduces the pressure |
| Increase temperature | In the endothermic direction | Absorbs the added heat |
| Decrease temperature | In the exothermic direction | Releases heat |
| Add a catalyst | No shift | Speeds up forward and reverse equally; equilibrium reached sooner |

If both sides have the same number of gas moles, pressure has no effect on position.

**Industrial compromise.** In the Haber process, N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹:

- Low temperature favours ammonia (exothermic forward), but the rate is too slow. About 450 °C is used with an iron catalyst.
- High pressure favours ammonia (4 gas moles → 2), but it is expensive and dangerous. About 200 atm is used.
- Unreacted gases are recycled.

Ethanol from ethene and steam, C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g), ΔH = −46 kJ mol⁻¹: about 300 °C, 60 to 70 atm, phosphoric acid catalyst. Excess steam pushes the equilibrium right, but too much would dilute the catalyst. The conversion per pass is only about 5%, so unreacted ethene is recycled, giving an overall conversion of about 95%.

The Contact process step 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (ΔH negative) uses about 450 °C, slightly above atmospheric pressure (the equilibrium already lies far right), and a V₂O₅ catalyst.

**Investigating equilibria (OCR A 3.2.3(d)).** Coloured systems make shifts visible:

- 2NO₂(g, brown) ⇌ N₂O₄(g, colourless), ΔH negative. Warm the gas syringe and it goes darker brown (shift left, endothermic direction).
- [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (pink) + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ (blue) + 6H₂O, ΔH positive. Adding concentrated HCl or heating turns it blue; adding water or cooling turns it pink.
- Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) + H₂O ⇌ 2CrO₄²⁻ (yellow) + 2H⁺. Adding acid turns it orange; adding alkali removes H⁺ and turns it yellow.

### Common mistakes

- Saying concentrations are equal at equilibrium.
- Saying a catalyst increases yield.
- Explaining pressure changes without counting gas moles.
- Writing "the equilibrium shifts to oppose the change" without saying which direction and why.

## 3.1.6.2 Equilibrium constant Kc for homogeneous systems

*Refs: AQA 3.1.6.2 · Edexcel 10.4, 11.2, 11.3, 11.5 · OCR A 3.2.3(f), (g), 5.1.2(b), (d) to (g) · OCR B ES(p), ES(q), CI(h) · Eduqas C2.1(c), PI5.1(a) to (c). Level: AS & A (temperature effect on Kc is A-level for Edexcel 11.3, OCR A 5.1.2(f), Eduqas PI5.1(a)).*

### What you need to know

- For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD, Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ ÷ ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ), with equilibrium concentrations.
- Work out the units of Kc.
- Calculate Kc from equilibrium amounts, and amounts from Kc.
- Kc is changed only by temperature. Concentration, pressure and catalysts change the position, not Kc.
- The size of Kc indicates the position of equilibrium: Kc much greater than 1 means mostly products.
- Heterogeneous equilibria (Edexcel, OCR A): leave out solids (and pure liquids) from Kc.

### Explained

**Units.** Put mol dm⁻³ for each concentration and cancel.

For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: Kc = [NH₃]² ÷ ([N₂][H₂]³). Units: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)⁴ = mol⁻² dm⁶.

For H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI: Kc = [HI]² ÷ ([H₂][I₂]), no units.

**Temperature.** If the forward reaction is exothermic, raising the temperature shifts equilibrium left, so Kc decreases. If endothermic, Kc increases. This is the only way to change K.

**Why concentration does not change Kc.** If you add reactant, the ratio in the expression is temporarily below Kc. The system reacts forward until the ratio equals Kc again. So the position moves but Kc stays the same.

**Heterogeneous example.** CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g): Kc = [CO₂]. The solids have constant "concentration" and are absorbed into K.

**Method for calculations (the ICE table).**

1. Write initial moles.
2. Use the equation ratio to find the change.
3. Equilibrium moles = initial ± change.
4. Divide by volume to get concentrations (if the total moles on each side are equal, volume cancels, but show it).
5. Substitute into Kc.

### Worked example

1. 1.00 mol of ethanoic acid and 1.00 mol of ethanol are mixed and left to reach equilibrium. 0.667 mol of ethyl ethanoate forms. Find Kc.

   CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.

   | | Acid | Alcohol | Ester | Water |
   | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
   | Start | 1.00 | 1.00 | 0 | 0 |
   | Equilibrium | 0.333 | 0.333 | 0.667 | 0.667 |

   Kc = (0.667 × 0.667) ÷ (0.333 × 0.333) = 4.0 (no units; V cancels).

2. For H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, Kc = 49 at a certain temperature. 1.00 mol of H₂ and 1.00 mol of I₂ are mixed. Find the equilibrium moles of HI.

   Let x mol of H₂ react. 49 = (2x)² ÷ (1 − x)². Square root: 7 = 2x ÷ (1 − x). 7 − 7x = 2x, x = 0.778. HI = 1.56 mol.

### Practical

**Eduqas: determining an equilibrium constant.** For ester hydrolysis: mix known amounts of ethyl ethanoate, water and a small known amount of hydrochloric acid catalyst in a sealed flask and leave for a week at constant temperature. Titrate the mixture with standard sodium hydroxide to find the total acid, subtract the HCl catalyst (found by titrating a blank), giving the moles of ethanoic acid at equilibrium. Work out the other amounts from the equation and calculate Kc. Titrate quickly and in a cold flask so the equilibrium does not shift during the titration.

### Common mistakes

- Using initial amounts instead of equilibrium amounts.
- Forgetting the powers from the balanced equation.
- Writing solids in a Kc expression.
- Saying that increasing pressure changes Kc.

## 3.1.10 Equilibrium constant Kp for homogeneous systems

*Refs: AQA 3.1.10 · Edexcel 11.1 to 11.5 · OCR A 5.1.2(a) to (h) · OCR B CI(h) · Eduqas PI5.1(a) to (c). Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- Mole fraction of a gas = moles of that gas ÷ total moles of gas.
- Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure. Partial pressures add up to the total pressure.
- For gases, Kp = p(C)ᶜ p(D)ᵈ ÷ (p(A)ᵃ p(B)ᵇ), and its units.
- Kp depends only on temperature. Effect of temperature on Kp for exothermic and endothermic reactions.
- Calculate Kp, or partial pressures, from data.

### Explained

The partial pressure of a gas in a mixture is the pressure it would exert if it alone filled the container. Square brackets are not used in Kp; write p(X) or pX.

For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g): Kp = p(NO₂)² ÷ p(N₂O₄). Units: kPa² ÷ kPa = kPa (or Pa, or atm, matching the data).

**Kp and total pressure.** Raising the total pressure changes partial pressures, and the position of equilibrium shifts to the side with fewer moles, but Kp itself is unchanged. Only temperature changes Kp, in the same way as for Kc.

**Heterogeneous Kp (Edexcel, OCR A).** Only gases appear. For C(s) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + H₂(g): Kp = p(CO) p(H₂) ÷ p(H₂O).

### Worked example

0.80 mol of N₂O₄ is heated in a closed vessel. At equilibrium 0.30 mol has dissociated: N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂. The total pressure is 200 kPa. Find Kp.

- N₂O₄ left: 0.50 mol. NO₂ formed: 0.60 mol. Total = 1.10 mol.
- Mole fractions: N₂O₄ 0.50 ÷ 1.10 = 0.455; NO₂ 0.60 ÷ 1.10 = 0.545.
- Partial pressures: N₂O₄ = 0.455 × 200 = 90.9 kPa; NO₂ = 0.545 × 200 = 109 kPa.
- Kp = 109² ÷ 90.9 = 131 kPa.

### Common mistakes

- Using the initial moles in the total.
- Forgetting to multiply mole fractions by total pressure.
- Stating that higher pressure increases Kp.
- Giving Kp units that do not match the pressure unit used.

## Check questions

1. State two features of a dynamic equilibrium. (2 marks)
2. For 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g), ΔH = −196 kJ mol⁻¹, state and explain the effect on the yield of SO₃ of (a) increasing pressure, (b) increasing temperature. (4 marks)
3. Explain why a catalyst does not change the yield of an equilibrium reaction. (2 marks)
4. Write the Kc expression and units for 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). (2 marks)
5. In a 2.00 dm³ flask at equilibrium there are 0.40 mol PCl₅, 0.20 mol PCl₃ and 0.20 mol Cl₂: PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂. Calculate Kc with units. (3 marks)
6. State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on Kc for an exothermic reaction. (2 marks)
7. In the equilibrium N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) at 15.0 MPa, the mole fractions are N₂ 0.20, H₂ 0.60, NH₃ 0.20. Calculate Kp with units. (3 marks)
8. Write a Kc expression for the heterogeneous equilibrium 3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) ⇌ Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g). (1 mark)

## Mark scheme

1. Rate of forward reaction equals rate of reverse reaction (1); concentrations remain constant / closed system (1).
2. (a) Yield increases (1); fewer gas moles on the right (3 → 2), so equilibrium shifts right to reduce pressure (1). (b) Yield decreases (1); forward reaction exothermic, so equilibrium shifts left / in endothermic direction to absorb heat (1).
3. Catalyst increases rates of forward and reverse reactions equally (1); so position unchanged, only reached faster (1).
4. Kc = [SO₃]² ÷ ([SO₂]²[O₂]) (1); mol⁻¹ dm³ (1).
5. Concentrations 0.20, 0.10, 0.10 mol dm⁻³ (1); Kc = (0.10 × 0.10) ÷ 0.20 = 0.050 (1); mol dm⁻³ (1).
6. Kc decreases (1); equilibrium shifts in endothermic (reverse) direction, so less product / more reactant (1).
7. Partial pressures: N₂ 3.0 MPa, H₂ 9.0 MPa, NH₃ 3.0 MPa (1); Kp = 3.0² ÷ (3.0 × 9.0³) = 4.1 × 10⁻³ (1); MPa⁻² (1).
8. Kc = [H₂]⁴ ÷ [H₂O]⁴ (1).
