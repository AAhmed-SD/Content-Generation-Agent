## ES Electrolysis and extracting the halogens

*Refs: OCR B ES(b), ES(c). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Electrolysis of molten salts: metal at the cathode, non-metal at the anode.
- Electrolysis of aqueous solutions: at the cathode, hydrogen forms for salts of Group 1, Group 2 and aluminium, otherwise the metal is plated; at the anode, a halogen forms from concentrated halide solutions, otherwise oxygen (or the anode metal dissolves if it is not inert).
- Half-equations at each electrode.
- Explain, from given information, how halogens are extracted from sea water.

### Explained

**Electrolysis** uses electricity to decompose an ionic compound that is molten or dissolved. Positive ions move to the cathode (negative electrode) and gain electrons (reduction). Negative ions move to the anode (positive electrode) and lose electrons (oxidation).

**Molten salts.** Molten lead(II) bromide: cathode Pb²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Pb; anode 2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻.

**Aqueous solutions.** Water provides H⁺ and OH⁻ (and can be reduced or oxidised itself).

| Electrode | Rule | Half-equation examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cathode | Metal plated if less reactive than hydrogen (Cu, Ag); hydrogen if Group 1, 2 or Al | Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu; 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → H₂ + 2OH⁻ (or 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ → H₂) |
| Anode (inert) | Halogen from concentrated halide; otherwise oxygen | 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻; 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (or 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻) |
| Anode (metal, e.g. copper) | The anode metal dissolves | Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ |

Brine (concentrated NaCl solution) gives hydrogen at the cathode, chlorine at the anode, and leaves sodium hydroxide in solution: the chlor-alkali industry.

**Practical (OCR B).** Electrolyse solutions in a small cell with carbon electrodes connected to a low-voltage DC supply. Test the gases: hydrogen pops with a lighted splint; oxygen relights a glowing splint; chlorine bleaches damp litmus paper. Notice copper plating on the cathode or colour changes from halogens at the anode.

**Extracting halogens from the sea (given information).** Chlorine is made by electrolysing brine. Bromine is extracted from concentrated sea water (bromide ions) by:

1. acidifying, then oxidising bromide with chlorine: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂;
2. blowing the bromine out with air or steam;
3. trapping it by reduction with sulfur dioxide and water: Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O → 2HBr + H₂SO₄ (this concentrates it);
4. oxidising again with chlorine and distilling the pure bromine.

Iodine can be obtained from seaweed or from iodate(V) minerals by reduction. In questions, use the given information to write equations and explain each step (which species is oxidised or reduced, and why).

### Common mistakes

- Putting oxidation at the cathode. Cathode = reduction.
- Predicting sodium metal at the cathode from aqueous NaCl. Hydrogen forms.

## CI Nitrogen chemistry and industrial processes

*Refs: OCR B CI(i), CI(j), CI(k). Level: A-level only.*

### What you need to know

- Bonding in N₂ (triple bond, very strong, so N₂ is unreactive), NH₃ (three bonds, one lone pair, pyramidal) and NH₄⁺ (dative bond, tetrahedral).
- The oxides N₂O, NO and NO₂: names, appearance, and oxidation states.
- Interconversions between nitrate(V), nitrate(III) (nitrite), ammonium and the oxides of nitrogen.
- Tests: ammonium (warm with NaOH, ammonia given off); nitrate(V) (warm with Devarda's alloy and NaOH, ammonia given off).
- Use given information to explain reactions in industrial processes; costs, co-products, by-products, benefits and risks.

### Explained

**Bonding.** N≡N has a bond enthalpy of 945 kJ mol⁻¹, so N₂ is very unreactive: it needs high temperatures (lightning, car engines) or catalysts (Haber process) or enzymes (nitrogen-fixing bacteria) to react. NH₃ is a base because of its lone pair, forming NH₄⁺ by a dative bond.

**Oxides of nitrogen.**

| Formula | Name | Oxidation state of N | Appearance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| N₂O | Dinitrogen oxide (nitrous oxide) | +1 | Colourless gas |
| NO | Nitrogen monoxide (nitrogen(II) oxide) | +2 | Colourless gas; turns brown in air as it forms NO₂ |
| NO₂ | Nitrogen dioxide (nitrogen(IV) oxide) | +4 | Brown gas |

**Interconversions (nitrogen cycle chemistry).**

| Change | N oxidation state | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| N₂ → NH₃ | 0 → −3 | Haber process; nitrogen-fixing bacteria |
| NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻ | −3 → +3 → +5 | Oxidation by nitrifying bacteria in soil |
| NO₃⁻ → N₂O / N₂ | +5 → +1 / 0 | Reduction by denitrifying bacteria in waterlogged soil |
| N₂ + O₂ → 2NO | 0 → +2 | High temperature (engines, lightning) |
| 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ | +2 → +4 | In air |
| NH₃ → NO → NO₂ → HNO₃ | −3 → +2 → +4 → +5 | Industrial: ammonia oxidised over a platinum–rhodium catalyst, then NO₂ absorbed in water with oxygen |
| NO₃⁻ → NH₃ | +5 → −3 | Devarda's alloy (Al, Cu, Zn) in alkali (test) |

Nitrate(III) is the systematic name for nitrite, NO₂⁻; nitrate(V) is NO₃⁻.

**Tests.**

- Ammonium ions: warm with sodium hydroxide; ammonia is given off and turns damp red litmus blue. NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃ + H₂O.
- Nitrate(V) ions: if the sample contains no ammonium ions, warm with sodium hydroxide and Devarda's alloy; ammonia is given off, turning damp red litmus blue. The aluminium reduces nitrate(V) to ammonia: 3NO₃⁻ + 8Al + 5OH⁻ + 18H₂O → 3NH₃ + 8[Al(OH)₄]⁻.

**Industrial processes (given information).** Exam questions describe a process and ask you to analyse it. Points to consider:

- **Raw materials:** cost, availability, renewable or not, purity (impurities can poison catalysts).
- **Energy:** temperature and pressure needed; is the reaction exothermic (heat recovered and used elsewhere in the plant) or endothermic?
- **Rate and yield:** choose conditions to balance them (files 5, 6, 9); catalysts; recycling of unreacted materials.
- **Plant costs:** building and maintaining equipment; high pressure is expensive. Continuous processes suit large volumes; batch processes suit small-volume products such as medicines.
- **Co-products** are formed alongside the main product by the reaction and can be sold (for example chlorine and hydrogen with sodium hydroxide from brine). **By-products** come from side reactions and may be waste.
- **Benefits and risks:** benefits of the product to society (fertilisers feed people; medicines; materials) against hazards (toxic, flammable or corrosive chemicals; high pressure; environmental release; transport risks). Plants are often sited near raw materials, transport links and a workforce, and away from housing.

**Example: the Haber process and fertilisers.** N₂ (from air) + 3H₂ (from methane and steam) ⇌ 2NH₃. Iron catalyst, about 450 °C, 200 atm, unreacted gases recycled. Ammonia is oxidised to nitric acid, and the two react to make ammonium nitrate fertiliser. Benefits: enough food for billions. Risks: the hydrogen comes from fossil fuels (CO₂ emissions); ammonia is toxic; ammonium nitrate is explosive; fertiliser run-off causes eutrophication of rivers.

### Common mistakes

- Saying NO is brown. NO is colourless; NO₂ is brown.
- Testing for nitrate with Devarda's alloy without first checking for ammonium ions.
- Confusing co-products (from the main reaction) with by-products (from side reactions).

## Check questions

1. Write half-equations for the reactions at each electrode in the electrolysis of aqueous copper(II) sulfate with inert electrodes. (2 marks)
2. Predict the products at each electrode in the electrolysis of concentrated aqueous potassium bromide. (2 marks)
3. Bromine is released from sea water by adding chlorine. Write an ionic equation and explain the reaction in terms of redox. (3 marks)
4. Explain why nitrogen gas is unreactive. (2 marks)
5. Give the oxidation state of nitrogen in N₂O, NO₂⁻ and NH₄⁺. (3 marks)
6. Describe how to test a fertiliser for nitrate(V) ions. (3 marks)
7. In a process, the main reaction is exothermic. Suggest how a chemical company can use this to reduce costs. (2 marks)
8. Distinguish between a co-product and a by-product. (2 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (1). Anode: 2H₂O → O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ (1).
2. Cathode: hydrogen (1). Anode: bromine (1).
3. Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (1); chlorine gains electrons / reduced (1); bromide loses electrons / oxidised; chlorine is the stronger oxidising agent (1).
4. Triple bond (1); very high bond enthalpy, so lots of energy needed to break it (1).
5. N₂O +1 (1); NO₂⁻ +3 (1); NH₄⁺ −3 (1).
6. Check no ammonium present first / warm with NaOH alone, no ammonia (1); then warm with NaOH and Devarda's alloy (1); gas turns damp red litmus blue (ammonia) (1).
7. Use heat released to warm reactants / generate steam or electricity (1); reduces fuel / energy costs (1).
8. Co-product: formed in the main reaction alongside the desired product (1); by-product: formed in an unwanted side reaction (1).
