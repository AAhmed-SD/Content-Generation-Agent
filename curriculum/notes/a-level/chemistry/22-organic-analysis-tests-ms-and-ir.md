# Organic analysis: test-tube tests, mass spectrometry and infrared

How do you find out what an unknown organic compound is? This file covers chemical tests for functional groups, mass spectrometry for relative molecular mass, molecular formula and fragments, and infrared spectroscopy for functional groups. It includes AQA required practical 6.

## Overview

AS content, with some A-level extensions. NMR, and combining all the evidence, is in file 30. Tests for aldehydes and ketones (Tollens', Fehling's, 2,4-DNPH, iodoform) are explained in file 23 and listed here.

## 3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test-tube reactions

*Refs: AQA 3.3.6.1, RP6 · OCR A 6.3.1(c) · Eduqas OA4 (planning tests). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Tests for alkenes, alcohols (primary, secondary and tertiary), aldehydes, carboxylic acids and halogenoalkanes, with observations.

### Explained

| Functional group | Test | Positive result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Alkene C=C | Shake with bromine water | Orange to colourless |
| Halogenoalkane | Warm with NaOH(aq) (or ethanol and water), acidify with HNO₃, add AgNO₃(aq) | White (Cl), cream (Br), yellow (I) precipitate |
| Primary or secondary alcohol | Warm with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | Orange to green |
| Tertiary alcohol | Same test | Stays orange |
| Alcohol (any) | PCl₅ (dry) | Steamy fumes of HCl |
| Aldehyde | Warm with Tollens' reagent (water bath) | Silver mirror |
| Aldehyde | Warm with Fehling's or Benedict's solution | Blue solution to red precipitate |
| Aldehyde or ketone | 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine | Orange / yellow precipitate (file 23) |
| Methyl ketone or ethanal; ethanol and secondary alcohols with CH₃CH(OH)– | Iodine and NaOH, warm | Pale yellow precipitate of CHI₃ (file 23) |
| Carboxylic acid | Add Na₂CO₃ or NaHCO₃ solution | Effervescence; gas turns limewater cloudy |
| Phenol | Neutral FeCl₃(aq) | Purple colour (file 25) |

**Distinguishing primary from secondary alcohols.** Both turn dichromate green. Distil off the product as it forms and test it: an aldehyde (from a primary alcohol) gives a silver mirror with Tollens' or red precipitate with Fehling's; a ketone (from a secondary alcohol) gives neither.

**Tollens' reagent.** Add NaOH(aq) to AgNO₃(aq) to give a brown precipitate, then add dilute ammonia dropwise until it just dissolves, giving [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. Make fresh and warm in a water bath (it can form explosive compounds if stored).

### Practical

**AQA RP6: tests for alcohol, aldehyde, alkene and carboxylic acid.** Use a few drops of each unknown, warm in a water bath (no naked flames; organic compounds are flammable), and record observations. Always compare with a control.

**Eduqas: planning a sequence of tests.** Order the tests so each result narrows the options, for example sodium carbonate (acid) first, then bromine water (alkene), then 2,4-DNPH (carbonyl), then Tollens' (aldehyde versus ketone), then dichromate (alcohol type).

### Common mistakes

- Heating Tollens' over a Bunsen flame.
- Saying ketones give a positive Tollens' test.
- Forgetting that the dichromate test does not distinguish primary from secondary alcohols by itself.

## 3.3.6.2 Mass spectrometry

*Refs: AQA 3.3.6.2 · Edexcel 7.1, 19.1 · OCR A 4.2.4(f) to (h) · OCR B WM(i), PL(r) · Eduqas C3.5(a). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- The molecular ion peak (M⁺) gives Mᵣ.
- High-resolution mass spectrometry gives Mᵣ to several decimal places, distinguishing compounds with the same nominal mass.
- Fragmentation peaks show the structure (OCR A, Edexcel, OCR B).
- The M+1 peak from ¹³C (not required by all boards).

### Explained

**The molecular ion.** In electron impact, a high-energy electron removes an electron from the molecule: M + e⁻ → M⁺• + 2e⁻. The M⁺ peak is the one with the highest m/z (ignoring the small M+1 peak). Its m/z is Mᵣ.

**High resolution (AQA, Edexcel).** Precise atomic masses: ¹H 1.0078, ¹²C 12.0000, ¹⁴N 14.0031, ¹⁶O 15.9949. Molecules with the same nominal Mᵣ have different precise values. For Mᵣ = 44:

| Formula | Precise mass |
| --- | --- |
| CO₂ | 43.9898 |
| C₃H₈ | 44.0624 |
| C₂H₄O | 44.0261 |
| N₂O | 44.0011 |

A peak at 44.0262 shows C₂H₄O.

**Fragmentation.** The molecular ion can break into a positive fragment (detected) and a radical (not detected, being uncharged): M⁺ → X⁺ + Y•. Common fragments:

| m/z | Fragment |
| --- | --- |
| 15 | CH₃⁺ |
| 29 | C₂H₅⁺ or CHO⁺ |
| 31 | CH₂OH⁺ |
| 43 | C₃H₇⁺ or CH₃CO⁺ |
| 45 | COOH⁺ |
| 77 | C₆H₅⁺ |

Losses from M: M − 15 (lost CH₃), M − 17 (lost OH), M − 18 (lost H₂O, from alcohols), M − 29 (lost C₂H₅ or CHO), M − 45 (lost COOH).

Propanone (Mᵣ 58) has a big peak at 43 (CH₃CO⁺, lost CH₃). Propanal (also 58) has peaks at 29 (C₂H₅⁺ and CHO⁺) and 57. More stable carbocations (tertiary) and acylium ions (RCO⁺) give taller peaks.

**M+1 peak.** About 1.1% of carbon is ¹³C, so there is a small peak at M+1. Its height relative to M⁺ ≈ 1.1 × number of carbons (%).

**Halogens.** A compound with one Cl has M and M+2 peaks in ratio 3 : 1; one Br, M and M+2 in ratio 1 : 1.

### Worked example

A compound of C, H and O has a molecular ion at m/z 74 and major peaks at 59, 45 and 29. Suggest its identity.

74 − 59 = 15 (lost CH₃); 45 (COOH⁺); 29 (C₂H₅⁺). Propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH, fits: Mᵣ 74, C₂H₅⁺ and COOH⁺ fragments.

### Common mistakes

- Taking the tallest peak (base peak) as M⁺.
- Writing fragments without a positive charge.
- Writing radical fragments as detected.

## 3.3.6.3 Infrared spectroscopy

*Refs: AQA 3.3.6.3 · Edexcel 7.2 · OCR A 4.2.4(a), (c) to (e), (h) · OCR B WM(j) · Eduqas C3.5(b). Level: AS & A.*

### What you need to know

- Bonds absorb infrared radiation at characteristic wavenumbers, making them vibrate (stretch and bend) more.
- Use an IR spectrum to identify functional groups, and the fingerprint region to identify a compound by comparison.
- IR is used to monitor gases such as CO and NO in exhausts, and alcohol in breath (OCR A).
- Links to the greenhouse effect (file 35).

### Explained

An IR spectrum plots transmittance (%) against wavenumber (cm⁻¹; 4000 on the left to 500 on the right). Absorptions show as dips (troughs). Wavenumber is 1/wavelength.

| Bond | Wavenumber / cm⁻¹ | Appearance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| O–H (alcohol) | 3230 to 3550 | Broad |
| O–H (carboxylic acid) | 2500 to 3000 | Very broad, overlapping C–H |
| N–H (amine, amide) | 3300 to 3500 | Medium |
| C–H | 2850 to 3300 | Sharp |
| C≡N | 2220 to 2260 | Medium, sharp |
| C=O | 1680 to 1750 | Strong, sharp |
| C=C | 1620 to 1680 | Medium |
| C–O | 1000 to 1300 | Strong |

Use the values given on the data sheet in an exam.

- **Alcohol:** broad O–H at about 3300 and C–O.
- **Aldehyde or ketone:** C=O at about 1700, no O–H.
- **Carboxylic acid:** very broad O–H at 2500 to 3000 plus C=O.
- **Ester:** C=O and C–O, no O–H.

**Fingerprint region** (below about 1500 cm⁻¹): a complex pattern unique to each molecule. Comparing it with a database identifies the compound exactly; extra peaks show impurities.

**Monitoring gases (OCR A 4.2.4(e)).** Roadside sensors measure CO and NO in car exhausts by their IR absorptions. Police breathalysers measure the C–H (or C–O) absorption of ethanol; the intensity shows how much ethanol is in the breath.

### Common mistakes

- Confusing the very broad acid O–H with the alcohol O–H.
- Trying to read structure from the fingerprint region. Use it only to compare with a known spectrum.
- Saying a peak at about 3000 alone proves an O–H: all organic compounds have C–H there.

## Check questions

1. Describe a test to show that a compound is an aldehyde and not a ketone. (2 marks)
2. Describe how to show an unknown liquid is a carboxylic acid. (2 marks)
3. A compound reacts with acidified potassium dichromate but its product does not react with Tollens' reagent. What type of compound is it? (1 mark)
4. Using precise masses (H 1.0078, C 12.0000, O 15.9949), show which of C₃H₆O and C₄H₁₀ has a high-resolution Mᵣ of 58.0419. (2 marks)
5. The mass spectrum of a ketone C₅H₁₀O has major peaks at 86, 57 and 29. Suggest its structure and explain. (3 marks)
6. Explain how an infrared spectrum could distinguish between propan-1-ol, propanal and propanoic acid. (3 marks)
7. Explain what the fingerprint region is used for. (2 marks)
8. A compound has a mass spectrum with peaks at m/z 122 and 124 of equal height. Explain what this shows. (2 marks)

## Mark scheme

1. Warm with Tollens' reagent (1); silver mirror with aldehyde, no change with ketone (1). Accept Fehling's: red precipitate vs stays blue.
2. Add sodium carbonate / hydrogencarbonate (1); effervescence, gas turns limewater cloudy (1).
3. Secondary alcohol (1).
4. C₃H₆O = 36.0000 + 6.0468 + 15.9949 = 58.0417 (1); C₄H₁₀ = 58.0780, so C₃H₆O (1).
5. Pentan-3-one, CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ (1); 86 molecular ion (1); 57 = CH₃CH₂CO⁺ and 29 = C₂H₅⁺ (1).
6. Propan-1-ol: broad O–H at 3230 to 3550, no C=O (1). Propanal: C=O at about 1720, no O–H (1). Propanoic acid: very broad O–H at 2500 to 3000 and C=O (1).
7. Unique pattern for each compound (1); compared with database / known spectrum to identify / check purity (1).
8. Contains one bromine atom (1); ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br isotopes in about 1 : 1 ratio (1).
